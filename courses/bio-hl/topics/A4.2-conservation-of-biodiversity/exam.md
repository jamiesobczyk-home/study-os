# A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Distinguish genetic, species and ecosystem diversity. **[3]**

Give a definition for each level of biodiversity.

### Mark scheme

- Genetic diversity is variation in genetic information / alleles within a species or population. **[1]**
- Species diversity is the variety of species in a community, including species richness and relative abundance / evenness. **[1]**
- Ecosystem diversity is the variety of ecosystems / habitats / biological communities in an area. **[1]**

_Examiner note: three versions of “variety” aren’t enough. Each definition must name the biological level at which the variation occurs._

## Q2. Compare present-day species diversity with species diversity in the past. **[4]**

Include why estimates of both present and past diversity have limitations.

### Mark scheme

- Millions of living species have been discovered / named / described, with many more still undiscovered. **[1]**
- Fossil evidence suggests that more species may be alive today than at any previous time. **[1]**
- The fossil record is incomplete / preservation and discovery are uneven, limiting estimates of past diversity. **[1]**
- Taxonomic decisions by splitters and lumpers / undiscovered living species cause uncertainty in current species counts. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “there are more species today” earns one point at most. Full marks require evidence and limitations on both sides of the comparison._

## Q3. Explain the anthropogenic extinction of the North Island giant moa and Caribbean monk seal. **[6]**

For each species, identify human actions and connect them to extinction.

### Mark scheme

- North Island giant moas were hunted by people for food. **[1]**
- Burning / clearance of vegetation reduced or fragmented moa habitat. **[1]**
- Hunting and habitat loss caused moa populations to decline to extinction. **[1]**
- Caribbean monk seals were hunted for oil / meat / skins. **[1]**
- Seals were killed as competitors by fishers / fishing reduced their prey supply. **[1]**
- Continued human exploitation caused the remaining monk seal populations to become extinct. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying both species were “affected by humans” misses the case-study detail. The answer needs distinct human pressures and a population consequence for each species._

## Q4. Analyze evidence from repeated biodiversity surveys. **[5]**

A habitat was surveyed with the same sampling method and effort in 2005 and 2025.

| Year | Species richness | Evenness index |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2005 | 36 | 0.82 |
| 2025 | 29 | 0.48 |

Analyze how these data could contribute to evidence for a biodiversity crisis.

### Mark scheme

- Species richness decreased by 7 species / from 36 to 29. **[1]**
- Evenness decreased from 0.82 to 0.48. **[1]**
- The later community contained fewer species and had a less even distribution of abundance / greater dominance by some species. **[1]**
- Use of the same sampling method and effort makes comparison between the years more valid. **[1]**
- Similar repeated declines across many habitats / taxa / regions would provide stronger evidence of a biodiversity crisis than this site alone. **[1]**

_Examiner note: one site supports a local trend, not a global conclusion. Full marks require both interpretation of the two measures and a limit on what the dataset can establish._

## Q5. Explain how human population growth can intensify the current biodiversity crisis. **[5]**

Develop a causal chain from population growth to at least three direct pressures on biodiversity.

### Mark scheme

- Human population growth increases demand for food / land / energy / materials. **[1]**
- Agricultural expansion / deforestation removes and fragments natural habitat. **[1]**
- Urbanization replaces or fragments habitats. **[1]**
- Hunting / fishing / resource extraction can cause overexploitation. **[1]**
- Pollution or global transport of pests / diseases / invasive alien species causes population decline or ecosystem disruption. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “more people cause more damage” doesn’t provide the requested chain. Demand must be connected to named human activities and then to biological pressures._

## Q6. Suggest a conservation plan for an evolutionarily distinct and globally endangered species. **[6]**

The species has fewer than 200 individuals in one degraded habitat, and seeds or reproductive tissues can be stored. Include EDGE reasoning and more than one conservation approach.

### Mark scheme

- The species receives high priority because it is evolutionarily distinct / represents unique evolutionary history. **[1]**
- It also receives high priority because it is globally endangered / has a high extinction risk. **[1]**
- Protect and manage the remaining natural habitat in situ / control the threats acting there. **[1]**
- Reclaim the degraded habitat / restore ecological conditions needed by the species. **[1]**
- Establish an ex situ breeding or propagation population to reduce the immediate risk of total loss. **[1]**
- Store germ plasm in a seed or tissue bank to preserve genetic material for future breeding / reintroduction. **[1]**

_Examiner note: EDGE explains why the species is prioritized, not how it will be saved. Full marks require both parts of EDGE and a coordinated set of conservation measures._
