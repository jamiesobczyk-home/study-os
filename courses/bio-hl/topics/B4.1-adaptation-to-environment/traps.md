# B4.1 Adaptation to environment — where the marks go

## Confusing habitat with niche

**Commonly written:** “A habitat is the role an organism has in its ecosystem.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A habitat is the place where an organism lives. Its role and interactions belong to its ecological niche.

**Scores instead:** “A habitat is the place where an organism, population, species or community lives.”

## Making adaptation happen because it’s needed

**Commonly written:** “Marram grass rolled its leaves because it needed to stop losing water.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The sentence implies that an individual produced an inherited feature in response to need, rather than natural selection acting across generations.

**Scores instead:** “Natural selection favored inherited rolled leaves because they reduced water loss and increased reproductive success on dunes.”

## Listing a feature without its advantage

**Commonly written:** “Marram grass has sunken stomata and rhizomes.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The features are named, but neither is linked to the dune condition that makes it adaptive.

**Scores instead:** “Sunken stomata trap humid air and reduce transpiration, while rhizomes anchor the grass in shifting sand.”

## Mixing adaptations from different mangrove species

**Commonly written:** “Every mangrove species has prop roots, pneumatophores and salt glands.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mangrove species use different combinations of adaptations, so the answer makes an unsupported claim about all of them.

**Scores instead:** “Black mangrove has pneumatophores for gas exchange above oxygen-poor mud and leaf salt glands that excrete excess salt.”

## Treating the optimum as the tolerance range

**Commonly written:** “The tolerance range is where the species reaches its highest abundance.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Highest performance occurs near the optimum. The tolerance range extends between the lower and upper limits where survival remains possible.

**Scores instead:** “The species performs best near its optimum but can survive throughout the wider range between its tolerance limits.”

## Claiming that correlation proves causation

**Commonly written:** “The transect proves that soil pH causes the plant’s distribution.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A correlation shows an association, but another variable or a biotic interaction could produce the same pattern.

**Scores instead:** “Plant abundance was correlated with soil pH, which supports a relationship but doesn’t by itself establish causation.”

## Defining a biome as one large ecosystem

**Commonly written:** “A biome is a very large ecosystem in one part of the world.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A biome groups multiple ecosystems that share similar abiotic conditions and communities with similar adaptations.

**Scores instead:** “A biome is a group of ecosystems with similar climates and organisms with similar adaptations.”

## Calling convergent features evidence of close ancestry

**Commonly written:** “Cacti and euphorbias have succulent stems because they’re closely related.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Their similar desert features evolved independently under similar selection pressures rather than coming from a recent common ancestor.

**Scores instead:** “Cacti and euphorbias independently evolved similar water-conserving features through convergent evolution.”
