# A3.1 Diversity of organisms — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If an answer only looked familiar after the reveal, it wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Distinguish the morphological species concept from the biological species concept. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- The morphological species concept groups organisms according to shared observable / structural traits. **[1]**
- Morphology can vary within one species / similar morphology may occur in different species. **[1]**
- The biological species concept groups organisms that can interbreed. **[1]**
- Interbreeding must produce fertile offspring. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “Members of a species can mate” misses the fertility requirement, while listing shared traits without naming morphology leaves the other concept undefined._

## Q2. Explain why the division between populations and species can be unclear during speciation. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Speciation is the splitting of one species into two or more species. **[1]**
- Separated / non-interbreeding populations accumulate genetic or phenotypic differences. **[1]**
- Divergence and reproductive isolation usually develop gradually. **[1]**
- There may be no precise point at which the populations become separate species / the chosen boundary may be arbitrary. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer that says only “the populations look different” doesn’t explain why the decision is difficult. Full marks require gradual change and the absence of a sharp boundary._

## Q3. Evaluate the evidence that human chromosome 2 arose by fusion of two ancestral primate chromosomes. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Humans have 46 chromosomes whereas chimpanzees / other great apes have 48. **[1]**
- DNA sequences / banding patterns in human chromosome 2 correspond to two separate ape chromosomes arranged end to end. **[1]**
- Telomere-like repeat sequences occur internally at the proposed fusion site. **[1]**
- A vestigial / inactive second centromere is present. **[1]**
- The independent chromosome-count and molecular evidence strongly supports the fusion hypothesis / Accept: evidence is consistent with fusion in the human lineage after divergence from a shared ancestor. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “humans have fewer chromosomes” earns one mark at most. Evaluation requires several independent observations and a conclusion about how strongly they support the hypothesis._

## Q4. Compare and contrast the following genome data. **[4]**

| Species | Genome size / Mb | Approximate protein-coding genes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Baker’s yeast | 12 | 6,000 |
| Human | 3,200 | 20,000 |
| Australian lungfish | 34,500 | 20,000 |

### Mark scheme

- Human and lungfish genomes contain a similar approximate number of protein-coding genes. **[1]**
- The lungfish genome is approximately ten times larger than the human genome. **[1]**
- Genome size can increase without a corresponding increase in protein-coding gene number. **[1]**
- The data do not support genome size as a reliable measure of organism complexity / large genomes may contain more non-coding or repetitive DNA. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the table doesn’t show that lungfish are more complex than humans. The scoring conclusion separates total DNA from gene number and avoids treating genome size as a complexity scale._

## Q5. Explain why the biological species concept is difficult to apply to asexually reproducing organisms and bacteria. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- The biological species concept defines species by the ability to interbreed and produce fertile offspring. **[1]**
- Asexually reproducing organisms do not interbreed, so reproductive compatibility cannot be tested. **[1]**
- Bacteria can acquire DNA by horizontal gene transfer / from organisms other than a parent. **[1]**
- Horizontal gene transfer can occur across species boundaries / produces genomes containing sequences from different lineages. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying “bacteria reproduce quickly” is irrelevant. The problem is that the definition tests sexual reproduction while bacterial genes can move between lineages._

## Q6. Outline how environmental DNA and DNA barcodes can be used to identify species in a lake. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- A water sample is collected containing DNA released in cells / waste / decaying material. **[1]**
- DNA is extracted from the environmental sample. **[1]**
- A short, standardized barcode region is amplified and sequenced. **[1]**
- The sequence is compared with reference sequences from identified species in a database. **[1]**
- A close match identifies a species that contributed DNA to the sample / Accept: multiple barcode matches allow rapid assessment of species diversity. **[1]**

_Examiner note: sequencing the sample isn’t enough. Full marks require matching a short barcode sequence to an identified reference in a database._
