# D2.1 Cell and nuclear division — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and produce the sequence from memory. A second explanation can make the diagrams feel familiar without making you able to explain them.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — D2.1 Cell and nuclear division**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+D2.1+Cell+and+nuclear+division>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the version built around D2.1.

**Watch for:**

- What separates during mitosis, meiosis I and meiosis II.
- Why chromosome number is maintained by mitosis but halved by meiosis.
- How crossing over differs from random orientation of bivalents.
- Which parts belong only to HL: the cell cycle, cyclins and tumours.

**Pause at:** the first full comparison of mitosis and meiosis. Cover the labels and say aloud the number of DNA replications, nuclear divisions and resulting nuclei in each process.

## If that didn’t land

**Amoeba Sisters — mitosis and meiosis**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@AmoebaSisters> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Amoeba+Sisters+mitosis+versus+meiosis>

Use this if the two processes keep blending together. Afterward, draw two columns from memory and compare what pairs, what lines up and what separates.

**Khan Academy — cell cycle and cancer**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@khanacademy> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Khan+Academy+cell+cycle+cyclins+cancer>

Use this if the HL control section is the problem. Keep the IB limit in mind: know that different cyclin concentrations rise and fall, and that a threshold must be reached to pass a checkpoint. You don’t need the roles of named cyclins.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Chromosome movement and cytokinesis are spatial processes, so an **animation** earns one exception to the one-video rule.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=mitosis+meiosis+cytokinesis+animation>

Pause before each separation. Predict whether homologous chromosomes, sister chromatids or cytoplasm will move next, then check the animation.
