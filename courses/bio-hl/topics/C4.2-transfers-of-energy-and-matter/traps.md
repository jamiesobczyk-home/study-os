# C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter — where the marks go

## Reversing food-chain arrows

**Commonly written:** “The arrow points from the fox to the rabbit because the fox eats the rabbit.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The arrow shows the direction in which energy and biomass are transferred, so it must point from the rabbit to the fox.

**Scores instead:** “An arrow points from the organism being eaten to its consumer, showing the transfer of energy and biomass.”

## Treating trophic level as a permanent species label

**Commonly written:** “An omnivore is always a secondary consumer.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Trophic level depends on the organism’s position in a particular food chain, so an omnivore can occupy more than one level.

**Scores instead:** “An omnivore is a primary consumer when it eats producers and a secondary consumer when it eats primary consumers.”

## Saying autotrophs don’t respire

**Commonly written:** “Plants photosynthesize, while animals respire.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Photosynthesis supplies carbon compounds, but both autotrophs and heterotrophs oxidize carbon compounds in cell respiration.

**Scores instead:** “Plants photosynthesize and also carry out cell respiration to transfer energy from carbon compounds to ATP.”

## Saying energy disappears between trophic levels

**Commonly written:** “Most of the energy is destroyed before it reaches the next trophic level.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Energy is conserved, but much of it becomes dispersed as heat or remains in material unavailable to the next consumer level.

**Scores instead:** “Cell respiration and ATP use transfer energy as heat, while uneaten and unassimilated material doesn’t reach the next consumer level.”

## Confusing less biomass with less energy per gram

**Commonly written:** “Biomass at higher trophic levels contains less energy per gram.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Total biomass and available energy usually decrease, but the energy content per unit mass isn’t required to decrease.

**Scores instead:** “Higher trophic levels have less total biomass and energy, often because they contain fewer or smaller organisms.”

## Defining secondary production by trophic level

**Commonly written:** “Secondary production is biomass made by secondary consumers.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Secondary production is accumulation of carbon compounds in the biomass of all heterotrophs, not one consumer level.

**Scores instead:** “Secondary production is the accumulation of carbon compounds in heterotroph biomass.”

## Calling every carbon store a sink

**Commonly written:** “A forest is a carbon sink because it contains carbon.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A sink requires net uptake during the measured period, which depends on carbon inputs exceeding carbon outputs.

**Scores instead:** “A forest is a carbon sink when photosynthetic carbon uptake exceeds carbon release by respiration, combustion and other processes.”

## Saying matter and energy both cycle

**Commonly written:** “Energy and nutrients cycle repeatedly through the food web.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Chemical elements are recycled, but energy enters, passes through trophic levels and becomes dispersed as heat.

**Scores instead:** “Matter is recycled through ecosystems, while energy flows through them and ultimately leaves as heat.”
