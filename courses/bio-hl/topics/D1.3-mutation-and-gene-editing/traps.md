# D1.3 Mutation and gene editing — where the marks go

## Saying every substitution changes an amino acid

**Commonly written:** "A base substitution changes one amino acid in the protein."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Degeneracy means the altered codon may specify the same amino acid, while another substitution could create a stop codon.

**Scores instead:** "A substitution may be synonymous, may change one amino acid or may introduce a premature stop codon."

## Calling every insertion or deletion a frameshift

**Commonly written:** "Insertions and deletions always shift the reading frame."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The reading frame shifts only when the number of inserted or deleted nucleotides isn’t divisible by three.

**Scores instead:** "An insertion or deletion causes a frameshift when its size isn’t divisible by three; otherwise it remains in frame."

## Treating random as equally likely everywhere

**Commonly written:** "Mutations are random, so every base has the same chance of mutating."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Randomness refers to the absence of a purposeful response to need, while mutation probabilities can differ among bases.

**Scores instead:** "Mutations aren’t directed toward needed traits, although some bases and genome regions mutate more often than others."

## Saying useful mutations appear because they’re needed

**Commonly written:** "The environment causes organisms to develop helpful mutations."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mutation produces variation without regard to need; the environment selects among heritable variants after they arise.

**Scores instead:** "Random mutation creates new alleles, and natural selection can increase a beneficial allele’s frequency in a particular environment."

## Saying all mutations can be inherited

**Commonly written:** "A mutation is passed from the organism to its offspring."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Only a mutation entering a gamete that contributes to a zygote can be inherited; somatic mutations normally remain within the individual.

**Scores instead:** "A germline mutation can be inherited if it enters a contributing gamete, while a somatic mutation normally can’t pass to offspring."

## Reversing the roles of guide RNA and Cas9

**Commonly written:** "Cas9 recognizes the matching bases, and the guide RNA cuts the DNA."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Complementary base pairing gives the guide RNA target specificity, while Cas9 is the nuclease that cuts DNA.

**Scores instead:** "The guide RNA binds complementary target DNA and directs Cas9, which cuts the DNA."

## Treating a knockout phenotype as complete proof

**Commonly written:** "The knockout shows that this gene alone causes the phenotype."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A changed phenotype supports a role for the gene, but other genes, indirect effects or developmental changes may contribute.

**Scores instead:** "A difference from the control provides evidence that the knocked-out gene contributes to the affected process."

## Saying conserved sequences never mutate

**Commonly written:** "The sequence is conserved because mutations can’t occur in it."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mutations may occur but be removed by purifying selection, or the sequence may have a lower rather than zero mutation rate.

**Scores instead:** "Conservation may result from selection removing harmful variants or from a relatively low mutation rate."
