# C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Distinguish open systems from closed systems and explain why most ecosystems are open.
- [ ] Explain why sunlight sustains most ecosystems, while recognizing ecosystems supported by chemoautotrophs.
- [ ] Construct food chains and food webs with arrows showing the transfer of energy and biomass from food to consumer.
- [ ] Classify organisms as producers, primary consumers, secondary consumers or tertiary consumers, including organisms that occupy different trophic levels in different food chains.
- [ ] Explain how dead organisms, dead body parts and feces supply carbon compounds and energy to detritivores and decomposers.
- [ ] Distinguish autotrophs, heterotrophs, photoautotrophs and chemoautotrophs by their sources of carbon and energy.
- [ ] Explain why both autotrophs and heterotrophs carry out cell respiration.
- [ ] Construct and interpret pyramids of energy, including calculations of percentage energy transfer between trophic levels.
- [ ] Explain why energy availability and biomass usually decrease along a food chain, without claiming that each gram of biomass contains less energy.
- [ ] Explain why energy losses restrict the length of food chains.
- [ ] Distinguish primary production from secondary production and use appropriate units for rates of production.
- [ ] Construct a carbon cycle showing photosynthesis, feeding, respiration and combustion.
- [ ] Determine whether an ecosystem is a carbon sink or carbon source by comparing photosynthesis with respiration.
- [ ] Analyze the annual fluctuation and long-term trend in the Keeling Curve.
- [ ] Explain why energy flows through ecosystems while carbon and every other required chemical element must be recycled.

- **C1.2 Cell respiration** — respiration explains why carbon compounds lose chemical energy and carbon dioxide returns to the environment. It also explains the heat loss between trophic levels.
- **C1.3 Photosynthesis** — photosynthesis is the main route by which light energy enters food chains and inorganic carbon becomes biomass.
- **C4.1 Populations and communities** — feeding relationships connect populations into communities. C4.2 adds the direction and quantity of the transfers.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open system | A system across whose boundary both energy and matter can pass | A system that contains unlimited energy or matter |
| Closed system | A system that can exchange energy with its surroundings but doesn’t exchange matter | An isolated system that exchanges neither energy nor matter |
| Food chain | A sequence showing transfers of energy and biomass through feeding | A complete map of every feeding relationship in a community |
| Food web | Interconnected food chains showing feeding relationships in a community | A cycle in which energy returns to producers |
| Trophic level | An organism’s feeding position in a food chain | A fixed label for a species regardless of what it eats |
| Autotroph | An organism that uses an external energy source to make carbon compounds from simple inorganic substances | An organism that obtains organic carbon compounds by feeding |
| Photoautotroph | An autotroph that uses light as its external energy source | Any organism that lives where light is present |
| Chemoautotroph | An autotroph that gains energy from oxidation reactions involving inorganic substances | A decomposer obtaining energy from dead organic matter |
| Heterotroph | An organism that obtains carbon compounds from other organisms and uses them to build the compounds it requires | An organism defined only by being unable to photosynthesize |
| Decomposer | An organism, often a bacterium or fungus, that digests dead organic matter externally and absorbs the products | A detritivore that ingests pieces of dead material |
| Detritivore | A heterotroph that ingests dead organic matter and digests it internally | A saprotroph that secretes digestive enzymes onto its food |
| Energy pyramid | A diagram showing energy transferred or available at successive trophic levels per unit area per unit time | A pyramid showing the energy content of one gram of each organism |
| Primary production | Accumulation of carbon compounds in autotroph biomass | All energy absorbed by an ecosystem |
| Secondary production | Accumulation of carbon compounds in heterotroph biomass | Production by secondary consumers only |
| Carbon sink | A system with net carbon uptake, so more carbon enters than leaves during the period measured | Any place where carbon is stored |
| Carbon source | A system with net carbon release, so more carbon leaves than enters during the period measured | An ecosystem containing organisms that respire |
| Flux | A rate of transfer between stores | The amount held in a store at one moment |
| Keeling Curve | The record showing atmospheric carbon dioxide concentration, its seasonal oscillation and its long-term rise | A graph of global temperature or total ecosystem biomass |
