# B4.2 Ecological niches — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain an ecological niche as a species’ role in an ecosystem, including its use of resources and the biotic and abiotic interactions affecting growth, survival and reproduction.
- [ ] Distinguish obligate anaerobes, facultative anaerobes and obligate aerobes by their tolerance of oxygen.
- [ ] Outline photosynthesis as the mode of nutrition used by plants, algae and several groups of prokaryotes.
- [ ] Explain holozoic nutrition as ingestion, internal digestion, absorption and assimilation in animals.
- [ ] Explain mixotrophic nutrition and distinguish obligate from facultative mixotrophs.
- [ ] Explain how saprotrophic fungi and bacteria obtain nutrients and act as decomposers.
- [ ] State that archaea form one of the three domains and have diverse ways of obtaining energy for ATP production.
- [ ] Infer an omnivorous or herbivorous diet from hominid dentition and related skull features.
- [ ] Explain how insect mouthparts help herbivores feed and how physical and chemical plant defenses resist herbivory.
- [ ] Compare chemical, physical and behavioral adaptations used by predators and prey.
- [ ] Compare the ways canopy trees, lianas, epiphytes, strangler epiphytes and shade-tolerant plants obtain light in forests.
- [ ] Distinguish a species’ fundamental niche from its realized niche.
- [ ] Explain competitive exclusion and predict elimination or niche restriction when species compete strongly.
- [ ] Connect a species’ nutritional mode, adaptations and interactions to the ecological niche it occupies.

## Links to other topics

- **B4.1 Adaptation to environment** — tolerance limits and adaptations set the potential conditions in which a species can survive. That gives you the abiotic side of its fundamental niche.
- **C1.2 Cell respiration** — oxygen tolerance depends on which forms of respiration an organism can use and whether it can survive exposure to oxygen.
- **C1.3 Photosynthesis** — B4.2 identifies which organisms use photosynthetic nutrition; C1.3 explains how light energy is converted into chemical energy.
- **C4.1 Populations and communities** — competition, predation and herbivory are interactions that alter population sizes and determine realized niches.
- **C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter** — producers, consumers and decomposers have different nutritional roles in food chains and nutrient cycling.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — predator–prey and plant–herbivore interactions impose selection pressures that can lead to reciprocal adaptations.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ecological niche | The role of a species in an ecosystem, including resource use and the biotic and abiotic interactions affecting growth, survival and reproduction | The physical place where the species lives; that’s its habitat |
| Obligate anaerobe | An organism that can grow only without oxygen and can’t tolerate its presence | Any organism that sometimes carries out anaerobic respiration |
| Facultative anaerobe | An organism that can grow with or without oxygen | An organism that must alternate between aerobic and anaerobic conditions |
| Obligate aerobe | An organism that requires oxygen in its environment | An organism that merely grows faster when oxygen is available |
| Autotroph | An organism that makes organic carbon compounds from inorganic carbon | An organism that obtains organic nutrients by feeding |
| Heterotroph | An organism that obtains organic carbon compounds from other organisms or their products | An organism defined only by whether it uses oxygen |
| Holozoic nutrition | Ingestion of food followed by internal digestion, absorption and assimilation | External digestion followed by absorption |
| Mixotroph | An organism that combines autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition | A consumer that eats several kinds of food |
| Saprotrophic nutrition | External digestion of dead organic matter followed by absorption of soluble products | Ingestion and internal digestion of pieces of food |
| Dentition | The number, types, arrangement and form of teeth | The shape of the whole skull with no reference to teeth |
| Fundamental niche | The potential niche allowed by a species’ adaptations and tolerance limits | The part of the niche the species occupies while competitors are present |
| Realized niche | The actual extent of a species’ niche when competition with other species occurs | Every condition in which the species could survive without competitors |
| Competitive exclusion | The outcome in which species with the same niche can’t coexist indefinitely because competition eliminates one or restricts their niches | Predation removing every individual of a competing species |
