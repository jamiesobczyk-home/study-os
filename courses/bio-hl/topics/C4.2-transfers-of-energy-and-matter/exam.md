# C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Distinguish open and closed systems, using an ecosystem as an example. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- An open system exchanges both energy and matter with its surroundings. **[1]**
- A closed system exchanges energy but not matter with its surroundings. **[1]**
- A typical ecosystem is an open system. **[1]**
- Accept: light enters and heat leaves / organisms, gases, water or nutrients cross the ecosystem boundary. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “A closed system has no inputs or outputs” loses the distinction because a closed system can still exchange energy._

## Q2. Explain the reduction in energy available at successive trophic levels. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Some organisms / parts of organisms at one trophic level are not consumed. **[1]**
- Some ingested material is not digested or assimilated / is egested in feces. **[1]**
- Carbon compounds are oxidized in cell respiration. **[1]**
- Chemical energy is converted to heat during ATP production and ATP use / heat is transferred to the environment. **[1]**
- Less energy remains in biomass available to the next trophic level, restricting food-chain length. **[1]**

_Examiner note: energy isn’t destroyed. Full marks separate material that never reaches the consumer from assimilated energy that becomes dispersed as heat._

## Q3. Analyze energy transfer in the food chain shown below. **[4]**

Producers contain 24,000 kJ m−2 yr−1, primary consumers receive 2,400 kJ m−2 yr−1, and secondary consumers receive 144 kJ m−2 yr−1.

### Mark scheme

- Producer-to-primary-consumer efficiency is `(2,400 ÷ 24,000) × 100 = 10%`. **[1]**
- Primary-to-secondary-consumer efficiency is `(144 ÷ 2,400) × 100 = 6%`. **[1]**
- Energy availability decreases at each successive trophic level. **[1]**
- Accept: the second transfer is less efficient / a smaller percentage is transferred to secondary consumers. **[1]**

_Examiner note: subtracting the two energy values gives an energy loss, not a percentage transfer. Each calculation needs the preceding trophic level as its denominator._

## Q4. Compare and contrast autotrophs and heterotrophs. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Autotrophs synthesize carbon compounds from simple inorganic substances / use inorganic carbon such as carbon dioxide. **[1]**
- Autotrophs use an external energy source, such as light or energy from oxidation of inorganic substances. **[1]**
- Heterotrophs obtain carbon compounds from other organisms. **[1]**
- Heterotrophs digest complex carbon compounds and assimilate the products into required compounds. **[1]**
- Both autotrophs and heterotrophs oxidize carbon compounds in cell respiration to release energy / produce ATP. **[1]**

_Examiner note: calling autotrophs “organisms that don’t eat” misses their carbon source and external energy source. The shared use of respiration supplies the comparison._

## Q5. Explain how an ecosystem can change between being a carbon sink and a carbon source. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere / water and fixes it into organic compounds. **[1]**
- Respiration by autotrophs, heterotrophs and decomposers releases carbon dioxide. **[1]**
- Photosynthesis exceeding respiration and other releases produces net carbon uptake / a carbon sink. **[1]**
- Respiration, combustion and other releases exceeding photosynthesis produce net carbon release / a carbon source. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the presence of stored carbon doesn’t define a sink. The answer must compare rates of carbon uptake and release during the period considered._

## Q6. Explain the annual oscillation and long-term trend in the Keeling Curve. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Atmospheric carbon dioxide decreases during the Northern Hemisphere spring and summer / growing season. **[1]**
- Photosynthetic carbon dioxide uptake exceeds release during this period. **[1]**
- Atmospheric carbon dioxide increases during the Northern Hemisphere fall and winter. **[1]**
- Respiration / decomposition exceeds reduced photosynthetic uptake during this period. **[1]**
- The long-term trend is an increase in atmospheric carbon dioxide concentration. **[1]**
- Increased combustion of coal, oil, natural gas and biomass adds carbon dioxide faster than it is removed. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “plants cause the seasonal pattern” is incomplete. Full marks require the direction of each seasonal change, the balance of processes and a separate explanation for the long-term rise._
