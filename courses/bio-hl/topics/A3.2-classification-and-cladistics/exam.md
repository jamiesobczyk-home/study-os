# A3.2 Classification and cladistics — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain why classification based on evolutionary relationships is useful. **[4]**

Classification systems can be based on general similarity or on evolutionary relationships. Explain two advantages of using evolutionary relationships.

### Mark scheme

- Organisms descended from a common ancestor are placed in the same group / clade. **[1]**
- Members of the group share inherited / homologous characteristics. **[1]**
- Shared ancestry allows characteristics of organisms in the group to be predicted. **[1]**
- Classification is less likely to be misled by superficial similarities produced by convergent evolution / analogous structures. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the organisms “look similar” misses the advantage. Full marks require common ancestry and a consequence of grouping by that ancestry._

## Q2. Distinguish the traditional taxonomic hierarchy from classification using clades. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- The traditional hierarchy uses fixed ranks of kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. **[1]**
- Fixed ranks are arbitrary / may not correspond to the gradation of variation or evolutionary divergence. **[1]**
- A clade contains a common ancestor and all of its descendants. **[1]**
- Clades are unranked / can be nested without being restricted to a fixed number of levels. **[1]**

_Examiner note: listing the seven ranks earns only the first mark. The distinction requires the limitation of fixed ranks and the ancestry-based definition of a clade._

## Q3. Explain how molecular sequence data can be used to construct a cladogram. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Base sequences from homologous genes / amino acid sequences from homologous proteins are obtained from the organisms. **[1]**
- Sequences are aligned so equivalent positions can be compared. **[1]**
- Fewer sequence differences indicate a more recent common ancestor / closer evolutionary relationship. **[1]**
- Alternative branching patterns can be compared using the observed sequence variation. **[1]**
- Parsimony analysis selects the cladogram requiring the smallest number of sequence changes. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “more similar DNA means more closely related” is a start, but it leaves out alignment, alternative branching patterns and the parsimony criterion used to choose between them._

## Q4. Analyze evolutionary relationships shown by a cladogram. **[4]**

In a rooted cladogram, species A and B meet at node 1. Their lineage joins species C at node 2. That combined lineage joins species D at the root.

### Mark scheme

- A and B are the most closely related pair / share the most recent common ancestor. **[1]**
- A, B and C form a clade if node 2 and all of its descendants are included. **[1]**
- D shares a common ancestor with A, B and C at the root. **[1]**
- Rotating the branches around node 1 or node 2 would not change the evolutionary relationships. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the answer must use nodes and common ancestry. The written order A, B, C, D isn’t evidence of relatedness by itself._

## Q5. Explain how a molecular clock estimates when two clades diverged. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Mutations / sequence differences accumulate gradually after lineages diverge from a common ancestor. **[1]**
- A larger number of differences generally indicates more time since divergence. **[1]**
- The number of differences is compared with an estimated / calibrated rate of sequence change. **[1]**
- The result is an estimate because mutation rates vary with generation time, population size, selection pressure or other factors. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming the molecular clock doesn’t explain it. Full marks require the link from accumulated differences to time and one reason the rate isn’t constant._

## Q6. Outline how molecular evidence led to the three-domain classification. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- rRNA base sequences were compared among organisms. **[1]**
- Sequence differences provided evidence of evolutionary relationships / divergence. **[1]**
- Organisms previously grouped together as prokaryotes were shown to form two distinct groups. **[1]**
- The three domains are Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya. **[1]**
- Domain was introduced as a taxonomic level above kingdom / the three-domain system was proposed in 1990. **[1]**

_Examiner note: cell structure alone can separate prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, but this question asks for the rRNA evidence that split the prokaryotes into Bacteria and Archaea._
