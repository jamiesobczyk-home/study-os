# A3.2 Classification and cladistics

- **Theme:** A — Unity and diversity
- **Level:** Organisms
- **Level of study:** HL only
- **Status:** ready

## The one-sentence version

Classification and cladistics use shared characteristics and molecular sequences to group organisms by common ancestry and represent that ancestry in branching diagrams.

## Why this topic is worth getting right

A3.2 turns diagrams and sequence comparisons into evidence about ancestry. The hard part isn’t remembering that DNA can show relatedness; it’s producing the chain from sequence differences, through parsimony and nodes, to a clade or revised classification. Practice by drawing and rotating cladograms, then explain each conclusion using the most recent common ancestor rather than where a name sits on the page.

## In your study guide

the capture sheet, never before. It's condensed enough to make a fast check of
what you left out, and a slow way to meet the topic for the first time.
