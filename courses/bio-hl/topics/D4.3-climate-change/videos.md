# D4.3 Climate change — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain the causal chains from memory. A second explanation can feel productive while building more recognition, which is the problem you’re trying to stop.

## Main

**Alex Lee Biology — D4.3 Climate change**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+D4.3+Climate+change>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation made for D4.3.

**Watch for:**

- Where each positive feedback cycle begins and how it returns to amplify warming.
- Why a boreal forest can change from a carbon sink to a carbon source.
- Which coral effect is caused by carbon dioxide and which is caused by temperature.
- Which examples belong to HL: phenology, spruce bark beetles and tawny owl evolution.

**Pause at:** the first positive feedback diagram. Cover its labels and rebuild it as a loop with arrows. Every arrow should have a spoken explanation, such as “less ice lowers albedo, so more solar radiation is absorbed.”

## Worth it once, not for revision

Ocean upwelling is a spatial process, so an **animation** earns an exception to the one-video rule. Motion makes the relationship among surface warming, stratification and nutrient movement easier to see.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=ocean+upwelling+warming+stratification+animation>

After watching, draw a vertical section through the ocean. Add warm surface water, cold deep water, nutrient movement and phytoplankton. Then explain why reduced mixing lowers primary production.
