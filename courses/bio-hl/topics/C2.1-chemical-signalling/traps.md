# C2.1 Chemical signalling — where the marks go

## Treating every cell reached by a hormone as a target

**Commonly written:** “A hormone travels in the blood and affects all the cells it reaches.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Blood distributes the hormone widely, but only cells with the specific receptor can detect it and respond.

**Scores instead:** “A hormone may reach many cells in the blood, but only target cells with its specific receptor produce a response.”

## Saying bacteria count their neighbors

**Commonly written:** “In quorum sensing, bacteria count nearby cells and switch on together.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The measured variable is signalling-molecule concentration, which rises with population density and activates receptors after reaching a threshold.

**Scores instead:** “As bacterial density rises, autoinducer concentration reaches a threshold that activates receptors and changes gene expression.”

## Making the ligand pass through a membrane receptor

**Commonly written:** “Epinephrine enters the cell through its receptor and activates cAMP.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Epinephrine remains outside; receptor activation is relayed through a G protein and adenylyl cyclase before cAMP is produced inside.

**Scores instead:** “Epinephrine binds to a GPCR, which activates a G protein and adenylyl cyclase, causing cAMP production inside the cell.”

## Calling cAMP the hormone

**Commonly written:** “cAMP carries the epinephrine signal through the blood to its receptor.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Epinephrine is the extracellular first messenger, while cAMP is an intracellular second messenger made after receptor activation.

**Scores instead:** “Epinephrine is the first messenger, and receptor activation causes cAMP to act as the second messenger inside the cell.”

## Saying insulin carries glucose through the membrane

**Commonly written:** “Insulin transports glucose into muscle and fat cells.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Insulin is a ligand, not a transporter; its pathway moves glucose transporter vesicles to the plasma membrane.

**Scores instead:** “Insulin receptor activation moves glucose transporters to the plasma membrane, increasing glucose uptake by facilitated diffusion.”

## Putting steroid receptors in the plasma membrane

**Commonly written:** “Progesterone binds to a membrane receptor because hormones can’t enter cells.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Progesterone is a lipid-soluble steroid that crosses the bilayer and binds an intracellular receptor in the cytoplasm or nucleus.

**Scores instead:** “Progesterone diffuses through the plasma membrane and activates an intracellular receptor that regulates transcription.”

## Defining feedback by whether the outcome is helpful

**Commonly written:** “Positive feedback is beneficial, while negative feedback is harmful.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The terms describe the direction of regulation, not whether the biological outcome is good or bad.

**Scores instead:** “Positive feedback reinforces the original change, whereas negative feedback opposes it and moves the variable toward its regulated range.”
