# D2.1 Cell and nuclear division — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how cytokinesis differs in animal and plant cells, including the contractile ring, cleavage furrow, vesicles and cell plate.
- [ ] Explain why cytokinesis may be equal or unequal, using human oogenesis and budding in yeast as examples.
- [ ] Compare the roles and outcomes of mitosis and meiosis, including their effects on chromosome number and genetic variation.
- [ ] Explain why DNA must replicate before mitosis or meiosis and distinguish a replicated chromosome from its sister chromatids.
- [ ] Explain how histones, microtubules and microtubule motors help chromosomes condense and move during nuclear division.
- [ ] Describe and identify prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase from chromosome behavior.
- [ ] Explain why meiosis is a reduction division, including what separates in meiosis I and meiosis II.
- [ ] Explain how crossing over and random orientation of bivalents generate genetic variation.
- [ ] Explain how non-disjunction during meiosis can produce a gamete that leads to trisomy 21 and Down syndrome.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how cell proliferation supports growth, routine cell replacement and tissue repair, using named examples.
- [ ] Put G1, S, G2, mitosis and cytokinesis in the correct order and state what happens during each part of the cell cycle.
- [ ] Explain why interphase is metabolically active, including biosynthesis and the growth and division of organelles.
- [ ] Explain how changing cyclin concentrations and threshold levels control passage through cell-cycle checkpoints.
- [ ] Explain how oncogenes and mutated tumour suppressor genes can cause uncontrolled cell division.
- [ ] Distinguish benign, malignant, primary and secondary tumours, and calculate and interpret a mitotic index.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell division | Production of daughter cells from a parent cell | Nuclear division alone |
| Mitosis | Nuclear division that maintains chromosome number and usually produces genetically identical nuclei | Division of the cytoplasm |
| Cytokinesis | Division of a parent cell’s cytoplasm between daughter cells | Separation of chromosomes inside the nucleus |
| Chromosome | One DNA molecule associated with proteins; after replication it consists of two sister chromatids | Always an X-shaped structure |
| Sister chromatids | Replicated copies of one chromosome, joined until they separate | A maternal and paternal pair of homologous chromosomes |
| Homologous chromosomes | A maternal and paternal chromosome with the same genes at corresponding loci | Identical copies produced by DNA replication |
| Bivalent | A pair of homologous chromosomes associated during meiosis I | The two sister chromatids of one chromosome |
| Diploid | Having two sets of chromosomes, one set inherited from each parent | Having two chromatids per chromosome |
| Haploid | Having one set of chromosomes | A cell with unreplicated DNA |
| Meiosis | Two nuclear divisions that produce four haploid nuclei from one diploid nucleus | Mitosis followed by cytokinesis |
| Crossing over | Exchange of DNA between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during prophase I | Random separation of whole homologous chromosomes |
| Non-disjunction | Failure of homologous chromosomes or sister chromatids to separate correctly | A DNA base substitution |
| Cell cycle | G1, S and G2 of interphase, followed by mitosis and cytokinesis | Mitosis alone |
| Cyclin | A regulatory protein whose changing concentration helps control passage through cell-cycle checkpoints | A chromosome-moving microtubule |
| Proto-oncogene | A normal gene whose product promotes cell growth or division when appropriate | A cancer-causing gene in its normal form |
| Oncogene | A mutated or overactive form of a proto-oncogene that can promote uncontrolled division | Any gene found in a tumour cell |
| Tumour suppressor gene | A gene whose product restrains cell-cycle progression or promotes responses such as apoptosis | A gene that stimulates normal cell proliferation |
| Metastasis | Spread of cancer cells from a primary tumour to other locations, where secondary tumours may form | Expansion of a tumour at its original location |
| Mitotic index | The proportion or percentage of observed cells undergoing mitosis | The percentage of cells in interphase |
