# B3.2 Transport — where the marks go

## Defining arteries by oxygen content

**Commonly written:** "Arteries carry oxygenated blood, while veins carry deoxygenated blood."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Oxygen content has pulmonary exceptions. Vessel type is defined by the direction of blood flow relative to the heart.

**Scores instead:** "Arteries carry blood away from the heart, while veins carry blood toward the heart."

## Giving valves the job of pumping blood

**Commonly written:** "Valves squeeze the blood through veins."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Valves don't generate force. Skeletal muscle contractions compress veins, while valves prevent the resulting blood movement from reversing.

**Scores instead:** "Skeletal muscles compress veins and move blood, while pocket valves prevent backflow."

## Treating correlation as proof of coronary disease

**Commonly written:** "A correlation between a high-fat diet and heart attacks proves that the diet caused them."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A correlation alone can't exclude confounding variables or establish the direction and mechanism of causation.

**Scores instead:** "The correlation identifies an association with risk, but controlled evidence and a plausible mechanism are needed to support causation."

## Saying cohesion creates transpiration pull

**Commonly written:** "Cohesion pulls water up the xylem."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Cohesion transmits tension through the water column. Evaporation from leaf cell walls is what generates the tension.

**Scores instead:** "Evaporation from leaves creates negative pressure in xylem, and cohesion transmits this tension through a continuous water column."

## Calling tissue fluid escaped blood

**Commonly written:** "Blood leaks out of capillaries and becomes tissue fluid."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Pressure filtration removes water and small solutes, but blood cells and most plasma proteins remain inside the capillary.

**Scores instead:** "Hydrostatic pressure filters water and small solutes through the capillary wall, forming tissue fluid without releasing blood cells."

## Saying a fish circuit has no pressure after the gills

**Commonly written:** "Blood stops after passing through the fish's gill capillaries because all its pressure is lost."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Gill capillaries reduce blood pressure, but a remaining pressure gradient continues to drive blood through systemic vessels.

**Scores instead:** "Pressure falls across the gill capillaries, so blood reaches the body at lower pressure but continues flowing."

## Saying heart valves actively open

**Commonly written:** "The valves contract to open and close during the cardiac cycle."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Heart valves aren't pumps or contracting gates. They move passively when the pressure difference across them changes.

**Scores instead:** "A valve opens when pressure behind it exceeds pressure ahead and closes when the pressure difference reverses."

## Making root pressure the main transpiration force

**Commonly written:** "Active transport in the roots pumps water to the top of every plant."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Active transport moves mineral ions, not water. Water enters by osmosis, and the resulting root pressure is limited.

**Scores instead:** "Active transport of ions into xylem lowers its water potential, so water enters by osmosis and generates positive root pressure."

## Calling all phloem movement active transport

**Commonly written:** "ATP pumps sucrose all the way from a leaf to a root through the phloem."

**Why it doesn’t score:** ATP is used during loading and unloading, while long-distance movement of phloem sap is pressure-driven mass flow.

**Scores instead:** "Sucrose loading creates an osmotic influx of water and a hydrostatic pressure gradient that drives mass flow toward a sink."
