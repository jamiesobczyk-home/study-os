# A2.2 Cell structure — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and draw or explain what you remember. A second full explanation builds recognition again, and recognition is the habit you’re trying to replace with recall.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — A2.2 Cell structure**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+A2.2+Cell+structure>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so this is the best first pass through the exact A2.2 scope.

**Watch for:**

- Which structures occur in all typical cells, and why each one is needed.
- Which observations distinguish a prokaryotic cell from a eukaryotic cell.
- How animal, fungal and plant cells differ without treating any diagram as universal.
- Which three observations support the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts.

**Pause at:** the first comparison of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. Cover the labels and make two columns from memory. Put DNA location, ribosome type, nucleus and membrane-bound organelles into both columns before continuing.

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@khanacademy> — verified

Use this when you recognize organelle names but can’t produce their functions or connect them into a cell.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Freeze fracture is a mechanical process, so an **animation** earns an extra watch. Seeing a frozen membrane split through the hydrophobic interior makes the resulting fracture faces easier to interpret.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=freeze+fracture+electron+microscopy+animation>

After watching, explain aloud what gets frozen, where the membrane splits and what the exposed faces can reveal. Don’t replay it until you’ve tried to produce all three answers.

For micrographs, spend one session practicing identification rather than watching another explanation.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=IB+Biology+A2.2+cell+micrograph+identification>

Pause before each answer appears. Name the cell type, give two visible pieces of evidence and identify any organelles you can defend from the image.
