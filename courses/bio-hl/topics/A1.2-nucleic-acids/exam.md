# A1.2 Nucleic acids — exam practice

> Write an answer before revealing the scheme. If you only recognize it after the reveal, you haven't produced it yet.

## Q1. Outline how RNA nucleotides form a strand with a sugar–phosphate backbone. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

* Each nucleotide contains a phosphate group, ribose sugar and nitrogenous base. **[1]**
* A condensation reaction joins nucleotides / releases water. **[1]**
* A covalent phosphodiester bond links the 3′ sugar position of one nucleotide to the phosphate at the 5′ position of the next. **[1]**
* Alternating sugars and phosphates form the backbone, with bases projecting from the strand. **[1]**

*Examiner note: naming phosphodiester bonds isn't enough for four marks. Place the bond between neighboring nucleotides, then show the continuous backbone and its outward-facing bases.*

## Q2. Compare and contrast DNA and RNA in structure. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

* Both are nucleotide polymers with covalent sugar–phosphate backbones. **[1]**
* DNA contains deoxyribose, whereas RNA contains ribose / ribose has a 2′ OH where deoxyribose has H. **[1]**
* DNA contains thymine, whereas RNA contains uracil. **[1]**
* Both contain adenine, guanine and cytosine. **[1]**
* DNA is usually double-stranded, whereas RNA is usually single-stranded. **[1]**

*Examiner note: answer in paired comparisons. Saying RNA never forms paired regions overstates the difference, since a single strand can fold back on itself.*

## Q3. Explain how DNA structure allows genetic information to be copied accurately. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

* The order of bases along a strand stores genetic information. **[1]**
* The two strands are complementary / each can act as a template. **[1]**
* Adenine pairs with thymine and guanine pairs with cytosine. **[1]**
* Specific hydrogen bonding between complementary bases selects bases on each new strand. **[1]**
* The new strand is synthesized 5′ to 3′ while the template is read antiparallel / in the opposite direction. **[1]**

*Examiner note: “DNA unzips and copies itself” skips the information-carrying base order and the pairing rules. State both before describing the new strand.*

## Q4. Explain why purine-to-pyrimidine pairing contributes to the stability of the DNA helix. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

* Adenine and guanine are two-ring purines. **[1]**
* Thymine and cytosine are one-ring pyrimidines. **[1]**
* A–T and G–C each pair one purine with one pyrimidine. **[1]**
* The pairs have similar widths / keep the distance between backbones consistent along the helix. **[1]**

*Examiner note: don't substitute the number of hydrogen bonds for the geometric reason. This question asks why the helix can keep a consistent width across different sequences.*

## Q5. Explain how the results of the Hershey–Chase experiment supported DNA as the genetic material of the bacteriophage. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

* Phage DNA was labeled with phosphorus-32 in one sample and phage protein with sulfur-35 in another. **[1]**
* Phages infected bacteria, then blending detached coats and centrifugation separated bacteria in the pellet from coats in the supernatant. **[1]**
* Most phosphorus-32 was found with the bacterial pellet. **[1]**
* Most sulfur-35 stayed in the supernatant / with the phage coats. **[1]**
* DNA entered bacteria and directed production of new phages / the result supports DNA rather than protein as the phage's hereditary material. **[1]**

*Examiner note: if you can't place each isotope after centrifugation, the conclusion has no evidence. Keep the label, fraction and inference together.*

## Q6. Suggest what Chargaff's measurements show about DNA, given one species with A 30%, T 30%, G 20% and C 20%, and another with A 20%, T 20%, G 30% and C 30%. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

* In each species A is approximately equal to T. **[1]**
* In each species G is approximately equal to C. **[1]**
* Base proportions vary between species / A+T content differs between species. **[1]**
* The variation refutes a fixed, equal-proportion repeating tetranucleotide pattern / supports capacity for differing base sequences. **[1]**

*Examiner note: four values that sum to 100% don't answer the question. Compare the paired proportions within each species and the composition between species.*
