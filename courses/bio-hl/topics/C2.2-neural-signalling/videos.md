# C2.2 Neural signalling — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and draw the process from memory. A second explanation can make the material feel familiar without making you able to produce it, which is the problem you’re fixing.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — C2.2 Neural signalling**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+C2.2+Neural+signalling>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so this is the best place to start.

**Watch for:**

- Which ion movements create the resting potential, depolarization and repolarization.
- How calcium connects arrival of an action potential to exocytosis at a synapse.
- Why an excitatory postsynaptic potential isn’t automatically an action potential.
- Which parts are additional HL, especially local currents, saltatory conduction and summation.

**Pause at:** the first complete action-potential graph. Cover its labels, point to resting potential, threshold, depolarization and repolarization, then name the channel and ion movement responsible for each change.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Propagation is spatial: one region of an axon changes the next, and myelin changes where action potentials are regenerated. An **animation** earns an extra watch because movement is hard to recover from a still diagram.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=action+potential+propagation+saltatory+conduction+animation>

After watching, draw an axon with three nodes of Ranvier. Add arrows showing local current, then explain why action potentials are regenerated at the nodes rather than underneath the myelin.
