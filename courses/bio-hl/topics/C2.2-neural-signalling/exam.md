# C2.2 Neural signalling — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain the generation of a resting potential in a neuron. **[4]**

Explain how ion transport and membrane permeability produce a negative potential inside a resting neuron.

### Mark scheme

- ATP supplies energy to the sodium-potassium pump / active transport is used. **[1]**
- Three Na+ are pumped out for every two K+ pumped in / Na+ and K+ are pumped in opposite directions. **[1]**
- The resting membrane is more permeable to K+ than to Na+ / K+ diffuses out through leak channels. **[1]**
- Net loss of positive charge / retained intracellular anions makes the inside negative relative to the outside. **[1]**

_Examiner note: stating that sodium is outside and potassium is inside describes the gradients but doesn’t explain the negative voltage. Full marks require selective permeability and unequal charge movement._

## Q2. Explain transmission across a cholinergic synapse. **[6]**

Begin with the arrival of an action potential at the presynaptic terminal and end with an excitatory postsynaptic potential.

### Mark scheme

- Arrival of an action potential depolarizes the presynaptic membrane. **[1]**
- Voltage-gated Ca2+ channels open and Ca2+ enters the presynaptic terminal. **[1]**
- Ca2+ causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane / acetylcholine is released by exocytosis. **[1]**
- Acetylcholine diffuses across the synaptic cleft. **[1]**
- Acetylcholine binds to specific transmembrane receptors on the postsynaptic membrane. **[1]**
- Ligand-gated cation channels open / net entry of positive ions depolarizes the postsynaptic membrane, producing an EPSP. **[1]**

_Examiner note: calcium entry and sodium entry occur at different membranes and have different jobs. Mixing them usually loses both mechanism marks._

## Q3. Explain propagation of an action potential along an axon. **[5]**

Include the ion movements that produce an action potential and the local currents that affect the next region.

### Mark scheme

- Threshold causes voltage-gated Na+ channels to open / Na+ enters and depolarizes the membrane. **[1]**
- Voltage-gated Na+ channels inactivate and voltage-gated K+ channels open. **[1]**
- K+ exits, causing repolarization / temporary hyperpolarization. **[1]**
- Na+ entering one region diffuses sideways inside the axon / produces a local current. **[1]**
- The adjacent membrane reaches threshold and generates a new action potential. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer describing the graph can earn the first three marks, but propagation requires local current and threshold in the adjacent membrane._

## Q4. Compare and contrast conduction in myelinated and non-myelinated axons. **[4]**

Refer to ion channels, the location of action potentials and conduction speed.

### Mark scheme

- Both propagate action potentials using changes in membrane potential / movements of ions across the axon membrane. **[1]**
- In myelinated axons, pumps and channels are concentrated at nodes of Ranvier / myelin insulates the regions between nodes. **[1]**
- In myelinated axons, action potentials are regenerated from node to node / conduction is saltatory. **[1]**
- In non-myelinated axons, action potentials are regenerated continuously along the membrane, so conduction is slower. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the impulse “jumps” can describe the pattern, but full marks require regeneration at nodes and continuous regeneration in the comparison._

## Q5. Explain how excitatory and inhibitory inputs determine whether a postsynaptic neuron fires. **[4]**

A postsynaptic neuron receives signals from several presynaptic neurons at about the same time.

### Mark scheme

- Excitatory neurotransmitters generate EPSPs / depolarize the postsynaptic membrane toward threshold. **[1]**
- Inhibitory neurotransmitters generate IPSPs / hyperpolarize the postsynaptic membrane away from threshold. **[1]**
- Postsynaptic potentials combine by summation / excitatory and inhibitory effects are integrated. **[1]**
- An action potential occurs only if the net effect reaches threshold; otherwise no action potential occurs. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “excitatory signals win” is too vague. The scoring answer links both kinds of graded potential to threshold and the all-or-nothing outcome._

## Q6. Suggest how capsaicin can cause the perception of burning pain without raising skin temperature. **[4]**

Capsaicin is a chemical found in chili peppers that stimulates free nerve endings in the skin.

### Mark scheme

- Capsaicin binds to / stimulates receptor channels in free sensory nerve endings. **[1]**
- Channels for positively charged ions open and positive ions enter the neuron. **[1]**
- The membrane reaches threshold and action potentials are generated. **[1]**
- Impulses pass through neurons to the brain, where pain is perceived. **[1]**

_Examiner note: capsaicin doesn’t heat the skin. It activates part of the neural pathway that can also respond to damaging heat._
