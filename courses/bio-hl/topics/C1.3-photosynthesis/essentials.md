# C1.3 Photosynthesis — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how photosynthesis transforms light energy into chemical energy stored in carbon compounds.
- [ ] Explain how carbon dioxide and water supply the atoms used to make glucose, including the origin of the oxygen released.
- [ ] Explain why photosynthetic pigments absorb some wavelengths of visible light more strongly than others.
- [ ] Distinguish an absorption spectrum from an action spectrum and explain why their patterns are related.
- [ ] Separate photosynthetic pigments by chromatography, calculate an Rf value and use reference data to identify a pigment.
- [ ] Design an investigation into how light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration or temperature affects photosynthetic rate.
- [ ] Interpret limiting-factor graphs, including plateaus caused by another factor becoming limiting.
- [ ] Evaluate how carbon dioxide enrichment experiments can be used to predict future photosynthesis and plant growth.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how pigments organized into photosystems absorb light, transfer energy and cause a reaction-center chlorophyll to emit an excited electron.
- [ ] Trace the light-dependent reactions from photosystem II through photolysis, electron transport, chemiosmosis, photosystem I and reduction of NADP.
- [ ] Explain how the thylakoid membrane, thylakoid lumen and stroma provide the organization needed for the light-dependent reactions.
- [ ] Explain carbon fixation by rubisco and the formation of glycerate 3-phosphate from carbon dioxide and RuBP.
- [ ] Explain how ATP and reduced NADP are used to produce triose phosphate and regenerate RuBP in the Calvin cycle.
- [ ] Explain how triose phosphate and mineral nutrients are used to make carbohydrates, amino acids and other carbon compounds.
- [ ] Explain the interdependence of the light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — ATP and reduced NADP transfer energy or reducing power between reactions. Recognizing their roles prevents photosynthesis from becoming a list of unexplained arrows.
- **B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization** — thylakoid membranes separate the lumen from the stroma, allowing a proton gradient to form. Compartmentalization explains why chemiosmosis can work.
- **B3.1 Gas exchange** — carbon dioxide must diffuse into a leaf and oxygen must diffuse out. Stomatal behavior therefore affects both photosynthesis and water loss.
- **C1.2 Cell respiration** — photosynthesis stores energy in reduced carbon compounds, while respiration releases usable energy from them. ATP is involved in both, but the pathways have different purposes.
- **C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter** — photosynthesis brings carbon into food chains and converts solar energy into chemical energy available to organisms.
- **D2.3 Water potential** — opening stomata increases carbon dioxide entry but also increases water loss, connecting photosynthetic rate with plant water balance.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photosynthesis | The transformation of light energy into chemical energy as cells produce carbon compounds from inorganic substances | The release of energy from glucose |
| Photosynthetic pigment | A molecule that absorbs particular wavelengths of light | An enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide |
| Absorption spectrum | A graph showing how strongly a pigment absorbs different wavelengths | A graph of photosynthetic rate |
| Action spectrum | A graph showing the rate of photosynthesis at different wavelengths | The absorption spectrum of one isolated pigment |
| Chromatography | Separation of substances according to differences in their movement through a stationary phase with a mobile solvent | Separation caused by pigment color alone |
| Rf value | Distance traveled by a pigment divided by distance traveled by the solvent front | The distance traveled by the pigment in centimeters |
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply relative to demand and therefore restricting the rate | Any factor that affects photosynthesis |
| Photosystem | An organized array of pigments and proteins containing a reaction center in a membrane | A single chlorophyll molecule floating in the stroma |
| Photolysis | Light-driven splitting of water, producing electrons, protons and oxygen | Splitting carbon dioxide to release oxygen |
| Chemiosmosis | Movement of protons down an electrochemical gradient through ATP synthase, driving ATP production | Electron movement through the electron transport chain |
| Photophosphorylation | Production of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate using light-driven electron flow and chemiosmosis | Addition of phosphate directly to glucose |
| Carbon fixation | Incorporation of inorganic carbon dioxide into an organic compound | Production of carbon dioxide during respiration |
| RuBP | A five-carbon carbon dioxide acceptor in the Calvin cycle | The enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide |
| Rubisco | The enzyme that catalyzes the reaction of carbon dioxide with RuBP | The five-carbon substrate in that reaction |
| Reduced NADP | An electron and hydrogen carrier produced by the light-dependent reactions, often written NADPH | ATP or an enzyme |
| Triose phosphate | A three-carbon product of the Calvin cycle used to make carbon compounds or regenerate RuBP | Glucose itself |
