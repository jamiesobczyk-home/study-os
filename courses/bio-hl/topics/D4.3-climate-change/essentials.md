# D4.3 Climate change — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how human activities increase atmospheric carbon dioxide and methane, and how these gases strengthen the greenhouse effect.
- [ ] Distinguish positive correlation from negative correlation and explain why correlation alone doesn’t establish causation.
- [ ] Construct positive feedback cycles involving ocean carbon dioxide, snow and ice, peat and permafrost, methane, drought and forest fires.
- [ ] Explain how boreal forests can cross a tipping point from net carbon accumulation to net carbon loss.
- [ ] Explain how the loss of landfast ice affects emperor penguins and how the loss of sea ice affects walruses.
- [ ] Explain how warmer surface water can reduce nutrient upwelling, primary production and energy flow through marine food chains.
- [ ] Explain why climate change causes poleward and upslope range shifts, using the required bird and tree examples.
- [ ] Explain separately how ocean acidification suppresses coral calcification and how higher water temperature causes coral bleaching.
- [ ] Explain how afforestation, forest regeneration and restoration of peat-forming wetlands can sequester carbon.

## Higher level

- [ ] Define phenology and explain how temperature and photoperiod influence the timing of biological events.
- [ ] Explain how climate change can disrupt synchrony between interacting species, using a named example.
- [ ] Explain why warming can increase the number of spruce bark beetle life cycles completed within a year.
- [ ] Explain how reduced snow cover can change the fitness of tawny owl color variants and cause evolution by natural selection.

## Links to other topics

- **B4.1 Adaptation to environment** — every species has a range of tolerance for abiotic conditions. That explains why warming changes distributions and why coral reefs are vulnerable to changes in temperature and pH.
- **C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter** — carbon sinks, carbon sources, primary production, food chains and combustion supply the mechanisms behind climate feedback and ecosystem change.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — climate change alters selection pressures. D4.1 lets you explain why a change in tawny owl morph frequencies counts as evolution.
- **D4.2 Stability and change** — positive feedback, tipping points and ecosystem collapse show how a stable system can be pushed into a different state.
- **A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity** — range contraction, habitat loss and coral reef collapse connect climate change to species and ecosystem conservation.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anthropogenic | Caused by human activity | Any change that happens during the period when humans are present |
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Additional warming caused by increased concentrations of greenhouse gases from human activity | The natural greenhouse effect, which keeps Earth warm enough for life |
| Positive correlation | A relationship in which both variables tend to increase together or decrease together | Proof that one variable caused the other to change |
| Causation | A relationship in which a change in one factor produces a change in another | A pattern in which two variables happen to change together |
| Positive feedback | A cycle in which an initial change causes effects that amplify that change | A beneficial effect or a response that returns a system toward its starting state |
| Albedo | The proportion of incoming radiation reflected by a surface | The amount of infrared radiation absorbed by a greenhouse gas |
| Tipping point | A threshold beyond which self-reinforcing changes shift a system into a different state | Every gradual change in an ecosystem |
| Carbon sink | A system that absorbs more carbon than it releases | Any place where carbon is present |
| Carbon source | A system that releases more carbon than it absorbs | A store that contains a large amount of carbon but has no net release |
| Legacy carbon | Carbon stored over long periods in old biomass or soil | Carbon fixed during the current growing season |
| Landfast ice | Sea ice attached to the coast or seafloor and held relatively stationary | Ice formed on land, such as a glacier |
| Upwelling | Movement of deeper, nutrient-rich water toward the ocean surface | Sinking of warm surface water |
| Ocean acidification | A decrease in ocean pH caused by absorption of carbon dioxide | Coral bleaching caused by high temperature |
| Calcification | Production of calcium carbonate structures, including coral skeletons | Uptake of carbon dioxide during photosynthesis |
| Coral bleaching | Loss of photosynthetic symbionts or their pigments from coral tissue, commonly caused by heat stress | Immediate death of every bleached coral or dissolution of its skeleton |
| Carbon sequestration | Capture and long-term storage of carbon | Any short-term transfer of carbon between organisms |
| Phenology | Study of the timing of recurring biological events | Study of an organism’s physical appearance |
| Phenological synchrony | Matching in the timing of biological events in interacting populations | All species responding to temperature at the same rate |
| Range shift | Change in the geographical area occupied by a species | An individual organism changing its adaptations during its lifetime |
