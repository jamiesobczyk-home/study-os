# B4.2 Ecological niches — where the marks go

## Treating a niche as a place

**Commonly written:** “A niche is the place where a species lives.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A place is a habitat. A niche includes resource use and the biotic and abiotic interactions affecting growth, survival and reproduction.

**Scores instead:** “A niche is a species’ role in an ecosystem, including how it obtains resources and interacts with its biotic and abiotic environment.”

## Calling every organism without oxygen an obligate anaerobe

**Commonly written:** “Any organism carrying out anaerobic respiration is an obligate anaerobe.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The category depends on tolerance of environmental oxygen. Facultative anaerobes can grow both with and without oxygen.

**Scores instead:** “An obligate anaerobe can grow only without oxygen, whereas a facultative anaerobe can grow in either its presence or absence.”

## Mixing up absorption and assimilation

**Commonly written:** “After digestion, food is assimilated through the wall of the intestine.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Movement of digested nutrients into body fluids or cells is absorption. Assimilation is their subsequent use in tissues.

**Scores instead:** “Products of internal digestion are absorbed and then assimilated into the animal’s cells and tissues.”

## Saying saprotrophs ingest dead material

**Commonly written:** “Saprotrophic fungi swallow dead material and digest it inside their bodies.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Saprotrophs secrete enzymes onto dead material, digest it externally and absorb the soluble products.

**Scores instead:** “A saprotroph digests dead organic matter externally with secreted enzymes, then absorbs the soluble products.”

## Treating epiphytes as parasites

**Commonly written:** “An epiphyte takes nutrients from the tree so it can reach the light.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** An epiphyte uses the tree as physical support. Growing on another plant doesn’t by itself mean obtaining nutrients from it.

**Scores instead:** “An epiphyte grows on a tree branch for support, placing it nearer the light without being defined as a parasite.”

## Reversing fundamental and realized niches

**Commonly written:** “The realized niche is everywhere a species could live, while the fundamental niche is where it actually lives.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Fundamental refers to potential based on adaptations and tolerance limits. Realized refers to the actual niche under competition.

**Scores instead:** “The fundamental niche is the species’ potential niche, while its realized niche is the actual extent occupied when competitors are present.”

## Making competitive exclusion mean immediate extinction

**Commonly written:** “When two species compete, one always becomes extinct.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Competition may cause local elimination, but it can instead restrict both species to different parts of their fundamental niches.

**Scores instead:** “Strong niche overlap may eliminate one competitor locally or restrict both species to narrower, more distinct realized niches.”
