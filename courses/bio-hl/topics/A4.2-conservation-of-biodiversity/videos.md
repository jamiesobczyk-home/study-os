# A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and produce the eight syllabus ideas from memory. A second explanation can make the material feel familiar without making it retrievable, and recognition is the problem you’re fixing.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+A4.2+Conservation+of+biodiversity>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the version designed around A4.2.

**Watch for:**

- What changes as the explanation moves from genetic to species to ecosystem diversity.
- Which observations count as evidence of a biodiversity crisis, rather than causes of the crisis.
- How each human activity produces a biological consequence for a species or ecosystem.
- Why conservation needs in situ, ex situ and stored genetic material instead of one universal method.

**Pause at:** the first list of conservation approaches. Cover the labels and sort each example into in situ, ex situ, rewilding, reclamation or germ-plasm storage. Then explain one limitation of each category aloud.

## If that didn’t land

**Amoeba Sisters — biodiversity and conservation**

Use this if the three levels of biodiversity or the reasons biodiversity matters still blur together. It isn’t IB-specific, so return to the checklist afterward and add the required case studies and EDGE programme.

**Khan Academy — biodiversity and human impacts**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@khanacademy> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Khan+Academy+biodiversity+human+impacts+conservation>

Use this if you can name human activities but can’t explain the causal links from an activity to habitat, population and extinction risk.

## Worth it once, not for revision

The EDGE system makes more sense when you meet several real species and ask why their evolutionary history changes their priority.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=EDGE+of+Existence+evolutionarily+distinct+globally+endangered>

After watching, write two separate tests for an EDGE priority: “How evolutionarily distinct is it?” and “How globally endangered is it?” If your explanation uses endangered status alone, you’ve left out half the rationale.
