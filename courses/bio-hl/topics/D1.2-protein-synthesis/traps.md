# D1.2 Protein synthesis — where the marks go

## Copying the wrong DNA relationship

**Commonly written:** “The mRNA has the same sequence as the DNA template strand, with uracil replacing thymine.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The RNA is complementary to the template strand. It matches the non-template strand apart from U replacing T.

**Scores instead:** “RNA polymerase uses complementary base pairing to synthesize RNA that is complementary to the DNA template strand.”

## Saying transcription consumes the DNA template

**Commonly written:** “RNA polymerase removes DNA bases and uses them to build the mRNA.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** RNA is assembled from free nucleotides. The DNA bases and covalent backbones remain in the original molecule.

**Scores instead:** “RNA polymerase reads the exposed DNA bases and joins free complementary RNA nucleotides without changing the DNA sequence.”

## Mixing up codons and anticodons

**Commonly written:** “A tRNA codon pairs with an anticodon on the mRNA.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Codons occur on mRNA, while complementary anticodons occur on tRNA and bind antiparallel to the codons.

**Scores instead:** “A tRNA anticodon pairs antiparallel with a complementary mRNA codon, positioning the tRNA's amino acid.”

## Defining degeneracy as ambiguity

**Commonly written:** “The code is degenerate because one codon can produce several different amino acids.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Degeneracy means several codons can specify one amino acid. A particular codon has only one meaning in the code.

**Scores instead:** “The genetic code is degenerate because most amino acids are specified by more than one codon.”

## Treating every point mutation as harmful

**Commonly written:** “Changing one DNA base always changes an amino acid and damages the protein.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Degeneracy can leave the amino acid unchanged, and the mutation's location and resulting codon determine its effect.

**Scores instead:** “A point mutation may be silent, or it may change an amino acid or introduce a stop codon and alter protein function.”

## Calling the promoter a start codon

**Commonly written:** “RNA polymerase begins translation when it reaches the AUG promoter.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A promoter is a DNA region for transcription initiation. AUG is an mRNA codon used to initiate translation.

**Scores instead:** “Transcription factors and RNA polymerase assemble at a DNA promoter, while a ribosome recognizes AUG on mRNA.”

## Removing introns from DNA

**Commonly written:** “During RNA processing, introns are cut out of the gene and the DNA exons are joined.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Splicing modifies the pre-mRNA transcript. The cell's DNA sequence remains unchanged during RNA processing.

**Scores instead:** “Introns are removed from pre-mRNA and its exons are spliced together to form mature mRNA.”

## Making proteasomes build proteins

**Commonly written:** “Proteasomes join recycled amino acids together to make replacement proteins.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Proteasomes break down damaged or unneeded proteins. Ribosomes use amino acids to synthesize new polypeptides.

**Scores instead:** “Proteasomes degrade proteins so their amino acids can be recycled, while ribosomes assemble new polypeptides.”
