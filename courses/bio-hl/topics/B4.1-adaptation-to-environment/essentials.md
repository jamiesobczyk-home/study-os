## Links to other topics

- **A4.1 Evolution and speciation** — convergent evolution explains why distantly related organisms exposed to similar selection pressures can evolve similar features.
- **B4.2 Ecological niches** — a habitat says where a species lives; a niche includes how it obtains resources and interacts with biotic and abiotic parts of the ecosystem.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — B4.1 identifies adaptations and environmental pressures, while D4.1 explains how adaptive alleles become more frequent across generations.
- **C4.1 Populations and communities** — transects and quadrats connect abiotic conditions to the abundance and distribution of populations.
- **D4.3 Climate change** — changing temperature and rainfall can move conditions beyond species’ tolerance limits and shift biome distributions.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habitat | The place where an organism, population, species or community lives | Its role in the ecosystem; that’s its ecological niche |
| Environment | All the biotic and abiotic surroundings that affect an organism | A synonym for habitat alone |
| Abiotic variable | A measurable non-living feature of the environment, such as temperature, light intensity, pH or salinity | A living influence such as predation or competition |
| Adaptation | An inherited characteristic that increases survival or reproductive success in a particular environment | A change an individual produces because it needs one |
| Distribution | The geographical area or range of locations in which a species occurs | The number of individuals present; that’s abundance |
| Limiting factor | A factor whose value restricts survival, growth, reproduction or distribution | Always the factor present in the smallest quantity |
| Range of tolerance | The range of values of an environmental variable within which a species can survive | A claim that performance is equally high throughout the range |
| Optimum | The value or narrow range at which performance or abundance is highest | Either limit of tolerance |
| Zone of physiological stress | Conditions within the tolerance limits but away from the optimum, where fewer organisms survive or reproduce | Conditions beyond the limits, where the species can’t persist |
| Transect | A line or belt along which organisms and environmental variables are sampled | Random sampling across the whole study area |
| Correlation | An association between two measured variables | Proof that one variable caused the other to change |
| Biome | A group of ecosystems with similar abiotic conditions and communities with similar adaptations | One ecosystem in one geographical location |
| Convergent evolution | Independent evolution of similar features in distantly related groups exposed to similar selection pressures | Similarity inherited from a recent common ancestor |
| Coral reef | A marine ecosystem whose physical reef structure is built mainly from calcium carbonate skeletons of hard corals | Any rocky area containing marine organisms |
