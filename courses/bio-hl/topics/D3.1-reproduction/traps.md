# D3.1 Reproduction — where the marks go

## Saying sexual reproduction always needs two parents

**Commonly written:** “Sexual reproduction requires two separate parents.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Sexual reproduction is defined by meiosis and fusion of gametes. A hermaphroditic organism can produce both gamete types.

**Scores instead:** “Sexual reproduction involves fusion of haploid gametes, which may come from separate organisms or from one hermaphroditic organism.”

## Treating ovulation and menstruation as the same event

**Commonly written:** “The LH surge causes menstruation and releases the egg.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The LH surge causes ovulation. Menstruation occurs later if ovarian hormones fall after degeneration of the corpus luteum.

**Scores instead:** “The LH surge causes ovulation, while a later fall in progesterone and oestradiol causes menstruation.”

## Saying progesterone causes ovulation

**Commonly written:** “Progesterone rises and triggers the egg to leave the ovary.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Ovulation is triggered by the LH surge. Progesterone rises afterward because the corpus luteum secretes it.

**Scores instead:** “Sustained high oestradiol causes an LH surge, which triggers ovulation; the resulting corpus luteum then secretes progesterone.”

## Calling pollination fertilization

**Commonly written:** “Pollination happens when the pollen nucleus fuses with the egg.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Pollination is pollen transfer to a stigma. Fusion of gametes occurs later inside an ovule and is fertilization.

**Scores instead:** “Pollination transfers pollen to the stigma, after which a pollen tube carries a male gamete to an ovule for fertilization.”

## Confusing seed dispersal with pollination

**Commonly written:** “Bees disperse seeds by carrying pollen from one flower to another.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Bees transferring pollen perform pollination. Seed dispersal moves a seed after fertilization and seed formation.

**Scores instead:** “An insect can transfer pollen between flowers, while a seed is dispersed away from the parent after it has formed.”

## Saying meiosis produces one egg because only one haploid cell forms

**Commonly written:** “Oogenesis produces one egg because meiosis makes only one haploid cell.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Meiosis produces several haploid products, but unequal cytokinesis concentrates most cytoplasm in one functional egg while polar bodies form.

**Scores instead:** “Oogenesis produces one large functional egg and polar bodies because cytokinesis during meiosis is unequal.”

## Saying the acrosome reaction prevents polyspermy

**Commonly written:** “The acrosome reaction hardens the egg so no other sperm can enter.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The acrosome reaction helps one sperm penetrate the zona pellucida. The cortical reaction modifies the zona and blocks additional sperm.

**Scores instead:** “Acrosomal enzymes help the first sperm penetrate, while the cortical reaction changes the zona pellucida and prevents polyspermy.”

## Treating an observational correlation as proof about HRT

**Commonly written:** “HRT prevented coronary heart disease because users had fewer cases.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** HRT users differed in socioeconomic status, which was linked to disease risk. The observational correlation couldn't isolate HRT as the cause.

**Scores instead:** “Socioeconomic status confounded the observational association, while randomized trials found a small increase in coronary heart disease risk with HRT.”
