# B3.3 Muscle and motility — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain the sliding filament model of skeletal muscle contraction. **[6]**

Describe the events from exposure of the binding sites on actin to repeated shortening of the sarcomere.

### Mark scheme

- Calcium ions bind to troponin, causing a conformational change. **[1]**
- Tropomyosin moves away from / exposes myosin-binding sites on actin. **[1]**
- Myosin heads attach to actin and form cross-bridges. **[1]**
- Myosin heads perform a power stroke, pulling actin toward the centre of the sarcomere. **[1]**
- ATP binding causes myosin to detach from actin. **[1]**
- ATP hydrolysis re-energizes / repositions the myosin head, allowing the cycle to repeat. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer that says only “actin slides over myosin using ATP” misses the regulated sequence. Full marks require calcium, cross-bridge formation, the power stroke and both roles of ATP._

## Q2. Explain how titin and antagonistic intercostal muscles contribute to movement of the ribcage. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Internal and external intercostal muscle fibres have different orientations / produce opposite movements of the ribcage. **[1]**
- External intercostal contraction moves the ribs up and out during inspiration. **[1]**
- Internal intercostal contraction moves the ribs down and in during forced expiration. **[1]**
- Contraction of one layer stretches the relaxing antagonistic layer / muscles exert force by contraction and cannot push. **[1]**
- Titin stores elastic potential energy when stretched and aids recoil / prevents overstretching of sarcomeres. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming the two muscle layers doesn’t show antagonism. The answer must connect their different fibre orientations to opposite rib movements and explain what titin does when the other layer is stretched._

## Q3. Outline how motor units allow control of the force produced by a skeletal muscle. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- A motor unit consists of one motor neuron and all the skeletal muscle fibres it innervates. **[1]**
- Branches of the motor neuron form neuromuscular junctions with the muscle fibres. **[1]**
- Fibres in the motor unit are stimulated to contract when the motor neuron fires. **[1]**
- Recruitment of more / larger motor units increases force, whereas small motor units allow finer control. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “more neurons make a stronger contraction” is too loose. The scoring relationship is that recruiting additional motor units activates additional muscle fibres._

## Q4. Explain how structures at the human hip joint permit movement while reducing damage or dislocation. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- The rounded head of the femur articulates with a socket in the pelvis. **[1]**
- Bones act as rigid levers around the joint. **[1]**
- Cartilage covers articulating surfaces and reduces friction / absorbs compression. **[1]**
- Synovial fluid lubricates the joint. **[1]**
- Ligaments connect bone to bone and stabilize the joint / limit excessive movement. **[1]**
- Tendons connect muscle to bone and transmit force from contracting muscles. **[1]**

_Examiner note: tendon and ligament aren’t interchangeable. A response loses both attachment marks if it says that ligaments join muscles to bones._

## Q5. Compare and contrast the roles of exoskeletons and endoskeletons in movement. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Both provide rigid anchorage / attachment points for muscles. **[1]**
- Both form lever systems in which skeletal parts rotate around joints. **[1]**
- Arthropod muscles attach to the internal surface of an external exoskeleton. **[1]**
- Vertebrate muscles pull through tendons on bones of an internal endoskeleton. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “one is outside and one is inside” earns only the structural contrast. Full marks require the shared mechanical roles of muscle anchorage and leverage._

## Q6. Explain adaptations of whales for swimming and periodic breathing. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- A streamlined body reduces drag / resistance during swimming. **[1]**
- Forelimbs modified as flippers provide steering / stability. **[1]**
- Horizontal tail flukes move up and down to generate propulsion. **[1]**
- Dorsally positioned blowholes allow breathing at the surface and can be closed to prevent water entry during dives. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “fins and a tail help it swim” doesn’t identify the mammalian pattern. The answer must distinguish flippers used for control from horizontal flukes used for propulsion._
