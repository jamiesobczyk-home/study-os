# B1.1 Carbohydrates and lipids — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain how condensation and hydrolysis reactions build and break polysaccharides. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Condensation joins monosaccharides by forming covalent / glycosidic bonds. **[1]**
- Water is released during each condensation reaction. **[1]**
- Hydrolysis breaks glycosidic bonds by adding water. **[1]**
- Water supplies H and OH to the products / hydrolysis releases smaller sugars or monosaccharides. **[1]**

_Examiner note: describing condensation as “removing water” misses the bond formation, while saying hydrolysis “dissolves the polymer” misses the chemical breaking of a glycosidic bond._

## Q2. Compare and contrast starch, glycogen and cellulose. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

Award any six:

- All are polysaccharides composed of glucose monomers. **[1]**
- Starch and glycogen contain alpha-glucose, whereas cellulose contains beta-glucose. **[1]**
- Starch is an energy store in plants, whereas glycogen is an energy store in animals. **[1]**
- Amylose is unbranched / coiled and amylopectin is branched. **[1]**
- Glycogen is more highly branched than amylopectin / has more frequent alpha-1,6 branch points. **[1]**
- Cellulose has straight, unbranched chains with alternate beta-glucose monomers rotated by 180°. **[1]**
- Cellulose chains form hydrogen bonds with neighboring chains / group into strong microfibrils. **[1]**
- Cellulose has a structural role in plant cell walls, whereas starch and glycogen have energy-storage roles. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “all three are made of glucose” earns one mark. Full marks require differences in glucose isomer, branching or chain shape, and biological function._

## Q3. Explain how the structure of cellulose makes it suitable for plant cell walls. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Cellulose consists of beta-glucose monomers joined by beta-1,4 glycosidic bonds. **[1]**
- Alternate glucose monomers are rotated by 180°, producing straight / unbranched chains. **[1]**
- Hydrogen bonds form between parallel cellulose chains / chains group into microfibrils. **[1]**
- Microfibrils have high tensile strength / resist stretching and help prevent bursting under turgor pressure. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the hydrogen bonds are between neighboring chains, not the glycosidic bonds running along a chain. Full marks also require a consequence for the wall._

## Q4. Explain how fatty-acid saturation affects the physical properties of fats and oils. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Saturated fatty acids have no C=C bonds, monounsaturated fatty acids have one, and polyunsaturated fatty acids have two or more. **[1]**
- A cis C=C bond causes a bend / kink in the hydrocarbon chain. **[1]**
- Kinked unsaturated chains pack less closely than saturated chains. **[1]**
- Reduced packing weakens intermolecular attractions / lowers the melting point. **[1]**
- Accept: lipids rich in cis-unsaturated fatty acids tend to be liquid oils, whereas those rich in saturated fatty acids tend to be solid fats at room temperature. **[1]**

_Examiner note: counting double bonds distinguishes the three categories, but it doesn't explain melting point. The missing chain is double bond, kink, reduced packing, lower melting point._

## Q5. Explain why phospholipids form bilayers in water and why testosterone can pass through the bilayer. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Phospholipids are amphipathic / contain hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions. **[1]**
- Hydrophilic phosphate-containing heads face the aqueous solutions. **[1]**
- Hydrophobic fatty-acid tails face inward / associate away from water. **[1]**
- Testosterone is a non-polar steroid / contains four fused carbon rings and is largely non-polar. **[1]**
- Testosterone dissolves in the hydrophobic interior and diffuses through the bilayer. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “testosterone is small” isn't the requested explanation. Its non-polar character allows it to enter the hydrophobic core._

## Q6. Explain the advantages of storing both glycogen and triglycerides in an animal. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Glycogen is branched and has many ends where glucose can be released by hydrolysis. **[1]**
- Glycogen provides a readily mobilized source of glucose / substrate for respiration. **[1]**
- Triglycerides yield about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates. **[1]**
- Triglycerides are insoluble and can be stored without a large effect on osmotic concentration. **[1]**
- Triglycerides provide a compact long-term energy store / adipose tissue also provides thermal insulation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: stating that both molecules store energy earns little because the question asks why both are useful. The answer must distinguish rapid glucose mobilization from energy-dense long-term storage._
