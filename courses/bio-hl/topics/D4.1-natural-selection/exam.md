# D4.1 Natural selection — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. Familiar wording after the reveal isn't evidence that you could produce it for marks.

## Q1. Explain evolution by natural selection. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Variation exists between individuals within a population. **[1]**
- Some variation is heritable / has a genetic basis. **[1]**
- More offspring are produced than can survive, resulting in competition for limited resources. **[1]**
- Individuals with advantageous heritable traits survive and reproduce more successfully. **[1]**
- Alleles associated with the advantageous traits increase in frequency over generations / the population evolves. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer ending with "the fittest survive" misses differential reproduction, inheritance and the change in allele frequency._

## Q2. Explain why acquired characteristics don't usually cause evolutionary change by natural selection. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Acquired characteristics develop during an individual's lifetime due to environmental effects / use or disuse. **[1]**
- Such changes are not encoded as changes in the base sequence of genes. **[1]**
- They are therefore not transmitted genetically to offspring. **[1]**
- Natural selection changes a population only when differences affecting reproductive success are heritable. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that an acquired trait "isn't useful" misses the issue. It may be useful to the individual, but it doesn't alter evolution unless its basis can be inherited._

## Q3. Explain the results expected from John Endler's guppy experiments when male guppies are kept under strong predation or without predators. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Male guppies vary in conspicuous coloration / number, size or brightness of colored spots. **[1]**
- Under strong predation, conspicuous males are detected more readily and less conspicuous males leave more offspring. **[1]**
- Without predators, female mate choice favors more conspicuous males / conspicuous males have greater mating success. **[1]**
- Controlled predator treatments show that natural and sexual selection can act in opposing directions on the same heritable trait. **[1]**

_Examiner note: describing brighter and duller fish earns little unless the answer connects each treatment to a different source of differential reproductive success._

## Q4. Compare and contrast directional, stabilizing and disruptive selection. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Directional selection favors individuals at one extreme of a phenotypic range. **[1]**
- Directional selection shifts the mean / distribution toward the favored extreme. **[1]**
- Stabilizing selection favors intermediate phenotypes and selects against both extremes. **[1]**
- Stabilizing selection reduces variation / narrows the distribution around the mean. **[1]**
- Disruptive selection favors both extreme phenotypes and selects against intermediates. **[1]**
- Disruptive selection tends to produce a bimodal distribution / two peaks. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the names alone don't compare the processes. Full marks require both which phenotypes are favored and what happens to each distribution._

## Q5. Calculate allele and genotype frequencies in a population at Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium if 9% show a recessive phenotype, then calculate the expected numbers of AA and Aa individuals in a population of 1,000. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Frequency of aa identified as q² = 0.09. **[1]**
- q = √0.09 = 0.30. **[1]**
- p = 1 - q = 0.70. **[1]**
- p² = 0.49, giving 490 AA individuals. **[1]**
- 2pq = 0.42, giving 420 Aa individuals. **[1]**

_Examiner note: treating 0.09 as q makes every later value fail. The recessive phenotype identifies the homozygous genotype frequency, q²._

## Q6. Distinguish artificial selection from the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Artificial selection involves deliberate human choice of individuals with desired heritable traits for breeding. **[1]**
- Repeated selective breeding increases the frequency of alleles associated with the desired traits. **[1]**
- Antibiotics kill susceptible bacteria while resistant bacteria survive and reproduce / transfer resistance alleles. **[1]**
- Antibiotic resistance is natural selection because humans do not deliberately choose resistant bacteria as parents, despite creating the selection pressure. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "both involve humans" doesn't answer the distinction. The deciding issue is whether humans choose the breeding individuals or create an environment in which differential survival and reproduction occur._
