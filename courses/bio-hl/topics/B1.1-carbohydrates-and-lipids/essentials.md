# B1.1 Carbohydrates and lipids — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how carbon’s four covalent bonds allow it to form stable branched chains, unbranched chains and rings.
- [ ] Distinguish monomers from polymers and explain how condensation and hydrolysis reactions build and break biological molecules.
- [ ] Recognize pentose and hexose monosaccharides from ring diagrams and relate glucose’s structure to solubility, transport, stability and energy release.
- [ ] Compare starch and glycogen as energy stores, linking coiling, branching, insolubility and accessible chain ends to their functions.
- [ ] Explain how alternating beta-glucose monomers, straight chains and hydrogen-bonded microfibrils make cellulose suitable for plant cell walls.
- [ ] Explain how cell-surface glycoproteins allow cell–cell recognition, using ABO blood-group antigens as an example.
- [ ] Explain why hydrocarbon-rich lipids are hydrophobic and recognize fats, oils, waxes and steroids as lipid groups.
- [ ] Describe how condensation reactions form triglycerides and phospholipids from glycerol, fatty acids and phosphate.
- [ ] Distinguish saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids from the number of C=C bonds.
- [ ] Explain how cis double bonds affect fatty-acid shape, packing and melting point, then connect this to fats and oils.
- [ ] Explain why triglycerides work as long-term energy stores and why adipose tissue provides thermal insulation.
- [ ] Explain why amphipathic phospholipids form bilayers in water, with the heads and tails in the correct positions.
- [ ] Recognize the four fused carbon rings of a steroid and explain why non-polar steroid hormones can cross a phospholipid bilayer.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.1 Water** — polarity explains why sugars dissolve, lipids remain poorly soluble and phospholipids organize into bilayers.
- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — condensation and hydrolysis also build and break nucleic acids, so the reaction pattern learned here transfers directly.
- **B1.2 Proteins** — amino acids form polypeptides by condensation, while carbohydrate chains attached to proteins produce glycoproteins.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — B1.1 explains why a bilayer forms and why non-polar steroids can diffuse through its hydrophobic interior.
- **C1.2 Cell respiration** — glucose and fatty acids store chemical energy; respiration explains how oxidation transfers that energy to ATP.
- **C3.2 Defense against disease** — ABO antigens show how specific cell-surface molecules can be recognized as self or foreign.
- **D2.2 Gene expression** — steroid hormones can enter cells and affect transcription after binding intracellular receptors.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monomer | A small molecular subunit that can be linked to other subunits | Any small molecule; water isn’t a carbohydrate monomer |
| Polymer | A large molecule made from many repeating monomer units | Every large biological molecule; triglycerides aren’t polymers |
| Condensation | A reaction that joins molecules by forming a covalent bond and releases water | Hydrolysis or the removal of an entire monomer |
| Hydrolysis | A reaction that breaks a covalent bond by adding water | Dissolving a molecule in water |
| Monosaccharide | A single sugar unit, such as glucose or ribose | A polysaccharide with one branch |
| Glycosidic bond | A covalent bond joining monosaccharides | A hydrogen bond between cellulose chains |
| Alpha-glucose | A glucose isomer with the carbon-1 hydroxyl group below the ring in the standard Haworth diagram | Beta-glucose or a different molecular formula |
| Beta-glucose | A glucose isomer with the carbon-1 hydroxyl group above the ring in the standard Haworth diagram | Alpha-glucose rotated on the page |
| Hydrophobic | Non-polar and unable to form favorable interactions with water, making it poorly soluble | Repelled by water through a separate pushing force |
| Amphipathic | Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions | Entirely polar or entirely non-polar |
| Triglyceride | Glycerol joined to three fatty acids | A polymer or a phospholipid with three tails |
| Saturated fatty acid | A fatty acid with no carbon–carbon double bonds in its hydrocarbon chain | A fatty acid containing no oxygen |
| Unsaturated fatty acid | A fatty acid with one or more carbon–carbon double bonds | A fatty acid that lacks hydrogen completely |
| Adipose tissue | Animal tissue specialized for storing triglycerides, cushioning organs and reducing heat transfer | A carbohydrate store comparable to plant starch |
| Glycoprotein | A protein with one or more attached carbohydrate chains | A carbohydrate trapped inside a protein |
| Steroid | A lipid whose carbon skeleton contains four fused rings | Every hormone or every molecule containing a ring |
