# C2.2 Neural signalling — where the marks go

## Saying the sodium-potassium pump makes the action-potential spike

**Commonly written:** "The sodium-potassium pump rapidly brings sodium in and potassium out during an action potential."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The pump moves Na+ out and K+ in while maintaining gradients. Voltage-gated channels produce the rapid spike and fall.

**Scores instead:** "Voltage-gated Na+ channels cause depolarization, voltage-gated K+ channels cause repolarization, and the sodium-potassium pump maintains the ion gradients."

## Treating a resting neuron as inactive

**Commonly written:** "At resting potential, ions stop moving because the neuron isn’t firing."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Pumps and leak channels remain active at rest, and their unequal ion movements maintain the negative membrane potential.

**Scores instead:** "At rest, the sodium-potassium pump maintains ion gradients while selective permeability causes a negative membrane potential."

## Calling every depolarization an action potential

**Commonly written:** "Acetylcholine binds, so the postsynaptic neuron always produces an action potential."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Acetylcholine first produces a graded EPSP. An action potential occurs only if summed inputs bring the membrane to threshold.

**Scores instead:** "Acetylcholine produces an EPSP, and an action potential begins only if summation brings the postsynaptic membrane to threshold."

## Making calcium cross the synaptic cleft

**Commonly written:** "Calcium leaves the first neuron and carries the signal across the synapse."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Ca2+ enters the presynaptic terminal and triggers vesicle fusion. Neurotransmitter, rather than calcium, diffuses across the cleft.

**Scores instead:** "Ca2+ enters the presynaptic terminal and triggers exocytosis, after which neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic cleft."

## Saying the action potential travels as one object

**Commonly written:** "The same action potential moves unchanged from one end of the axon to the other."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Local current brings each adjacent region to threshold, so the action potential is regenerated along the axon membrane.

**Scores instead:** "Na+ entry creates local current that depolarizes the adjacent membrane to threshold, regenerating the action potential there."

## Saying myelin contains the impulse

**Commonly written:** "The action potential travels through the myelin and jumps over the nodes."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Current spreads along the axon beneath myelin, while action potentials are regenerated at the nodes of Ranvier.

**Scores instead:** "Myelin insulates the axon, so local current spreads between nodes and action potentials are regenerated at successive nodes of Ranvier."

## Treating inhibition as destruction of a signal

**Commonly written:** "An inhibitory neurotransmitter removes the excitatory impulse from the postsynaptic neuron."

**Why it doesn’t score:** An inhibitory neurotransmitter produces a graded hyperpolarization that makes threshold less likely to be reached.

**Scores instead:** "An inhibitory neurotransmitter generates an IPSP, moving the postsynaptic membrane potential farther from threshold."

## Saying pain is detected in the brain

**Commonly written:** "The brain detects capsaicin in the skin and sends a pain impulse back."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Free sensory nerve endings detect the stimulus in the skin. Impulses travel to the brain, where pain is perceived.

**Scores instead:** "Capsaicin activates ion channels in a free nerve ending, and the resulting impulses travel to the brain, where pain is perceived."
