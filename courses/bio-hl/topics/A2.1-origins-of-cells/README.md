# A2.1 Origins of cells

- **Theme:** A — Unity and diversity
- **Level:** Cells
- **Level of study:** HL only
- **Status:** ready

## The one-sentence version

A2.1 uses experiments, self-assembling membranes, catalytic RNA, fossils and shared genes to build a testable but incomplete explanation of how non-living chemistry led to cells and LUCA.

## Why this topic is worth getting right

This topic is hard because every claim has a different strength. Miller–Urey shows that building blocks can form abiotically, RNA and vesicles make proposed intermediate stages plausible, and shared genes support common ancestry, but none of them recreates the whole origin of life. Study it as a chain of claim, evidence and limitation. That keeps “supports” from turning into “proves” and protects marks on evaluate and explain questions.

## In your study guide

Allott, _Biology Study Guide_ (2023), **section A2.1, pp. 56–58**. Read it *after*
the capture sheet, never before. It's condensed enough to make a fast check of
what you left out, and a slow way to meet the topic for the first time.
