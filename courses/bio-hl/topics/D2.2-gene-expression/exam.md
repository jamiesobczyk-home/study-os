# D2.2 Gene expression — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain how information in a gene can affect phenotype. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- The DNA base sequence of a gene is transcribed into mRNA. **[1]**
- The mRNA is translated at a ribosome into a polypeptide / determines the amino acid sequence of a polypeptide. **[1]**
- The polypeptide folds / is modified to form a functional protein. **[1]**
- The protein’s function alters cell structure or activity and therefore contributes to phenotype. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer that ends at “a protein is made” misses the final link. Full marks require the protein’s function to be connected to phenotype._

## Q2. Explain how transcription factors, promoters and enhancers regulate gene expression. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- A promoter is a DNA sequence at which transcription machinery assembles / transcription begins. **[1]**
- Transcription factors are proteins that bind to specific DNA base sequences. **[1]**
- An activator can bind to an enhancer. **[1]**
- Enhancer-bound activators increase assembly or activity of transcription machinery at the promoter / increase transcription. **[1]**
- Repressors reduce or block transcription, so the proteins present in a cell affect which genes are expressed. **[1]**

_Examiner note: promoters and enhancers must be identified as DNA sequences, while transcription factors must be identified as proteins. Treating all three as proteins loses the distinction._

## Q3. Distinguish the genome, transcriptome and proteome of a differentiated cell. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- The genome is the complete genetic information / all DNA of the cell. **[1]**
- The genome is generally the same in different nucleated cells of one organism. **[1]**
- The transcriptome is all RNA transcripts present in the cell at a particular time. **[1]**
- The proteome is all proteins present or produced in the cell at a particular time. **[1]**
- The transcriptome and proteome vary between cell types / with time or environmental conditions because different genes are expressed. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “genome is DNA, transcriptome is RNA, proteome is protein” earns only the definitions. Full marks require the contrast in how they vary between differentiated cells._

## Q4. Explain how epigenetic regulation can produce and preserve cell differentiation. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Different genes are activated or repressed in different cells. **[1]**
- Different patterns of transcription produce different transcriptomes / patterns of translation produce different proteomes. **[1]**
- Different proteins produce specialized cell structures or functions / differentiated phenotypes. **[1]**
- Epigenetic tags on DNA or histones affect transcription without altering the DNA nucleotide sequence. **[1]**
- Epigenetic patterns can be retained or copied through mitosis, preserving gene-expression patterns in daughter cells. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that differentiated cells “have different genes” contradicts the mechanism. They generally share a genome but express different genes._

## Q5. Suggest how monozygotic twins could be used to investigate environmental effects on gene expression. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Monozygotic twins have nearly identical genomes / genetic variation is largely controlled. **[1]**
- Compare twins exposed to different environments / raised apart / with different exposure to a named environmental factor. **[1]**
- Compare gene expression, transcriptomes or epigenetic tags in equivalent tissues. **[1]**
- Differences associated with exposure provide evidence of an environmental effect, but correlation doesn’t by itself establish causation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “identical twins have the same DNA” explains their value but doesn’t describe a study. Full marks require a differing exposure, a measured outcome and a limit on the conclusion._

## Q6. Compare and contrast regulation of gene expression by lactose in E. coli and by estrogen in a human target cell. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both are external chemical signals that alter transcription / gene expression. **[1]**
- Both act through binding to a regulatory protein and changing its activity. **[1]**
- In the absence of lactose, a repressor binds the operator and blocks transcription of lac genes. **[1]**
- Lactose / a lactose-derived inducer binds the repressor, causing it to release from the operator and allowing transcription. **[1]**
- Estrogen crosses the plasma membrane and binds an intracellular receptor. **[1]**
- The estrogen–receptor complex binds a specific regulatory DNA sequence and acts as a transcription factor to alter transcription. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the shared idea is regulation through a protein, but the directions differ. Lactose removes repression in a bacterium, while estrogen forms a receptor complex that regulates transcription in a target cell._
