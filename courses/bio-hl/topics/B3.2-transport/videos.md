# B3.2 Transport — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and reconstruct the mechanisms from memory. A second explanation can feel productive while strengthening recognition, which is the skill that has been hiding the gaps.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — B3.2 Transport**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+B3.2+Transport+IB+Biology>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation made for B3.2.

**Watch for:**

- Which pressure difference drives each movement: blood flow, capillary filtration, transpiration, root pressure and phloem translocation.
- How each vessel's wall and lumen fit the pressure it experiences.
- Which transport processes require ATP and which are passive.
- How the SL plant and blood-vessel material changes when the HL mechanisms are added.

**Pause at:** the first comparison of an artery, vein and capillary. Cover the labels and identify each vessel from its wall, lumen and valves. Then give one function-linked reason for every identification.

## Worth it once, not for revision

The cardiac cycle earns an **animation** because valve movements depend on changing pressure as the chambers contract and relax. Seeing the pump change shape can make the sequence easier to reconstruct.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=cardiac+cycle+valves+pressure+animation>

Pause once during ventricular systole. State which chambers are contracting, which valves are open, which valves are closed and where the blood is moving. If one answer depends on remembering a picture rather than comparing pressures, replay that transition.
