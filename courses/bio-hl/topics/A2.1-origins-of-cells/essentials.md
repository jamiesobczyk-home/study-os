## Core

- [ ] Explain how the lack of free oxygen and ozone, stronger ultraviolet radiation, higher temperatures, carbon dioxide and methane affected prebiotic chemistry on early Earth.
- [ ] Explain why cells are considered the smallest units of self-sustaining life and why viruses aren’t classified as living.
- [ ] Explain why the origin of the first cells is difficult to investigate using experiments or fossils.
- [ ] Explain why catalysis, molecular self-replication, self-assembly and compartmentalization were all needed before cells could evolve.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — RNA’s nucleotide sequence can store information, while folding gives some RNA catalytic activity. Those two roles explain why RNA is central to hypotheses about the first cells.
- **A2.3 Viruses** — deciding whether viruses are living tests the meaning of cellular organization, metabolism and autonomous reproduction.
- **A3.2 Classification and cladistics** — conserved sequences and molecular clocks use comparisons between organisms to reconstruct common ancestry and estimate divergence times.
- **B1.1 Carbohydrates and lipids** — the amphipathic structure of fatty acids explains their self-assembly into membrane-like vesicles.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — protocell compartments are the evolutionary starting point for understanding how modern membranes control internal conditions.
- **B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization** — the advantage of separating chemical environments begins with protocells and continues in the organelles of eukaryotic cells.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — self-replication with heritable variation allowed chemical systems and early cells to evolve rather than remaining unchanged.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abiogenesis | The origin of living systems from non-living matter through chemical processes | Spontaneous appearance of a complete modern cell |
| Prebiotic | Existing or occurring before life arose | A synonym for primitive or poorly adapted |
| Carbon compound | A compound containing carbon; many compounds needed by cells are carbon based | Evidence by itself that a living organism made the compound |
| Self-sustaining life | A system able to carry out the processes needed to maintain and reproduce itself at the cellular level | Complete independence from matter or energy in the environment |
| Catalysis | Increasing the rate of a chemical reaction without the catalyst being consumed | Supplying all the energy or reactants for the reaction |
| Self-assembly | Organization caused by the properties and interactions of the components | Assembly directed by genes, enzymes or conscious action |
| Molecular self-replication | Production of copies that preserve information and allow inheritance | Ordinary growth by adding material without copying information |
| Compartmentalization | Enclosure of reactions and molecules within a boundary such as a membrane | Random separation with no maintained internal conditions |
| Vesicle | A closed, membrane-bound compartment containing an aqueous interior | A living cell merely because it has a membrane |
| Protocell | A non-living or early cell-like compartment proposed as an intermediate in cell origins | A fully developed modern prokaryotic cell |
| Ribozyme | An RNA molecule with catalytic activity | A protein enzyme attached to RNA |
| RNA world hypothesis | The hypothesis that RNA once had major roles in both heredity and catalysis before DNA and protein dominated those roles | A claim that scientists have recreated the complete origin of life |
| LUCA | The most recent common ancestral population of all organisms alive today | The first organism ever to exist or the ancestor of every early lineage |
| Biosignature | A structure, substance or isotope pattern that may have been produced by life | Conclusive proof when non-biological processes could produce a similar pattern |
| Molecular clock | A method that estimates divergence time from sequence differences and an estimated rate of change | A clock with a perfectly constant rate across every gene and lineage |
| Conserved sequence | A DNA, RNA or protein sequence that has changed little during evolution | A sequence found only in one recently evolved species |
| Reducing atmosphere | An atmosphere with little or no free oxygen and chemicals able to donate electrons in reactions | An atmosphere proven to match every condition on early Earth |
