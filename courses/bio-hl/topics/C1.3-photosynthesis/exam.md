# C1.3 Photosynthesis — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain the relationship between the absorption spectrum of photosynthetic pigments and the action spectrum of photosynthesis. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- An absorption spectrum shows absorption of different wavelengths by a pigment / pigments. **[1]**
- An action spectrum shows the rate of photosynthesis at different wavelengths. **[1]**
- Peaks in photosynthetic activity generally correspond to wavelengths strongly absorbed by photosynthetic pigments. **[1]**
- Differences may occur because the action spectrum represents the combined contribution of multiple pigments / energy can be transferred between pigments. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that both graphs have peaks in red and blue earns limited credit. Full marks require what each axis represents and why absorption can affect photosynthetic rate._

## Q2. Explain how chromatography can separate and identify photosynthetic pigments. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Pigment extract is placed on a baseline above the solvent level / solvent moves through the stationary phase. **[1]**
- Pigments move at different rates because of differences in solubility in the solvent and attraction to the stationary phase. **[1]**
- Rf is calculated as distance traveled by pigment divided by distance traveled by solvent front. **[1]**
- Pigments are identified by comparison of Rf values with reference values obtained under the same conditions. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming the colors of the bands doesn’t explain separation. The response needs differential movement and an Rf ratio measured from a common baseline._

## Q3. Suggest how an investigation could determine whether light intensity or carbon dioxide concentration limits photosynthesis in an aquatic plant. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Measure oxygen production / carbon dioxide uptake per unit time as an estimate of photosynthetic rate. **[1]**
- Vary light intensity while maintaining carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. **[1]**
- Vary carbon dioxide concentration while maintaining light intensity and temperature. **[1]**
- Include repeats and calculate a mean / use multiple values across a suitable range. **[1]**
- The limiting factor is identified when increasing that factor increases rate, whereas increasing a non-limiting factor produces little or no increase. **[1]**

_Examiner note: changing lamp distance without controlling temperature confounds two variables. The conclusion also has to be based on which treatment raises the measured rate._

## Q4. Explain ATP production by chemiosmosis in the thylakoids of a chloroplast. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Light excites electrons in photosystem II / reaction-center chlorophyll emits electrons. **[1]**
- Electrons pass through an electron transport chain in the thylakoid membrane. **[1]**
- Energy released by electron transfer is used to pump protons from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen. **[1]**
- Photolysis of water contributes additional protons to the thylakoid lumen. **[1]**
- Protons move down their electrochemical gradient through ATP synthase into the stroma. **[1]**
- ATP synthase couples proton flow to phosphorylation of ADP with inorganic phosphate. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “electrons pass through ATP synthase” is the key near miss. Electrons move through carriers, while protons move through ATP synthase._

## Q5. Explain how carbon dioxide is converted into triose phosphate and how RuBP is regenerated in the Calvin cycle. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Rubisco catalyzes the combination of carbon dioxide with RuBP. **[1]**
- RuBP is a five-carbon compound / carbon dioxide is a one-carbon compound. **[1]**
- An unstable six-carbon intermediate splits into two molecules of glycerate 3-phosphate. **[1]**
- Glycerate 3-phosphate is converted / reduced to triose phosphate using ATP. **[1]**
- Reduced NADP supplies reducing power / hydrogen and electrons for triose phosphate production. **[1]**
- Most triose phosphate is rearranged using ATP to regenerate RuBP. **[1]**

_Examiner note: rubisco and RuBP can’t be swapped. One is the enzyme, while the other is the five-carbon substrate regenerated by the cycle._

## Q6. Explain the interdependence of the light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Light-dependent reactions produce ATP for use in the Calvin cycle. **[1]**
- Light-dependent reactions produce reduced NADP for use in the Calvin cycle. **[1]**
- The Calvin cycle uses ATP and reduced NADP to reduce carbon compounds / produce triose phosphate and regenerate RuBP. **[1]**
- The Calvin cycle returns ADP and inorganic phosphate for photophosphorylation. **[1]**
- The Calvin cycle returns oxidized NADP to accept further electrons and protons in the light-dependent reactions. **[1]**

_Examiner note: listing the products of each stage isn’t enough. Full marks require movement in both directions between the two sets of reactions._
