# B3.1 Gas exchange — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain the topic without the screen. A second explanation can make the same ideas feel familiar without making them retrievable, and recognition is the problem you’re fixing.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — B3.1 Gas exchange**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+B3.1+Gas+exchange>

Alex Lee organizes his IB Biology material around the 2023 syllabus codes, so start with the version built for B3.1.

**Watch for:**

- Which feature of an exchange surface changes surface area, diffusion distance or the concentration gradient.
- How pressure changes during ventilation, and which muscle movements cause those changes.
- Why stomata create a conflict between carbon dioxide uptake and water conservation.
- How cooperative binding and carbon dioxide change haemoglobin’s oxygen affinity.

**Pause at:** the first diagram comparing inhalation with exhalation. Cover the labels and narrate a complete chain from muscle contraction to air movement. Include thoracic volume, pressure and the atmospheric pressure comparison.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Ventilation is a mechanical process, so an **animation** earns an exception to the one-video rule. A moving diaphragm and rib cage make the volume and pressure changes easier to track than a static diagram does.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=lung+ventilation+diaphragm+intercostal+muscles+animation>

After watching, draw two thoraxes. Label one inhalation and the other forced exhalation, then add muscle action, rib movement, diaphragm shape, volume, pressure and air direction without replaying the animation.
