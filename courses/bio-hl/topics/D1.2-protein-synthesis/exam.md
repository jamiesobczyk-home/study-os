# D1.2 Protein synthesis — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If a point only looked familiar after the reveal, it wasn't recalled and didn't score.

## Q1. Explain transcription and why the DNA template remains unchanged. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- RNA polymerase binds to DNA at / near the gene and separates the two strands. **[1]**
- One DNA strand acts as the template for RNA synthesis. **[1]**
- RNA nucleotides pair with exposed DNA bases by complementary base pairing / A–U and C–G pairing. **[1]**
- RNA polymerase joins RNA nucleotides to form the transcript. **[1]**
- Only hydrogen bonds between DNA strands are temporarily disrupted / the DNA sugar–phosphate backbones and base sequences remain intact / DNA strands re-form after transcription. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an answer that says DNA “makes mRNA” misses the mechanism. Full marks require the template strand, complementary base pairing, polymerase and a reason the DNA sequence is retained._

## Q2. Deduce the amino acid sequence encoded by the mRNA 5′-CCA-AUG-GCU-UUU-UGA-GGC-3′. **[4]**

The relevant codons are AUG = methionine, GCU = alanine, UUU = phenylalanine, UGA = stop, CCA = proline and GGC = glycine.

### Mark scheme

- Translation begins at AUG / the CCA before AUG is not translated in this reading frame. **[1]**
- Methionine is the first amino acid. **[1]**
- Alanine and then phenylalanine are added. **[1]**
- UGA terminates translation / GGC after the stop codon is not translated; sequence is methionine–alanine–phenylalanine. **[1]**

_Examiner note: translating from the first printed triplet gives the wrong sequence. The start codon sets the reading frame, and the stop codon ends it without contributing an amino acid._

## Q3. Explain how tRNA and the ribosome elongate a polypeptide. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Each tRNA carries a specific amino acid and contains an anticodon. **[1]**
- The tRNA anticodon pairs by complementary base pairing with an mRNA codon. **[1]**
- The ribosome holds the tRNAs / mRNA in position and catalyzes peptide-bond formation. **[1]**
- The growing polypeptide is transferred to the amino acid on the incoming tRNA. **[1]**
- The ribosome moves one codon in the 5′ to 3′ direction and the empty tRNA leaves / the cycle repeats. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that tRNA “brings amino acids” earns limited credit. The missing marks are usually codon–anticodon pairing, peptide-bond formation and movement by one codon._

## Q4. Explain how eukaryotic pre-mRNA can be processed to produce mature mRNA and different polypeptides. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Introns are removed from pre-mRNA. **[1]**
- Exons are spliced together. **[1]**
- A 5′ cap is added to the transcript. **[1]**
- A poly-A tail is added to the 3′ end / cap and tail stabilize the transcript. **[1]**
- Alternative splicing joins different combinations of exons into mature mRNAs. **[1]**
- Different mature mRNA codon sequences are translated into different polypeptides from the same gene. **[1]**

_Examiner note: introns and exons must be discussed as parts of the RNA being processed. Saying that introns are removed from the cell's DNA describes a permanent genome change and loses the processing mark._

## Q5. Outline the initiation of transcription and translation, including directionality. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Transcription factors bind to a promoter / help RNA polymerase bind at the promoter. **[1]**
- RNA polymerase reads the DNA template 3′ to 5′ and synthesizes RNA 5′ to 3′. **[1]**
- The small ribosomal subunit attaches at the 5′ end of mRNA and moves to the start codon. **[1]**
- An initiator tRNA pairs with the AUG start codon. **[1]**
- The large ribosomal subunit attaches / another tRNA binds to complete initiation. **[1]**
- The ribosome moves along the mRNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction during translation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: promoter and start codon can't be treated as synonyms. The promoter starts transcription on DNA, while AUG establishes the start and reading frame of translation on mRNA._

## Q6. Explain how protein modification and breakdown maintain a functional proteome, using insulin as an example. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Many newly synthesized polypeptides require modification before becoming functional proteins. **[1]**
- The signal peptide is removed from preproinsulin to form proinsulin. **[1]**
- Proinsulin folds and forms disulfide bonds between the future A and B chains. **[1]**
- The C-peptide is removed, leaving functional insulin with A and B chains linked by disulfide bonds. **[1]**
- Proteasomes break down damaged / misfolded / unneeded proteins, enabling amino acids to be recycled for new protein synthesis. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “insulin is cut” lacks the sequence needed for full marks. Name preproinsulin, proinsulin, the signal peptide, C-peptide and the linked A and B chains._
