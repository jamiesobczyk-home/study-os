# B2.3 Cell specialization — where the marks go

## Saying specialized cells have different DNA

**Commonly written:** "Cells become different because each cell receives different genes."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mitosis usually gives embryonic cells the same genome. Specialization results from different patterns of gene expression.

**Scores instead:** "Morphogen concentrations regulate gene expression, so cells with the same genome produce different proteins and differentiate."

## Defining a stem cell only by division

**Commonly written:** "A stem cell is a cell that keeps dividing."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Division alone doesn't define a stem cell. The answer must include both self-renewal and the capacity to differentiate.

**Scores instead:** "A stem cell can self-renew to maintain the stem-cell population and can differentiate into specialized cell types."

## Treating pluripotent and totipotent as synonyms

**Commonly written:** "Pluripotent cells can form every tissue and develop into a complete organism."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Pluripotent cells can form body-cell types from all three germ layers but can't form every extraembryonic tissue required for a whole organism.

**Scores instead:** "Totipotent cells can produce a whole organism, while pluripotent cells can produce the major body-cell types but not a whole organism."

## Saying a larger cell has less surface area

**Commonly written:** "Large cells can't exchange enough material because they have a smaller surface area."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A larger cell has more total surface area, but its volume rises faster, so it has less surface area per unit volume.

**Scores instead:** "As a cell grows, its surface area-to-volume ratio decreases because volume increases faster than surface area."

## Confusing microvilli with villi

**Commonly written:** "Each kidney tubule cell has villi that increase its surface area."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Microvilli are extensions of one cell's plasma membrane. Villi are larger multicellular projections of a tissue.

**Scores instead:** "Proximal convoluted tubule cells have microvilli that increase membrane area for selective reabsorption."

## Swapping the pneumocyte functions

**Commonly written:** "Type II pneumocytes are thin for diffusion, while type I pneumocytes secrete surfactant."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The functions have been reversed. Type I cells form the thin exchange surface, while type II cells contain lamellar bodies and secrete surfactant.

**Scores instead:** "Thin type I pneumocytes shorten the diffusion path, while type II pneumocytes release surfactant that reduces alveolar surface tension."

## Claiming skeletal muscle fibres are ordinary single cells

**Commonly written:** "A skeletal muscle fibre is a normal cell with one nucleus."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A skeletal muscle fibre forms by fusion of precursor cells, has one continuous plasma membrane and contains many nuclei.

**Scores instead:** "A skeletal muscle fibre functions as one multinucleate unit, but its origin by cell fusion makes it unlike a typical single cell."

## Saying mitochondria push the sperm forward

**Commonly written:** "The mitochondria in a sperm's midpiece move the sperm toward the egg."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mitochondria supply ATP, but bending of the microtubule-based axoneme in the tail produces propulsion.

**Scores instead:** "Midpiece mitochondria supply ATP for axoneme movement, and bending of the tail propels the sperm."
