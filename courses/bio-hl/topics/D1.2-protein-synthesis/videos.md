# D1.2 Protein synthesis — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and produce the process from memory. A second explanation of the same sequence builds recognition, which is already the problem. The animation exception below earns its place because ribosome movement and changing tRNA positions are hard to reconstruct from a still diagram.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — D1.2 Protein synthesis**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+D1.2+Protein+synthesis>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation made for D1.2.

**Watch for:**

- Which DNA strand RNA polymerase reads and why the RNA sequence is complementary to it.
- What mRNA, a ribosome and tRNA each contribute to translation.
- How codon–anticodon pairing determines which amino acid arrives.
- Where the HL additions begin: directionality, RNA processing, initiation and protein modification.

**Pause at:** the first complete transcription-and-translation diagram. Cover the labels and explain the path from a DNA base sequence to a polypeptide, naming RNA polymerase, mRNA, a ribosome, tRNA, codons, anticodons and peptide bonds.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Translation is a spatial, mechanical process. An **animation** can show a tRNA entering the A site, shifting to the P and E sites, and leaving while the ribosome advances along mRNA.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=translation+ribosome+A+P+E+sites+animation>

After watching, draw a ribosome with A, P and E labeled in that order. Add two tRNAs, the mRNA’s 5′ and 3′ ends, and an arrow showing ribosome movement. Then explain what changes after one peptide bond forms.
