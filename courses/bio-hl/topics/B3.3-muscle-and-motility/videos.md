# B3.3 Muscle and motility — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain the mechanism without help. A second full explanation builds recognition, which is already the habit causing trouble here. The sliding filament mechanism is spatial, though, so one focused animation is worth watching after the main video.

## Main

**Alex Lee Biology — B3.3 Muscle and motility**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+B3.3+Muscle+and+motility>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the explanation made for B3.3.

**Watch for:**

- What moves during contraction and what stays the same length.
- How calcium ions, ATP and myosin heads each contribute to the cross-bridge cycle.
- Why relaxation needs titin and an antagonistic muscle rather than a muscle pushing itself longer.
- How contraction becomes movement through tendons, bones and a joint.

**Pause at:** the first complete sarcomere diagram. Sketch it, label actin, myosin and both Z lines, then predict what happens to the I band, H zone and A band before the video tells you.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Actin and myosin overlap inside a three-dimensional structure, so an **animation** earns an exception to the one-video rule.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=sliding+filament+muscle+contraction+animation+actin+myosin+ATP+calcium>

After watching, draw three frames: exposed actin binding sites, cross-bridge and power stroke, then ATP-driven detachment. Under the frames, write which sarcomere regions shorten and which one stays constant.
