# B3.1 Gas exchange — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If a point only looked familiar after the reveal, it wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain why specialized gas-exchange systems are needed in large multicellular organisms. **[4]**

Gas exchange across the outer body surface can meet the needs of many small organisms. Explain why it becomes inadequate as organism size increases.

### Mark scheme

- Surface area-to-volume ratio decreases as organism size increases. **[1]**
- Less external surface is available for gas exchange per unit volume / per cell. **[1]**
- Distance between internal cells and the exterior increases / diffusion distance increases. **[1]**
- Diffusion across the outer surface cannot supply or remove gases rapidly enough, requiring specialized exchange surfaces / internal transport. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “Large organisms need more oxygen” earns little by itself. Full marks require both geometric changes and their effects on diffusion._

## Q2. Explain the mechanism of inhalation in a mammal. **[5]**

Begin with the actions of the muscles and end with the movement of air.

### Mark scheme

- Diaphragm contracts and flattens. **[1]**
- External intercostal muscles contract. **[1]**
- Ribs move upward and outward / thoracic volume increases. **[1]**
- Pressure in the lungs decreases below atmospheric pressure. **[1]**
- Air moves into the lungs down the pressure gradient. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the diaphragm doesn’t pull air into the lungs directly. The missing link in a near miss is usually that increased volume lowers pressure._

## Q3. Explain how mammalian alveoli are adapted for efficient gas exchange. **[5]**

Include features that affect surface area, diffusion distance, concentration gradients and stability of the exchange surface.

### Mark scheme

- Numerous alveoli provide a large surface area for diffusion. **[1]**
- Alveolar epithelium / type I pneumocytes form an extremely thin barrier, reducing diffusion distance. **[1]**
- A moist lining allows oxygen and carbon dioxide to dissolve before diffusion. **[1]**
- Extensive capillary beds / continuous blood flow maintain steep concentration gradients. **[1]**
- Surfactant reduces surface tension and helps prevent alveolar collapse. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming five structures isn’t enough. Each marking point links a feature to its effect on gas exchange or alveolar stability._

## Q4. Explain how gas exchange in a leaf causes transpiration and how two environmental factors affect its rate. **[6]**

Choose any two of light intensity, temperature, humidity and wind for the environmental part.

### Mark scheme

- Water evaporates from moist mesophyll cell walls into the leaf air spaces. **[1]**
- Stomata open to allow carbon dioxide to diffuse into the leaf. **[1]**
- Water vapor diffuses out through open stomata down a concentration gradient. **[1]**
- One named environmental factor is linked to a change in the water-vapor gradient / stomatal opening / evaporation rate. **[1]**
- A second named environmental factor is linked to a change in the water-vapor gradient / stomatal opening / evaporation rate. **[1]**
- The direction of the transpiration-rate change is stated correctly for both factors. **[1]**

_Examiner note: lists such as “heat, light and wind increase it” don’t explain the changes. Each factor needs a mechanism and a direction._

## Q5. Explain how cooperative binding produces the shape of the haemoglobin oxygen dissociation curve. **[4]**

Refer to affinity at low, intermediate and high oxygen partial pressures.

### Mark scheme

- The first oxygen molecule binds relatively less readily at low oxygen partial pressure. **[1]**
- Binding causes a conformational change that increases the affinity of remaining haem groups. **[1]**
- Subsequent oxygen molecules bind more readily, producing the steep middle section of the curve. **[1]**
- As haem groups become occupied, haemoglobin approaches saturation and the curve plateaus. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “The graph is S-shaped because binding is cooperative” names the idea but doesn’t explain the changing gradient or the plateau._

## Q6. Compare and contrast adult and foetal haemoglobin, and explain the effect of increased carbon dioxide on oxygen delivery. **[6]**

Your answer should refer to oxygen affinity and dissociation curves.

### Mark scheme

- Both adult and foetal haemoglobin bind oxygen reversibly to haem groups / show cooperative oxygen binding. **[1]**
- Foetal haemoglobin has a higher oxygen affinity than adult haemoglobin at the same oxygen partial pressure. **[1]**
- The foetal haemoglobin dissociation curve lies to the left of the adult curve. **[1]**
- Higher foetal affinity allows oxygen transfer from maternal blood to foetal blood across the placenta. **[1]**
- Increased carbon dioxide / decreased pH reduces haemoglobin’s oxygen affinity and shifts the curve to the right. **[1]**
- This Bohr shift increases oxygen unloading in actively respiring tissues. **[1]**

_Examiner note: don’t confuse a left-shifted foetal curve with the rightward Bohr shift. They describe different changes under different conditions._
