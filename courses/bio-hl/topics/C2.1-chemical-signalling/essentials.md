# C2.1 Chemical signalling — what you need to be able to do

> C2.1 is entirely HL-only. “Core” here means the foundation for the mechanisms that follow, not SL content. Tick a line only after you can explain it with the page closed.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how a ligand binds to a specific receptor and why only cells with that receptor respond.
- [ ] Explain quorum sensing and use bioluminescence in *Vibrio fischeri* as an example.
- [ ] Distinguish hormones, neurotransmitters, cytokines and calcium ions by where they act and what they do.
- [ ] Describe the chemical diversity of hormones and neurotransmitters, including why signalling chemicals need different properties.
- [ ] Contrast distant hormonal signalling through blood with localized neurotransmitter signalling across a synaptic gap.
- [ ] Distinguish transmembrane receptors from intracellular receptors using ligand solubility, receptor location and amino acid distribution.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how ligand binding activates a receptor and begins a signal transduction pathway.
- [ ] Explain how acetylcholine opens a receptor ion channel and changes the postsynaptic membrane potential.
- [ ] Outline how a G protein-coupled receptor transfers a signal from outside a cell to proteins inside it.
- [ ] Explain the epinephrine signalling pathway, including the G protein and cAMP as a second messenger.
- [ ] Explain how insulin receptor activation causes glucose transporter vesicles to move to the plasma membrane.
- [ ] Explain how steroid hormones cross the membrane, activate intracellular receptors and alter gene transcription.
- [ ] Explain the effects of oestradiol on GnRH-secreting hypothalamic cells and progesterone on endometrial cells.
- [ ] Distinguish positive from negative feedback and outline one hormonal example of each.
- [ ] Compare acetylcholine, epinephrine, insulin and steroid signalling from ligand binding to cellular response.

## Links to other topics

- **B1.2 Proteins** — receptors, G proteins and kinases depend on protein shape. Complementary binding and conformational change explain how a signal activates one pathway rather than every pathway.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — phospholipid bilayers separate hydrophilic ligands from intracellular proteins. Ion channels, facilitated diffusion and vesicle fusion appear inside the signalling pathways.
- **C2.2 Neural signalling** — acetylcholine links chemical transmission across a synapse to a change in postsynaptic membrane potential.
- **D2.2 Gene expression** — activated intracellular receptors bind specific DNA sequences and regulate transcription.
- **D3.1 Reproduction** — oestradiol, progesterone, GnRH, FSH and LH connect receptor action to control of the menstrual cycle.
- **D3.3 Homeostasis** — insulin signalling and negative feedback show how cells coordinate a whole-body response to changing blood glucose concentration.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ligand | A signalling chemical that binds to a specific site on a receptor | The receptor protein itself |
| Receptor | A protein with a binding site for a specific ligand | Any protein touched by a signalling chemical |
| Target cell | A cell that has the receptor needed to respond to a particular ligand | Every cell reached by a hormone in the blood |
| Quorum sensing | Regulation of gene expression in response to the concentration of signalling molecules released by bacteria | Bacteria counting one another or communicating through nerves |
| Hormone | A signalling chemical released into the blood and able to affect target cells, often at distant locations | A signal that affects every cell it reaches |
| Neurotransmitter | A chemical released by a neuron that diffuses across a synaptic gap to a nearby target cell | An electrical impulse moving along an axon |
| Cytokine | A signalling protein or peptide released by cells, often acting locally in immune and inflammatory responses | A steroid hormone carried only by the blood |
| Second messenger | An intracellular molecule or ion that relays a signal after receptor activation, such as cAMP or Ca²⁺ | The extracellular ligand, which is the first messenger |
| Signal transduction | Conversion of receptor activation into a sequence of intracellular events and a cellular response | Movement of the ligand through the receptor |
| Transmembrane receptor | A receptor spanning the plasma membrane, with regions exposed outside and inside the cell | A receptor floating freely in the cytoplasm |
| Intracellular receptor | A receptor in the cytoplasm or nucleus that binds a ligand able to cross the plasma membrane | A membrane channel that admits the hormone |
| G protein | A membrane-associated protein activated by a GPCR and able to regulate another protein inside the cell | A receptor that binds epinephrine directly and makes cAMP itself |
| cAMP | Cyclic adenosine monophosphate, an intracellular second messenger | ATP or the hormone epinephrine |
| Phosphorylation | Addition of a phosphate group to a molecule, often changing a protein’s activity | Adding glucose to a protein |
| Positive feedback | Regulation in which a response increases the original stimulus or pathway activity | A response that restores a variable toward its set range |
| Negative feedback | Regulation in which a response reduces the original stimulus or pathway activity | A pathway being harmful or switched off permanently |
