# C3.1 Integration of body systems — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can explain it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain why a multicellular organism needs system integration, using the hierarchy from cells to tissues, organs and body systems and the idea of emergent properties.
- [ ] Compare nervous and hormonal signaling, and explain how blood transports materials, energy and hormones between organs.
- [ ] Distinguish the brain’s integration of conscious information from the spinal cord’s integration of unconscious responses.
- [ ] Trace information from a receptor through sensory neurons to the central nervous system, then from the cerebral hemispheres through motor neurons to skeletal muscle.
- [ ] Identify the protective sheath, myelinated nerve fibres and unmyelinated nerve fibres in a transverse section of a nerve.
- [ ] Explain a pain reflex arc involving a free sensory nerve ending, sensory neuron, interneuron, motor neuron and skeletal muscle.
- [ ] Outline how the cerebellum coordinates skeletal muscle contractions and balance, and how melatonin helps establish the sleep–wake cycle.
- [ ] Explain how epinephrine prepares several body systems for vigorous activity and how the hypothalamus and pituitary coordinate endocrine control.
- [ ] Explain feedback control of heart rate and ventilation using baroreceptors, chemoreceptors, the medulla, nerves and respiratory muscles.
- [ ] Distinguish voluntary control of swallowing and egestion from the enteric nervous system’s involuntary control of peristalsis.

## Higher level

- [ ] Record qualitative and quantitative observations of tropic responses, and explain positive phototropism in a shoot exposed to lateral light.
- [ ] Explain how coordinated placement of auxin efflux carriers creates an auxin concentration gradient through plant tissue.
- [ ] Explain how auxin promotes cell elongation by acidifying the apoplast, and how auxin and cytokinin integrate root and shoot growth.
- [ ] Explain why the relationship between ethylene production and fruit ripening is positive feedback and how it produces rapid, synchronized ripening.

## Links to other topics

- **B2.3 Cell specialization** — receptor cells, neurons, muscle fibres and gland cells have structures suited to different jobs. That lets you explain how specialized parts contribute to an integrated response.
- **B3.1 Gas exchange** — changing ventilation alters the removal of carbon dioxide. This supplies the physiological effect in the ventilation feedback loop.
- **B3.2 Transport** — blood links organs by moving oxygen, nutrients, hormones, heat and wastes. C3.1 adds the signals that coordinate those movements.
- **B3.3 Muscle and motility** — motor neurons and the cerebellum produce coordinated movement by controlling skeletal muscle contraction.
- **C2.1 Chemical signaling** — hormones bind to specific receptors on target cells. C3.1 scales that mechanism up to communication between organs.
- **C2.2 Neural signaling** — action potentials and synapses explain how the sensory, interneuron and motor stages of a reflex transmit information.
- **D3.3 Homeostasis** — baroreceptor and chemoreceptor loops are examples of feedback maintaining internal conditions within tolerable limits.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System integration | Coordination and communication among components so they collectively perform an overall function | Different organs working independently at the same time |
| Emergent property | A property of an integrated system that its separate components don’t possess alone | A new physical structure appearing without interactions among components |
| Sensory neuron | A neuron carrying impulses from a receptor toward the central nervous system | A neuron carrying commands from the brain to a muscle |
| Motor neuron | A neuron carrying impulses from the central nervous system to an effector | Any neuron located inside the spinal cord |
| Nerve | A bundle of nerve fibres enclosed by protective connective tissue | One neuron or one nerve fibre |
| Reflex | A rapid, involuntary response to a stimulus | Any movement completed quickly |
| Interneuron | A neuron within the central nervous system that connects neurons in a processing pathway | A sensory neuron running from a receptor to the spinal cord |
| Cerebellum | A brain region that coordinates skeletal muscle contraction and balance | The spinal integration center for the pain withdrawal reflex |
| Circadian rhythm | A biological cycle lasting about 24 hours | A response that occurs once whenever light disappears |
| Baroreceptor | A sensory receptor that detects changes in blood pressure | A receptor that directly measures blood oxygen concentration |
| Chemoreceptor | A sensory receptor that detects chemical conditions such as blood pH, carbon dioxide or oxygen concentration | A receptor that detects pressure stretching an artery wall |
| Peristalsis | Coordinated waves of contraction in the gut wall that move its contents | Voluntary contraction of skeletal muscles throughout the digestive tract |
| Tropism | A directional growth response toward or away from a directional stimulus | Movement of the whole plant from one location to another |
| Phytohormone | A plant signaling chemical that regulates growth, development or responses | A nutrient transported from roots to shoots |
| Auxin efflux carrier | A membrane transport protein that moves auxin out of a plant cell | A receptor that detects the direction of light |
| Positive feedback | A process in which a change promotes further change in the same direction | Regulation that reverses a change and restores a set range |
