# A2.2 Cell structure — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If a structure appears in your answer, connect it to evidence or a function.

## Q1. Explain how cell theory can be used to make a prediction about a newly discovered organism. **[3]**

Cell theory is an example of a scientific theory from which predictions can be deduced. Explain the reasoning and prediction that apply to a newly discovered living organism.

### Mark scheme

- Living organisms consist of one or more cells. **[1]**
- Deductive reasoning applies a general theory to a specific new case. **[1]**
- The newly discovered organism is predicted to consist of at least one cell. **[1]**

_Examiner note: stating that organisms contain cells gives the theory but not the deduction. Full marks require the prediction about the new organism and the reasoning that produced it._

## Q2. Calculate the actual length of a cell. **[3]**

A micrograph shows a cell with an image length of 45 mm at a magnification of ×1500. Calculate the cell's actual length in micrometers.

### Mark scheme

- Uses actual size = image size ÷ magnification. **[1]**
- Converts 45 mm to 45,000 µm before division / converts the final result correctly from millimeters. **[1]**
- Actual length = 30 µm. **[1]**

_Examiner note: 45 ÷ 1500 = 0.03 isn't a complete answer unless the unit is handled. The scoring result is 0.03 mm or 30 µm, with 30 µm matching the requested unit._

## Q3. Compare and contrast prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structure. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

Award any six:

- Both contain a plasma membrane and cytoplasm. **[1]**
- Both contain DNA as genetic material and ribosomes for protein synthesis. **[1]**
- Prokaryotic DNA is naked and usually circular, whereas eukaryotic nuclear DNA is linear and associated with histones. **[1]**
- Prokaryotic DNA occupies a nucleoid region, whereas eukaryotic chromosomes are enclosed within a double-membrane nucleus with pores. **[1]**
- Prokaryotic cytoplasm contains 70S ribosomes, whereas eukaryotic cytoplasm contains 80S ribosomes. **[1]**
- Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound cytoplasmic organelles, whereas eukaryotes contain organelles such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus. **[1]**
- Prokaryotic cells are generally smaller than eukaryotic cells. **[1]**

_Examiner note: two separate lists earn less than paired comparisons. Each difference should place the prokaryotic and eukaryotic versions of the same feature in one statement._

## Q4. Distinguish four atypical eukaryotic cell structures by their numbers of nuclei. **[4]**

Refer to aseptate fungal hyphae, skeletal muscle fibers, mature mammalian red blood cells and mature phloem sieve tube elements.

### Mark scheme

- Aseptate fungal hyphae have many nuclei in continuous cytoplasm / lack septa dividing the hypha into separate cells. **[1]**
- Skeletal muscle fibers are multinucleate / form by fusion of precursor cells. **[1]**
- Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus. **[1]**
- Mature phloem sieve tube elements lack a nucleus / depend metabolically on companion cells. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that these cells are “unusual” earns nothing by itself. The distinction being tested is whether each example has many nuclei or no nucleus._

## Q5. Explain the evidence for the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- An ancestral eukaryotic cell engulfed an aerobic prokaryote that survived and became a mitochondrion. **[1]**
- A photosynthetic prokaryote was later engulfed in some lineages and became a chloroplast. **[1]**
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts contain 70S ribosomes, as do prokaryotes. **[1]**
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts contain naked circular DNA resembling prokaryotic DNA. **[1]**
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts can replicate within the cell / show a prokaryote-like capacity for division. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the organelle features score as evidence only when their similarity to prokaryotes is clear. “They have DNA and ribosomes” misses the significant forms: naked circular DNA and 70S ribosomes._

## Q6. Explain how differentiation and multicellularity allow specialized tissues to develop. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Cells in an organism generally contain the same genome. **[1]**
- Different cell types express different sets / patterns of genes. **[1]**
- Changes in the environment / signals can activate or suppress gene expression. **[1]**
- Different proteins are produced, causing cells to develop different structures and functions / differentiate. **[1]**
- Specialized cells cooperate in tissues / division of labor permits more efficient functions and larger body size. **[1]**

_Examiner note: differentiation doesn't mean that each cell receives different genes. The central chain is different gene expression, different proteins, different structure and function._
