# D3.2 Inheritance — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and produce a cross or explanation from memory. A second explanation of the same material builds recognition, which is the problem you’re trying to leave behind.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — D3.2 Inheritance**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+D3.2+Inheritance>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation built for D3.2.

**Watch for:**

- How chromosome movements during meiosis explain segregation and independent assortment.
- How the phenotype of a heterozygote changes among complete dominance, incomplete dominance and codominance.
- What evidence in a pedigree supports autosomal recessive or X-linked recessive inheritance.
- Why linked genes produce more parental offspring than recombinant offspring.

**Pause at:** the first completed genetic cross. Cover the offspring grid, write every possible gamete from each parent, rebuild the grid and state the predicted phenotypic ratio aloud.

Use this when you can follow a finished grid but can’t generate the gametes or explain why linkage changes the ratio.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Independent assortment and crossing over are spatial processes, so an **animation** earns an exception to the one-video rule. You need to see homologous chromosomes orient independently and non-sister chromatids exchange corresponding DNA.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=meiosis+independent+assortment+crossing+over+animation>

After watching, draw two homologous pairs at metaphase I twice, using the two possible orientations. Then draw one crossover between two linked loci and label the parental and recombinant chromatids.
