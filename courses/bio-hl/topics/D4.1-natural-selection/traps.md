# D4.1 Natural selection — where the marks go

## Saying individuals evolve

**Commonly written:** "The cold made each animal evolve thicker fur."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Evolution is a change in a population across generations. An individual can develop, but its allele frequencies can't evolve.

**Scores instead:** "Cold favored individuals with heritable traits for insulation, so the associated alleles became more frequent over generations."

## Making useful mutations happen on demand

**Commonly written:** "The bacteria mutated because they needed resistance to the antibiotic."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mutation isn't directed by need. The antibiotic selects resistant variants that arose before or during exposure.

**Scores instead:** "Random mutation produced resistant variants, and antibiotic treatment increased their relative survival and reproduction."

## Defining fitness as survival or strength

**Commonly written:** "The strongest organisms survive, so they have the greatest fitness."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Fitness is relative reproductive success. Survival contributes only when it leads to greater genetic contribution to the next generation.

**Scores instead:** "Individuals with the greatest fitness pass their alleles to more offspring than other individuals in the population."

## Inheriting an acquired characteristic

**Commonly written:** "A giraffe stretches its neck, so its offspring inherit a longer neck."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Stretching doesn't change the gene base sequence passed through gametes, so the acquired change isn't inherited by that mechanism.

**Scores instead:** "Giraffes with heritable variation for longer necks may reproduce more, increasing the associated allele frequencies."

## Treating sexual selection as separate from reproductive success

**Commonly written:** "Bright plumage evolves because it helps the bird survive."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A sexually selected trait can reduce survival. It spreads when it increases mating success enough to raise overall reproductive success.

**Scores instead:** "Preferred plumage increases mating success, so males carrying its alleles contribute more offspring to the next generation."

## Mixing up the three selection patterns

**Commonly written:** "Stabilizing selection favors both extreme phenotypes."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Stabilizing selection favors intermediates. Selection favoring both extremes while acting against intermediates is disruptive selection.

**Scores instead:** "Stabilizing selection narrows the distribution around the mean, while disruptive selection tends to produce two peaks."

## Treating q as the recessive phenotype frequency

**Commonly written:** "If 9% show the recessive phenotype, q equals 0.09."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The recessive phenotype represents the homozygous genotype frequency q². Its square root gives the recessive allele frequency q.

**Scores instead:** "Because q² = 0.09, q = 0.30 and p = 0.70."

## Calling antibiotic resistance artificial selection

**Commonly written:** "Antibiotic resistance is artificial selection because humans use the antibiotic."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Humans create the pressure but don't deliberately choose bacterial parents. Differential survival and reproduction make this natural selection.

**Scores instead:** "Antibiotics select resistant bacteria naturally, whereas artificial selection requires deliberate human choice of breeding individuals."
