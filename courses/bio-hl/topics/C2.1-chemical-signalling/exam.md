# C2.1 Chemical signalling — exam practice

## Q1. Explain how quorum sensing controls bioluminescence in Vibrio fischeri. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- *Vibrio fischeri* cells release a signalling molecule / autoinducer. **[1]**
- Autoinducer concentration increases as bacterial population density increases. **[1]**
- A threshold concentration results in binding to receptors / activation of a signal transduction pathway. **[1]**
- Receptor activation changes gene expression, resulting in production of proteins required for bioluminescence. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the bacteria “know how many cells are present” misses the measurable signal. Full marks require increasing autoinducer concentration, a threshold and altered gene expression._

## Q2. Distinguish transmembrane receptors from intracellular receptors. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Transmembrane receptors span the plasma membrane, whereas intracellular receptors occur in the cytoplasm or nucleus. **[1]**
- Transmembrane receptors contain hydrophobic amino acid regions in contact with the phospholipid bilayer and hydrophilic regions exposed to aqueous environments. **[1]**
- Hydrophilic ligands remain outside the cell and bind to transmembrane receptors. **[1]**
- Hydrophobic / lipid-soluble ligands cross the plasma membrane and bind to intracellular receptors. **[1]**

_Examiner note: receptor location alone earns only part of the answer. The distinction must connect ligand solubility to membrane penetration and receptor structure._

## Q3. Explain how epinephrine produces a response in a target cell using cAMP. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Epinephrine binds specifically to a G protein-coupled receptor in the plasma membrane. **[1]**
- Ligand binding changes the receptor’s conformation and activates a G protein on the cytoplasmic surface. **[1]**
- The activated G protein activates adenylyl cyclase. **[1]**
- Adenylyl cyclase converts ATP to cAMP. **[1]**
- cAMP acts as a second messenger and activates an intracellular enzyme cascade / cellular response. **[1]**

_Examiner note: epinephrine doesn’t enter the cell, and the receptor doesn’t produce cAMP directly. The answer must place the G protein and adenylyl cyclase between receptor activation and cAMP._

## Q4. Explain how insulin causes increased glucose uptake by a target cell. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Insulin binds to a specific transmembrane insulin receptor. **[1]**
- Binding activates tyrosine kinase activity / causes phosphorylation of tyrosine residues inside the cell. **[1]**
- Phosphorylation initiates a sequence of intracellular reactions / signal transduction pathway. **[1]**
- Vesicles containing glucose transporters move to and fuse with the plasma membrane. **[1]**
- More glucose transporters increase glucose entry by facilitated diffusion. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “insulin moves glucose into the cell” misses the mechanism. Insulin activates the receptor; transporter-containing vesicles move, and glucose then crosses through those transporters._

## Q5. Compare and contrast epinephrine and progesterone signalling in target cells. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both act as ligands that bind to specific receptor proteins in target cells. **[1]**
- Epinephrine is hydrophilic / remains outside the cell, whereas progesterone is hydrophobic / crosses the plasma membrane. **[1]**
- Epinephrine binds to a transmembrane GPCR, whereas progesterone binds to an intracellular receptor. **[1]**
- Epinephrine activates a G protein and cAMP second-messenger pathway. **[1]**
- The activated progesterone-receptor complex binds to specific DNA sequences and promotes transcription. **[1]**
- Epinephrine can produce a rapid enzyme-mediated response, whereas progesterone produces a response involving altered protein synthesis. **[1]**

_Examiner note: two separate descriptions won’t automatically score as a comparison. Each contrast should pair the two pathways using the same feature, such as membrane crossing, receptor location or cellular response._

## Q6. Explain positive and negative feedback using one hormonal example of each. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Positive feedback increases / reinforces the original stimulus or response. **[1]**
- Uterine contractions stimulate secretion of oxytocin. **[1]**
- Oxytocin strengthens uterine contractions, causing further oxytocin secretion until birth. **[1]**
- Negative feedback reduces / opposes the original change. **[1]**
- High blood glucose stimulates insulin secretion, and insulin promotes glucose uptake / storage. **[1]**
- Falling blood glucose reduces the stimulus for insulin secretion / returns glucose toward its regulated range. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming childbirth and blood glucose isn’t enough. Each example needs the direction of the loop: the response either strengthens the initiating change or reduces it._
