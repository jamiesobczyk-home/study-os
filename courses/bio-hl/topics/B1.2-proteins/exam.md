## Q1. Outline how a dipeptide is formed from two amino acids. **[3]**

### Mark scheme

- The reaction is condensation. **[1]**
- The amine group of one amino acid reacts with the carboxyl group of the
  other, forming a peptide bond. **[1]**
- A molecule of water is released / eliminated. **[1]**

_Examiner note: all three points are separate marks, and candidates routinely
drop the water. "They join by a peptide bond" is one mark, not three._

## Q2. Explain the effect of high temperature on the structure and function of an enzyme. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Increased temperature increases kinetic energy / vibration of the
  molecule. **[1]**
- This disrupts the hydrogen bonds / ionic bonds / hydrophobic interactions
  holding the tertiary structure. **[1]**
- The three-dimensional conformation changes — the protein is denatured. **[1]**
- The active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits / the enzyme
  loses function. **[1]**
- Accept: peptide bonds and primary structure are not affected. **[1 max within total]**

_Examiner note: an answer that says "the enzyme denatures and stops working"
scores one mark at most. "Explain" wants the chain — energy, bonds, shape,
function — and each link is a separate mark._

## Q3. Distinguish between the secondary and tertiary structure of a protein. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

Comparative statements. Maximum [4]:

- Secondary structure is regular repeating folding (alpha helix or beta pleated
  sheet) whereas tertiary structure is the overall three-dimensional shape of
  the whole polypeptide. **[1]**
- Secondary structure is stabilised by hydrogen bonds between backbone amine
  and carboxyl groups, whereas tertiary structure is stabilised by interactions
  between R groups. **[1]**
- Tertiary structure involves four types of interaction — hydrogen bonds, ionic
  bonds, disulfide covalent bonds and hydrophobic interactions — whereas
  secondary structure involves hydrogen bonds only. **[1]**
- Secondary structure is independent of the R groups present whereas tertiary
  structure depends directly on which R groups are in the sequence. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the backbone-versus-R-group distinction is the mark that
separates grades here. An answer that attributes secondary structure to R group
interactions loses it and usually confuses the rest of the answer too._

## Q4. Explain how the primary structure of a polypeptide determines its tertiary structure. **[3]**

### Mark scheme

- Primary structure is the sequence of amino acids. **[1]**
- The sequence determines which R groups are present and their positions in the
  chain. **[1]**
- The R groups interact — by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bonds and
  hydrophobic interactions — and these interactions fold the chain into a
  specific three-dimensional shape. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "the sequence determines the shape" restates the question and
scores nothing on its own. The mark is in naming R groups as the link between
the two._

## Q5. Compare and contrast fibrous and globular proteins. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

Comparative statements only. Maximum [4]:

- Both are polypeptides / both are built from amino acids joined by peptide
  bonds. **[1]**
- Fibrous proteins are long and narrow whereas globular proteins are rounded
  and compact. **[1]**
- Fibrous proteins have a repetitive amino acid sequence whereas globular
  proteins have an irregular sequence. **[1]**
- Fibrous proteins are largely insoluble in water whereas globular proteins are
  soluble. **[1]**
- Fibrous proteins have structural roles whereas globular proteins have
  functional roles such as catalysis or transport. **[1]**
- Named examples used comparatively: collagen or keratin versus haemoglobin,
  insulin or an enzyme. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "compare and contrast" requires at least one similarity, and
every point must be written comparatively. "Fibrous proteins are insoluble"
scores nothing on its own._

## Q6. State what is meant by a conjugated protein, and give one example. **[2]**

### Mark scheme

- A protein containing a non-polypeptide prosthetic group in addition to its
  polypeptide(s). **[1]**
- Example: haemoglobin, which contains haem groups. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "state" wants two sentences at most. A protein with more than
one chain is not automatically conjugated — that is quaternary structure, which
is a different idea._
