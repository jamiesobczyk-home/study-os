# D4.3 Climate change — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain one positive feedback cycle involving melting snow and ice. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Increased global temperature causes melting / loss of snow and ice. **[1]**
- Loss of reflective snow and ice lowers albedo / exposes darker land or water. **[1]**
- More incoming solar radiation is absorbed / less incoming radiation is reflected. **[1]**
- Increased absorption causes further warming and therefore further melting. **[1]**

_Examiner note: a list of changes earns fewer marks than a closed loop. The final point must return to further warming or melting._

## Q2. Distinguish correlation from causation using carbon dioxide and temperature data from Antarctic ice cores. **[3]**

### Mark scheme

- Positive correlation means that higher carbon dioxide concentrations are associated with higher global temperatures / the variables tend to change in the same direction. **[1]**
- Correlation alone does not demonstrate that changing carbon dioxide caused the temperature change. **[1]**
- Other evidence, such as absorption of long-wave infrared radiation by carbon dioxide, supports the causal link. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying “the graph proves carbon dioxide causes warming” misses the nature-of-science distinction. The ice-core pattern shows correlation, while causation requires additional evidence._

## Q3. Explain how a boreal forest can change from a net carbon sink to a net carbon source. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Warmer temperatures and decreased winter snowfall increase the incidence of drought in taiga. **[1]**
- Drought causes forest browning / reduces primary production and carbon dioxide uptake. **[1]**
- Forest fires become more frequent and/or more intense. **[1]**
- Fire releases carbon dioxide by combustion of biomass and legacy carbon. **[1]**
- Carbon losses exceed carbon accumulation, causing net carbon release / further warming. **[1]**

_Examiner note: full marks require both sides of the carbon balance. Reduced fixation and increased release explain the switch from sink to source._

## Q4. Explain how climate change threatens coral reef ecosystems. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Increased atmospheric carbon dioxide causes more carbon dioxide to dissolve in seawater. **[1]**
- Carbonic acid forms / hydrogen ion concentration increases / ocean pH decreases. **[1]**
- Carbonate ion availability decreases, suppressing calcium carbonate deposition / coral calcification. **[1]**
- Increased water temperature causes heat stress and coral bleaching. **[1]**
- Bleaching involves loss of photosynthetic symbionts or their pigments from coral tissue. **[1]**
- Loss of corals removes habitat and resources for other species, potentially causing reef ecosystem collapse. **[1]**

_Examiner note: acidification and bleaching must follow separate causal chains. Carbon dioxide suppresses calcification through lower pH, while high temperature causes bleaching._

## Q5. Explain how warming of surface ocean water can affect marine food chains. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Warmer surface water strengthens stratification / reduces mixing with deeper water. **[1]**
- Nutrient upwelling from deeper water is reduced or delayed. **[1]**
- Fewer nutrients reach phytoplankton in the sunlit surface layer, decreasing primary production. **[1]**
- Less biomass / chemical energy is available to consumers at higher trophic levels. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “warming kills plankton” misses the required mechanism. The answer needs the chain through stratification, nutrient supply and primary production._

## Q6. Explain how climate change can alter biological timing and cause evolutionary change. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Phenology is research into the timing of recurring biological events. **[1]**
- Temperature and photoperiod act as cues for events such as flowering, migration or nesting. **[1]**
- Climate change alters temperature patterns but does not alter the annual photoperiod cycle. **[1]**
- Interacting populations may shift their events by different amounts, disrupting phenological synchrony. **[1]**
- Accept: reduced snow cover increases the relative fitness / survival and reproductive success of brown tawny owl variants. **[1]**
- Heritable color variants change in frequency over generations / alleles associated with the fitter variant increase in frequency. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an individual owl doesn’t evolve or change color. The evolutionary mark requires a heritable population-level change across generations._
