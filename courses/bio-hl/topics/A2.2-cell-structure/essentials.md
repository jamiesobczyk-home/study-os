# A2.2 Cell structure — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] State cell theory and use it to predict that a newly discovered organism consists of one or more cells.
- [ ] Prepare, stain, focus and measure a temporary mount using a light microscope.
- [ ] Calculate magnification, actual size and scale-bar length after converting measurements to the same units.
- [ ] Explain what electron microscopy, freeze fracture, cryogenic electron microscopy, fluorescent stains and immunofluorescence let scientists observe.
- [ ] Explain why all typical cells need DNA, cytoplasm, a plasma membrane and ribosomes.
- [ ] Describe a Gram-positive prokaryotic cell and distinguish its structure from that of a eukaryotic cell.
- [ ] Identify the major structures of eukaryotic cells and connect each structure to its function.
- [ ] Explain how a unicellular organism carries out all the functions of life within one cell.
- [ ] Compare animal, fungal and plant cells using cell walls, vacuoles, plastids, centrioles, cilia and flagella.
- [ ] Explain how numbers of nuclei make aseptate fungal hyphae, skeletal muscle fibers, mature mammalian red blood cells and phloem sieve tube elements atypical.
- [ ] Identify prokaryotic, animal and plant cells, and their visible structures, in light and electron micrographs.
- [ ] Draw and annotate structures from electron micrographs using clear lines, accurate proportions and functions in the annotations.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts, including the evidence from their ribosomes, DNA and replication.
- [ ] Explain how different patterns of gene expression produce differentiated cells and specialized tissues.
- [ ] Explain why multicellularity evolved repeatedly and how larger body size and cell specialization can be advantageous.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — DNA stores genetic information in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. This connection explains why DNA counts as a feature shared by all cells.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — A2.2 identifies the plasma membrane as the cell boundary. B2.1 explains its molecular structure and how substances cross it.
- **B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization** — the organelles introduced here become a system of specialized compartments. Knowing their identities first makes the later transport pathways easier to follow.
- **B2.3 Cell specialization** — differentiation changes gene expression, while specialization connects the resulting cell structure to a particular function.
- **D2.2 Gene expression** — cells with the same genome can develop differently because different genes are expressed. D2.2 supplies the molecular control behind that claim.
- **A3.1 Diversity of organisms** — cell walls, plastids, cilia and other cell features help compare major groups of organisms.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell theory | The theory that living organisms consist of one or more cells, cells are the smallest units of life, and cells arise from pre-existing cells | A claim that every cell has a nucleus |
| Magnification | Image size divided by actual size | Resolution, which is the ability to distinguish nearby points |
| Resolution | The ability to distinguish two close points as separate | How large the image appears |
| Micrograph | An image produced using a microscope | A biological drawing made from that image |
| Ultrastructure | Cell structure visible at the resolution of an electron microscope | Only the structures visible with a classroom light microscope |
| Prokaryote | A cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; its DNA occupies a nucleoid region | A cell with no DNA or internal organization |
| Eukaryote | A cell with chromosomes inside a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles | Any cell belonging to a multicellular organism |
| Nucleoid | The region of a prokaryotic cell containing its main DNA molecule | A membrane-bound nucleus |
| Naked DNA | DNA that isn’t associated with histone proteins | DNA without genes or nucleotides |
| Organelle | A specialized structure within a cell | A complete cell within a tissue |
| Immunofluorescence | Use of fluorescently labeled antibodies to locate a specific antigen, usually a protein | Fluorescent staining that labels every structure equally |
| Differentiation | Development of different cell types through different patterns of gene expression | A change in the DNA sequence of every specialized cell |
| Endosymbiosis | A relationship in which one cell lived inside another and eventually became an organelle | Temporary digestion of one cell by another |
| Multicellular | Consisting of many cooperating cells | Any group of independent unicellular organisms |
