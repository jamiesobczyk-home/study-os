# D2.3 Water potential — exam practice

## Q1. Explain the solvation of ions and polar molecules in water. **[4]**

Explain how water molecules interact with a positive ion, a negative ion and a polar solute.

### Mark scheme

- Polar water molecules orient with their partially negative oxygen regions toward positive ions. **[1]**
- Polar water molecules orient with their partially positive hydrogen regions toward negative ions. **[1]**
- Water forms hydrogen bonds with suitable polar solute molecules. **[1]**
- Attractions between water and solute particles surround and disperse the particles in solution. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that water is polar earns little without matching the correct partial charge to each ion and distinguishing ion–dipole attraction from hydrogen bonding with polar solutes._

## Q2. Explain osmosis and its effect on an animal cell. **[5]**

An animal cell is transferred from isotonic tissue fluid to a hypertonic solution. Explain the resulting changes.

### Mark scheme

- The surrounding solution has a higher solute concentration than the cell / is hypertonic relative to the cell. **[1]**
- Water moves in both directions through the partially permeable cell membrane. **[1]**
- There is net movement of water out of the cell by osmosis. **[1]**
- The cell loses volume / shrinks. **[1]**
- The cell becomes crenated / develops an irregularly shrunken surface. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “water moves from high to low concentration” is too vague. Full marks require the direction of net water movement, the partially permeable membrane and the named cellular effect._

## Q3. Explain how plant tissue can be used to estimate an isotonic solute concentration. **[5]**

Plant cylinders with different initial masses are placed in a range of sucrose concentrations. Explain how the data should be processed and interpreted.

### Mark scheme

- Measure the final mass of each cylinder after a controlled period / remove surface solution before weighing. **[1]**
- Calculate percentage change in mass to allow comparison among different initial masses. **[1]**
- Calculate a mean for repeated samples at each sucrose concentration. **[1]**
- Plot mean percentage change in mass against sucrose concentration. **[1]**
- The concentration at zero percentage change / the x-intercept estimates the isotonic concentration. **[1]**

_Examiner note: choosing the sample with the smallest final mass doesn’t identify the isotonic point. The estimate comes from zero change relative to the starting mass._

## Q4. Compare and contrast the effects of a hypotonic solution on plant and animal cells. **[5]**

Compare the movement of water and the resulting condition of each cell type.

### Mark scheme

- Water enters both cell types by osmosis because the external solution is hypotonic. **[1]**
- Both cell types initially increase in volume / swell. **[1]**
- An animal cell may lyse because it lacks a cell wall. **[1]**
- A plant cell develops turgor pressure as its contents press against the cell wall. **[1]**
- The plant cell becomes turgid and resists further expansion / usually does not burst because of the wall. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the cell wall doesn’t block osmosis. The comparison needs the shared water entry followed by the different outcomes caused by the presence or absence of a wall._

## Q5. Explain water movement using water-potential values. **[5]**

Cell X has Ψs = −800 kPa and Ψp = +300 kPa. Cell Y has Ψs = −650 kPa and Ψp = 0 kPa. The cell membranes are permeable to water. Calculate each water potential and explain the direction of net water movement.

### Mark scheme

- Uses Ψw = Ψs + Ψp. **[1]**
- Cell X: Ψw = −800 kPa + 300 kPa = −500 kPa. **[1]**
- Cell Y: Ψw = −650 kPa + 0 kPa = −650 kPa. **[1]**
- Net water movement is from cell X to cell Y. **[1]**
- Water moves from higher / less negative water potential to lower / more negative water potential. **[1]**

_Examiner note: comparing −800 kPa with −650 kPa compares solute potentials only. Pressure changes the total for cell X, so both Ψw values must be calculated first._

## Q6. Explain changes in a plant cell placed in a hypotonic solution in terms of water potential. **[6]**

Include solute potential, pressure potential and the condition reached at equilibrium.

### Mark scheme

- The external solution has a higher water potential than the cell. **[1]**
- Water enters the cell down the water-potential gradient. **[1]**
- Entry of water dilutes the cell sap / makes Ψs less negative. **[1]**
- The cell contents press against the cell wall / Ψp increases. **[1]**
- Increasing Ψp raises the cell's total water potential. **[1]**
- Net movement stops when internal and external water potentials are equal / the cell reaches equilibrium and is turgid. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “water enters until the cell is full” misses the mechanism. Full marks require the changing contributions of both Ψs and Ψp and equality of total water potential at equilibrium._
