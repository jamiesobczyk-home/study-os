# D2.1 Cell and nuclear division — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that felt familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Compare and contrast cytokinesis in animal and plant cells. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Both processes divide the cytoplasm between daughter cells / follow nuclear division. **[1]**
- In animal cells, a contractile ring of actin and myosin constricts the plasma membrane. **[1]**
- The constriction forms a cleavage furrow that moves inward and pinches the cell apart. **[1]**
- In plant cells, vesicles fuse at the centre to form a cell plate, which grows outward and supplies new membrane and cell wall material. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that both cells “split in half” earns only the similarity mark. Full marks require the different mechanisms and directions of division._

## Q2. Explain how mitosis produces two genetically identical nuclei. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- DNA replication before mitosis produces two sister chromatids per chromosome. **[1]**
- Chromosomes condense and spindle microtubules attach to them. **[1]**
- Duplicated chromosomes align at the equator during metaphase. **[1]**
- Sister chromatids separate during anaphase and move to opposite poles. **[1]**
- Each new nucleus receives one copy of every chromosome / the chromosome number and genome are maintained. **[1]**

_Examiner note: a list of phase names can’t reach full marks. The answer must track the replicated DNA into the two nuclei._

## Q3. Explain how meiosis both halves chromosome number and generates genetic variation. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- One diploid nucleus undergoes one round of DNA replication followed by two nuclear divisions. **[1]**
- Homologous chromosomes pair to form bivalents. **[1]**
- Homologous chromosomes segregate in meiosis I, reducing the chromosome number from diploid to haploid. **[1]**
- Sister chromatids segregate in meiosis II, producing four haploid nuclei. **[1]**
- Crossing over between non-sister chromatids produces recombinant chromatids. **[1]**
- Random orientation of bivalents produces different combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “meiosis mixes the genes” is too vague. Crossing over and random orientation need separate mechanisms, and reduction must be tied to homologous chromosome separation._

## Q4. Explain how non-disjunction during meiosis can result in a child with Down syndrome. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Homologous chromosomes fail to separate in anaphase I / sister chromatids fail to separate in anaphase II. **[1]**
- A resulting gamete can contain two copies of chromosome 21. **[1]**
- Fusion with a normal gamete adds a third copy of chromosome 21. **[1]**
- The resulting zygote has trisomy 21 / three copies of chromosome 21, causing Down syndrome. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “an extra chromosome causes Down syndrome” misses the requested chain. The answer must identify failed separation, the abnormal gamete and fertilization._

## Q5. Explain how cell-cycle control can be lost and lead to tumour formation. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Concentrations of different cyclins increase and decrease during the cell cycle. **[1]**
- A threshold concentration of a specific cyclin is required for passage through a checkpoint. **[1]**
- A mutated / overexpressed proto-oncogene can become an oncogene that promotes excessive cell-cycle progression. **[1]**
- Mutation of a tumour suppressor gene can remove inhibition of division / reduce apoptosis. **[1]**
- Loss of control causes uncontrolled cell proliferation and tumour formation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: cyclins don’t cause cancer merely by existing. Full marks require normal threshold control followed by a genetic change that disrupts the balance._

## Q6. Compare and contrast two tumour samples and suggest which is more concerning. **[6]**

Sample A contains 12 cells in mitosis among 300 observed cells. Sample B contains 54 cells in mitosis among 300 observed cells. Sample B also contains cells invading neighboring tissue.

### Mark scheme

- Mitotic index of sample A is 12 ÷ 300 × 100 = 4%. **[1]**
- Mitotic index of sample B is 54 ÷ 300 × 100 = 18%. **[1]**
- Sample B has a higher rate of cell division / growth than sample A. **[1]**
- Invasion of neighboring tissue indicates that sample B is malignant / cancerous. **[1]**
- A benign tumour remains localized and lacks the capacity to invade or metastasize. **[1]**
- Sample B is more concerning because malignant cells may metastasize and form secondary tumours. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the higher mitotic index supports faster growth, but invasion is the evidence for malignancy. Treating those as the same property loses the distinction._
