# D1.3 Mutation and gene editing — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain the effect of a one-base deletion. **[4]**

A single nucleotide is deleted near the beginning of a protein-coding sequence. Explain why this mutation is likely to prevent the polypeptide from functioning.

### Mark scheme

- Deletion of one nucleotide changes the grouping of bases into codons / causes a frameshift. **[1]**
- All codons downstream of the deletion are likely to be altered. **[1]**
- Different amino acids are incorporated into the polypeptide / the primary structure is extensively changed. **[1]**
- A premature stop codon may be produced / altered primary structure may change folding and the active site, causing loss of function. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that one nucleotide produces one changed amino acid misses the frameshift. Full marks require the effect on multiple downstream codons and a link to protein function._

## Q2. Compare mutations in germ cells and somatic cells. **[4]**

Compare the possible consequences of a mutation in a germ cell with the consequences of a mutation in a somatic cell.

### Mark scheme

- A mutation in a germ cell can enter a gamete and be inherited by offspring. **[1]**
- An inherited germline mutation can introduce a new allele into a population / contribute to evolution. **[1]**
- A somatic mutation is transmitted to descendant cells by mitosis but normally is not inherited by offspring. **[1]**
- A somatic mutation affecting control of cell division can contribute to cancer. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “germ-cell mutations are inherited” needs the condition that the mutated cell contributes to a gamete and zygote. Somatic mutations can spread through a cell lineage even though they aren’t passed to offspring._

## Q3. Explain mutation and natural selection. **[4]**

Explain why mutation is described as random but remains essential for evolution by natural selection.

### Mark scheme

- Mutations occur without regard to the needs of the organism / no known natural mechanism deliberately changes a selected base to produce a needed trait. **[1]**
- Mutations can occur throughout the genome, although mutation probabilities can differ among bases or regions. **[1]**
- Mutation is the original source of new alleles / genetic variation. **[1]**
- Natural selection acts on heritable variation and can increase the frequency of alleles that improve reproductive success in a particular environment. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “random” doesn’t mean that every site mutates equally. It means that a mutation’s occurrence isn’t directed toward a useful outcome._

## Q4. Explain the use of gene knockout. **[3]**

Researchers make one gene in a model organism inoperative. Explain how this could provide evidence about the function of the gene.

### Mark scheme

- The organism with the inoperative gene is compared with an otherwise similar control / organism with the functioning gene. **[1]**
- A phenotypic or biochemical difference is identified in the knockout organism. **[1]**
- The difference provides evidence that the gene contributes to the affected process or phenotype. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the missing function can’t be assumed from the gene’s name. The evidence comes from a controlled comparison between the knockout and a functioning version._

## Q5. Outline CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing. **[5]**

Outline how CRISPR-Cas9 can target and alter a selected DNA sequence.

### Mark scheme

- A guide RNA is produced with a sequence complementary to the target DNA. **[1]**
- The guide RNA associates with Cas9 / directs Cas9 to the matching DNA sequence. **[1]**
- The guide RNA binds to the complementary target sequence. **[1]**
- Cas9 acts as a nuclease and cuts the DNA. **[1]**
- Cellular DNA repair rejoins the cut and may disrupt the gene / a supplied repair template can allow a selected sequence change. **[1]**

_Examiner note: guide RNA and Cas9 have different roles. The guide supplies target specificity, while Cas9 cuts the DNA; reversing those roles loses marks._

## Q6. Suggest explanations for sequence conservation. **[4]**

A DNA sequence is very similar in distantly related species. Suggest two hypotheses that could explain why the sequence has been highly conserved.

### Mark scheme

- The sequence may have an important function / its gene product may have strict functional requirements. **[1]**
- Mutations in the sequence may reduce fitness and be removed by purifying selection. **[1]**
- The sequence may have a lower mutation rate than other regions. **[1]**
- Fewer new variants would therefore arise over evolutionary time. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “the sequence is important” identifies only the functional hypothesis. Full marks require the selection mechanism and the separate lower-mutation-rate hypothesis._
