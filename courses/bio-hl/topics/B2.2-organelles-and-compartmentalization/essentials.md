# B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Define an organelle as a discrete cell subunit adapted for a specific function, and classify the structures named by the syllabus as organelles or non-organelles.
- [ ] Explain how cell fractionation and ultracentrifugation made it possible to isolate organelles and investigate their functions.
- [ ] Explain how the nuclear envelope separates transcription from translation, allowing RNA to be modified before it reaches ribosomes.
- [ ] Explain how compartmentalization can concentrate enzymes and metabolites, increasing the efficiency of biochemical reactions.
- [ ] Explain how compartments separate incompatible or potentially damaging processes, using lysosomes and phagocytic vacuoles as examples.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how the double membrane, small intermembrane space, cristae and matrix adapt a mitochondrion for aerobic ATP production.
- [ ] Explain how thylakoid membranes, small thylakoid lumens and the stroma adapt a chloroplast for photosynthesis.
- [ ] Compare how mitochondria and chloroplasts use membranes, small compartments and enzyme-containing fluid regions.
- [ ] Explain the benefits of the double nuclear membrane, the need for nuclear pores and the breakdown of the nuclear envelope during mitosis and meiosis.
- [ ] Contrast proteins synthesized by free ribosomes with proteins synthesized by ribosomes bound to rough endoplasmic reticulum.
- [ ] Describe how the rough endoplasmic reticulum receives newly synthesized proteins and sends them onwards in vesicles.
- [ ] Describe how the Golgi apparatus receives, processes, sorts and packages proteins for secretion.
- [ ] Explain how transport vesicles bud from membranes, fuse with target membranes and use clathrin during vesicle formation.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organelle | A discrete subunit of a cell adapted to perform a specific function | A synonym for every cell structure; the cell wall, cytoskeleton and cytoplasm aren’t organelles in this syllabus |
| Compartmentalization | Division of a cell into regions where particular conditions and reactions can be maintained | Merely having several named structures in a diagram |
| Cell fractionation | Breaking open cells and separating their components so particular fractions can be studied | Viewing intact cells through a microscope |
| Ultracentrifugation | Spinning cell material at very high speeds to separate components according to properties including size and density | A process that identifies organelles from color alone |
| Nuclear envelope | The double membrane surrounding the nucleus | A solid wall; nuclear pores allow controlled exchange |
| Nuclear pore | A protein-lined opening through the nuclear envelope that controls movement between nucleus and cytoplasm | A tear caused by damage to the nucleus |
| Intermembrane space | The narrow compartment between the inner and outer mitochondrial membranes | The mitochondrial matrix inside the inner membrane |
| Cristae | Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane that increase its surface area | Folds of the outer mitochondrial membrane |
| Matrix | The fluid compartment enclosed by the inner mitochondrial membrane, containing Krebs cycle enzymes and substrates | The space between the two mitochondrial membranes |
| Thylakoid | A flattened membrane sac containing photosystems and other components of the light-dependent reactions | A stack of sacs; that stack is a granum |
| Thylakoid lumen | The small fluid-filled space inside a thylakoid | The stroma surrounding the thylakoids |
| Stroma | The chloroplast fluid containing enzymes and substrates of the Calvin cycle | The internal space of a thylakoid |
| Free ribosome | A ribosome in the cytosol that synthesizes proteins retained for use within the cell | A ribosome with a different basic structure from one bound to rough ER |
| Rough endoplasmic reticulum | A membrane system with bound ribosomes that receives proteins for intracellular transport or secretion | The site that carries out the final processing and sorting performed by the Golgi apparatus |
| Golgi apparatus | A stack of flattened membrane sacs that processes, sorts and packages proteins | The site where ribosomes translate mRNA |
| Vesicle | A small membrane-bound compartment used for transport, storage or isolation of substances | A permanent channel connecting every organelle |
| Clathrin | A coat protein that helps curve a membrane and form a budding vesicle | A membrane protein that remains as the vesicle’s permanent outer coat |
