# B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport — what to watch

> This topic has more moving parts than A1.1, so the temptation to watch three
> videos back to back is strong. Resist it. Watch one, then draw the membrane
> from memory before you watch anything else. The drawing is the point.

## Main

**Alex Lee — B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** (~20–25 min, may be split)

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@AlexLeeBiology>
- Find it: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Alex+Lee+IB+Biology+B2.1+membranes+transport>

Watch for:
- The moment the bilayer is explained as *spontaneous*. If you come away
  thinking something builds it, you have missed the central idea.
- The difference between facilitated diffusion and active transport, stated in
  terms of **gradient direction** and **ATP**. Those two variables separate
  every transport mechanism in this topic.
- Anything said about cholesterol. It gets one line and is worth a mark.

Pause at: the transport summary. Before playing on, write the four mechanisms
(simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis, active transport) and for
each one answer: protein needed? ATP needed? which way down the gradient?
That table is most of the topic.

## For the HL material

**Sodium–potassium pump and cotransport**

- Find it: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=sodium+potassium+pump+animation+3+sodium+2+potassium>
- Find it: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=sodium+glucose+cotransporter+secondary+active+transport>

Watch an **animation**, not a lecture, for these two. The pump's shape change
and the cotransporter's dependence on the sodium gradient are spatial ideas —
they are much cheaper to learn from movement than from a paragraph. This is the
one place in the topic where a second video genuinely earns its time.

## If that did not land

**Amoeba Sisters — Cell Transport**

- Find it: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Amoeba+Sisters+cell+transport>

Not HL depth, but if the passive/active distinction itself is the problem, this
sorts it out in ten minutes and you can return to Alex Lee afterwards.
