## Core

- [ ] Define a pathogen and identify viruses, bacteria, fungi and protists as groups containing human pathogens.
- [ ] Explain how intact skin and mucous membranes act as physical and chemical barriers to infection.
- [ ] Outline the clotting sequence from platelet activation and clotting factors to thrombin, fibrin and a sealed wound.
- [ ] Distinguish the innate immune system from the adaptive immune system by specificity, change during life and immune memory.
- [ ] Explain how phagocytes engulf and digest pathogens by phagocytosis.
- [ ] Explain how antigens, antigen-specific helper T-cells and B-cells cooperate to start antibody production.
- [ ] Explain how clonal expansion produces antibody-secreting plasma cells and long-lived memory cells.
- [ ] Compare primary and secondary immune responses and connect the difference to immunity.
- [ ] Explain how HIV is transmitted in body fluids and how infection of helper T-cells can lead to AIDS.
- [ ] Explain why antibiotics can act selectively against bacteria but can’t treat viral infections.
- [ ] Explain antibiotic resistance by natural selection, including how antibiotic use changes allele frequencies in bacterial populations.
- [ ] Define a zoonosis and illustrate different transmission routes using rabies, zoonotic tuberculosis, Japanese encephalitis and COVID-19.
- [ ] Explain how vaccines containing antigens or nucleic acids produce immunity without causing the disease.
- [ ] Explain how herd immunity slows transmission and indirectly protects people who aren’t immune.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pathogen | A disease-causing organism or infectious agent; the term is typically used for viruses, bacteria, fungi and protists | Every microorganism; many microorganisms are harmless or beneficial |
| Primary defense | A barrier that prevents pathogens from entering body tissues, such as skin or a mucous membrane | The primary adaptive immune response after first exposure to an antigen |
| Innate immunity | Defense against broad categories of pathogen that doesn’t become more specific through previous exposure | Immunity based on antigen-specific memory cells |
| Adaptive immunity | An antigen-specific response involving lymphocytes that can leave immune memory | A rapid, unchanged response to any foreign material |
| Phagocyte | A leucocyte that engulfs material by endocytosis and digests it in vesicles | A lymphocyte that secretes a specific antibody |
| Antigen | A molecule recognized as foreign that can trigger production of a specific antibody | The antibody that binds to it, or the entire pathogen |
| Antibody | A protein produced by plasma cells that binds specifically to a corresponding antigen | A cell that engulfs pathogens or a chemical that kills bacteria |
| Plasma cell | A differentiated B-lymphocyte that secretes large quantities of a specific antibody | The liquid part of blood |
| Memory cell | A long-lived antigen-specific lymphocyte retained after an immune response | A pathogen stored in the body to maintain immunity |
| Clonal expansion | Repeated mitosis of an activated lymphocyte, producing genetically identical cells with the same antigen specificity | A pathogen reproducing inside the host |
| Immunity | The capacity to eliminate a specific infection rapidly because suitable memory cells are retained | A guarantee that exposure or symptoms can never occur |
| HIV | Human immunodeficiency virus, which infects and destroys helper T-lymphocytes | AIDS itself |
| AIDS | Acquired immune deficiency syndrome caused by severe loss of immune function following HIV infection | The virus that can cause the syndrome |
| Antibiotic | A chemical that blocks a process in bacteria while having little or no effect on host cells | A treatment for viruses |
| Antibiotic resistance | An inherited ability of bacteria to survive an antibiotic that would kill or inhibit susceptible bacteria | A human body becoming used to an antibiotic |
| Zoonosis | An infectious disease that can transfer from another animal species to humans | Any disease found in both animals and humans without transfer between them |
| Vaccine | A preparation containing antigens or nucleic acids coding for antigens that stimulates immunity without causing the disease | A dose of ready-made antibodies or a medicine that cures an existing infection |
| Herd immunity | Reduced transmission when enough of a population is immune to a particular pathogen | Every member of the population becoming immune |
