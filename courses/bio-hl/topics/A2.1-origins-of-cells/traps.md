# A2.1 Origins of cells — where the marks go

## Saying Miller and Urey made life

**Commonly written:** "The Miller–Urey experiment created the first living cells from gases."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The experiment produced amino acids and other carbon compounds, not cells or self-replicating living systems.

**Scores instead:** "The experiment showed that carbon compounds could form abiotically from simpler substances under the tested conditions."

## Treating a carbon compound as proof of life

**Commonly written:** "Finding an organic molecule proves that an organism made it."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Carbon compounds can form through abiotic reactions, so their presence alone doesn’t identify a biological source.

**Scores instead:** "A carbon compound may be consistent with life, but its context and possible abiotic formation must also be evaluated."

## Calling every membrane vesicle living

**Commonly written:** "Once fatty acids formed a vesicle, the vesicle became a cell."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A membrane provides compartmentalization but doesn’t supply metabolism, heredity or self-replication.

**Scores instead:** "Fatty-acid vesicles could maintain distinct internal chemistry, making them a proposed intermediate rather than complete living cells."

## Saying RNA came first because it is single stranded

**Commonly written:** "RNA was the first genetic material because it has only one strand."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Strand number doesn’t establish the required roles. The hypothesis depends on RNA storing information, being copied and catalyzing reactions.

**Scores instead:** "RNA may have preceded DNA because it can store sequence information and some RNA molecules can catalyze reactions."

## Describing the ribosome’s catalyst as protein

**Commonly written:** "A protein enzyme in the ribosome forms the peptide bonds."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Peptide bond formation at the ribosome’s catalytic center is carried out by ribosomal RNA.

**Scores instead:** "Ribosomal RNA catalyzes peptide bond formation, providing a modern example of a ribozyme."

## Calling LUCA the first organism

**Commonly written:** "LUCA was the first living cell on Earth."

**Why it doesn’t score:** LUCA was the most recent common ancestor of surviving life; older cells and extinct lineages may have preceded it.

**Scores instead:** "LUCA was the ancestral population from which all organisms alive today descended, not necessarily the first life."

## Treating the oldest fossil as the date life began

**Commonly written:** "Life began when the oldest known stromatolite formed."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The oldest accepted evidence gives a minimum age; the organisms that produced it must already have evolved.

**Scores instead:** "A dated stromatolite shows that cellular life existed by that time, so life’s origin must have occurred earlier."

## Saying hydrothermal vents are proven as LUCA’s habitat

**Commonly written:** "Fossils at vents prove that LUCA lived in a hydrothermal vent."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Vent fossils show ancient life there, while genomic reconstruction supports a compatible metabolism; neither identifies LUCA directly.

**Scores instead:** "Ancient vent fossils and reconstructed conserved genes together support, but don’t prove, a hydrothermal-vent habitat for LUCA."
