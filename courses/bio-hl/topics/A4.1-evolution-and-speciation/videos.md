# A4.1 Evolution and speciation — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain speciation from memory. A second explanation of the same material builds recognition, which is the problem you’re trying to get past.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — A4.1 Evolution and speciation**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+A4.1+Evolution+and+speciation>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation made for A4.1.

**Watch for:**

- What makes a population-level change evolution, rather than a change acquired by one organism.
- How molecular sequences, selective breeding and homologous structures provide different kinds of evidence.
- Why reproductive isolation has to come before two populations can diverge independently.
- How polyploidy can make a plant reproductively isolated in one generation.

**Pause at:** the first explanation of speciation. Write a four-link chain using **barrier**, **gene flow**, **selection** and **reproductive isolation**. Say the chain aloud without looking at the video.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Polyploid speciation depends on the spatial problem of chromosomes pairing during meiosis, so an **animation** earns one extra watch.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=polyploidy+speciation+meiosis+animation>

After watching, draw a diploid parent, a polyploid offspring and the chromosomes in their hybrid. Explain why unmatched chromosomes can disrupt meiosis and prevent production of functional gametes.
