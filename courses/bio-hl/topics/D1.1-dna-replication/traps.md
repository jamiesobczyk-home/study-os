# D1.1 DNA replication — where the marks go

## Treating semi-conservative as half a sequence

**Commonly written:** "Each new DNA molecule gets half of the original genetic information."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Each product has the complete base sequence. What is divided between the products is the two parental strands.

**Scores instead:** "Each product DNA molecule contains one complete parental strand and one newly synthesized complementary strand."

## Giving helicase the polymerase's job

**Commonly written:** "Helicase adds complementary nucleotides after unzipping the DNA."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Helicase separates the parental strands. DNA polymerase adds complementary nucleotides to the growing strands.

**Scores instead:** "Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds to separate the strands, and DNA polymerase builds new complementary strands."

## Saying Taq DNA polymerase separates PCR strands

**Commonly written:** "Taq DNA polymerase unzips the DNA during denaturation."

**Why it doesn’t score:** High temperature separates the strands during denaturation. Taq DNA polymerase extends primers during the extension stage.

**Scores instead:** "Heating separates the DNA strands, and heat-stable Taq DNA polymerase extends the annealed primers."

## Reversing the direction of lagging-strand synthesis

**Commonly written:** "The lagging strand is synthesized 3′ to 5′."

**Why it doesn’t score:** All new DNA is synthesized 5′ to 3′. The lagging strand solves the direction problem by forming separate Okazaki fragments.

**Scores instead:** "The lagging strand is synthesized discontinuously as Okazaki fragments, each of which grows 5′ to 3′."

## Making ligase replace the primers

**Commonly written:** "DNA ligase removes the RNA primers and fills the gaps with DNA."

**Why it doesn’t score:** DNA polymerase I removes and replaces the primers. DNA ligase seals the remaining nicks in the sugar–phosphate backbone.

**Scores instead:** "DNA polymerase I replaces RNA primers with DNA, and DNA ligase seals the nicks between adjacent DNA sections."

## Saying large fragments travel farther through a gel

**Commonly written:** "Large DNA fragments move farther because they have more negative charge."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Smaller fragments pass through the gel matrix more easily and therefore move farther in the same time.

**Scores instead:** "DNA moves toward the positive electrode, and smaller fragments travel farther through the gel than larger fragments."

## Treating a DNA profile match as absolute proof

**Commonly written:** "Matching bands prove that the two samples came from the same person."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A match supports a conclusion but can occur by chance. Its reliability depends partly on how many independent markers were tested.

**Scores instead:** "A match across many independent genetic markers provides stronger evidence because a coincidental match becomes less probable."

## Claiming proofreading prevents every mutation

**Commonly written:** "DNA polymerase III corrects every error, so replication can't cause mutations."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Proofreading lowers the error frequency but isn't perfect. An uncorrected change can remain in the replicated DNA.

**Scores instead:** "DNA polymerase III removes mismatched terminal nucleotides, greatly reducing but not eliminating replication errors."
