# D3.1 Reproduction — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Compare sexual and asexual reproduction, including how meiosis and fertilization affect genetic variation and chromosome number.
- [ ] Explain how differences in the size, number, mobility and food reserves of male and female gametes lead to different reproductive strategies.
- [ ] Draw and annotate the male-typical and female-typical human reproductive systems with the functions of their main structures.
- [ ] Explain the ovarian and uterine cycles using FSH, LH, oestradiol, progesterone, ovulation, the corpus luteum and positive and negative feedback.
- [ ] Sequence fertilization in humans and explain how IVF hormones suspend the usual cycle and induce superovulation.
- [ ] Explain sexual reproduction in flowering plants from gamete production and pollination through pollen-tube growth, fertilization and embryo formation.
- [ ] Draw and annotate an insect-pollinated flower, linking each reproductive structure and adaptation to its function.
- [ ] Explain how differences in maturation time, separate sexes and pollen transfer promote cross-pollination, and how self-incompatibility reduces inbreeding.
- [ ] Distinguish pollination from seed dispersal and explain germination using embryo growth and the mobilization of food reserves.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how increased GnRH secretion starts puberty through FSH, LH and increased production of steroid sex hormones.
- [ ] Compare spermatogenesis and oogenesis using mitosis, growth, two meiotic divisions, differentiation, cytokinesis and the number and size of functional gametes.
- [ ] Explain how the acrosome reaction permits fertilization and how the cortical reaction prevents polyspermy.
- [ ] Explain how mitotic divisions produce a blastocyst and how the blastocyst implants in the endometrium.
- [ ] Explain how hCG is detected in a pregnancy test and how placental villi support exchange between maternal and fetal blood.
- [ ] Explain how progesterone maintains pregnancy and how falling progesterone and positive feedback involving oxytocin control childbirth.
- [ ] Explain why observational studies and randomized controlled trials produced different conclusions about HRT and coronary heart disease.

## Links to other topics

- **D2.1 Cell and nuclear division** — meiosis produces haploid gametes, while mitosis supports asexual reproduction, gamete formation and embryo growth. Knowing which division occurs at each stage prevents several common errors.
- **D3.2 Inheritance** — meiosis and random fertilization create new allele combinations. D3.1 supplies the reproductive events behind the genetic crosses in D3.2.
- **C2.1 Chemical signalling** — GnRH, FSH, LH, steroid hormones, hCG and oxytocin are signaling molecules. Reproduction gives you named examples of feedback and communication between organs.
- **B3.2 Transport** — the placenta exchanges respiratory gases, nutrients and wastes between two circulatory systems without the blood normally mixing.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — sexual reproduction generates variation, while cross-pollination and self-incompatibility help preserve it. That variation can become the raw material for natural selection.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asexual reproduction | Production of offspring from one parent without fusion of gametes | Reproduction that always produces perfectly identical phenotypes; environmental differences can still affect them |
| Sexual reproduction | Production of offspring involving meiosis and fusion of gametes | Reproduction that requires two separate organisms in every species; one hermaphroditic plant can produce both gamete types |
| Fertilization | Fusion of male and female gametes, restoring the diploid chromosome number | Pollination, which only transfers pollen |
| Gamete | A haploid reproductive cell that can fuse with another gamete | A diploid body cell or an entire pollen grain |
| Ovulation | Release of an egg cell from an ovarian follicle | Menstruation or fertilization |
| Menstruation | Shedding of part of the endometrium following a fall in ovarian hormones | The release of an egg from an ovary |
| Corpus luteum | Endocrine tissue formed from the follicle after ovulation that secretes progesterone | The egg or the early embryo |
| Superovulation | Maturation of several ovarian follicles following fertility-hormone treatment | The release of one egg during an untreated cycle |
| Pollination | Transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma | Fusion of plant gametes or movement of seeds |
| Cross-pollination | Transfer of pollen between different plants of the same species | Transfer of pollen between flowers on the same plant |
| Self-incompatibility | Genetic recognition that prevents pollen from the same plant or genotype from completing fertilization | Physical separation of male and female flowers |
| Seed dispersal | Movement of seeds away from the parent plant | Transfer of pollen before fertilization |
| Germination | Resumption of embryo growth using mobilized food reserves | The first appearance of a seed after fertilization |
| Spermatogenesis | Production and differentiation of haploid sperm cells | Production of semen by accessory glands |
| Oogenesis | Production of a large haploid egg and polar bodies by unequal cell divisions | Ovulation, which releases an egg |
| Polyspermy | Entry of more than one sperm into an egg | The arrival of several sperm near an egg |
| Blastocyst | A hollow early embryonic structure containing an inner cell mass and an outer cell layer | An unfertilized egg or the placenta |
| Implantation | Attachment and embedding of the blastocyst in the endometrium | Fertilization in the oviduct |
| hCG | Human chorionic gonadotropin, secreted by the embryo or developing placenta and detected in pregnancy tests | A hormone that causes ovulation in an untreated menstrual cycle |
| Placenta | A temporary organ that supports exchange and hormone secretion during pregnancy | A place where maternal and fetal blood normally mix |
| Hormone replacement therapy | Treatment using hormones, commonly including estrogen with or without a progestogen | Fertility treatment designed to induce superovulation |
