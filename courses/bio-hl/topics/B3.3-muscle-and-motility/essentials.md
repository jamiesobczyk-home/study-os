- [ ] Describe a motor unit, including its motor neuron, muscle fibres and neuromuscular junctions.
- [ ] Explain how recruitment of different numbers and sizes of motor units changes the force and precision of a contraction.

- [ ] Explain movement at the hip joint using the femur, pelvis, cartilage, synovial fluid, ligaments, tendons and muscles.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motility | The ability of an organism or part of an organism to produce movement | Locomotion only; sessile organisms can move without changing location |
| Locomotion | Movement of the whole organism from one place to another | Any movement inside an organism |
| Sarcomere | The repeating contractile unit of a myofibril between two Z lines | An entire muscle fibre or an individual protein filament |
| Actin filament | A thin filament that slides toward the center of a sarcomere during contraction | A filament that becomes shorter |
| Myosin filament | A thick filament with heads that form cross-bridges with actin | A stationary anchor that never changes its interaction with actin |
| Cross-bridge | A temporary attachment between a myosin head and actin | A permanent bond between the two filaments |
| Power stroke | The movement of an attached myosin head that pulls actin toward the sarcomere’s center | Shortening of the actin or myosin filament |
| Titin | An elastic sarcomere protein that aids recoil and limits overstretching | The motor protein that performs the power stroke |
| Antagonistic pair | Two muscles that produce opposite movements, with one contracting while the other lengthens | Two muscles that always contract equally in the same direction |
| Motor unit | One motor neuron and all the skeletal muscle fibres it controls | One motor neuron connected to one fibre only |
| Neuromuscular junction | The synapse between a motor neuron and a muscle fibre | The connection between a tendon and a bone |
| Tendon | Tough connective tissue joining muscle to bone | A ligament joining bone to bone |
| Ligament | Tough connective tissue joining bone to bone and stabilizing a joint | A tendon transmitting force from muscle |
| Synovial joint | A freely movable joint with a fluid-filled cavity between articulating bones | A fused or immovable connection between bones |
| Range of motion | The angle or extent through which a joint can move in one or more dimensions | The force produced by the muscles around the joint |
| Fluke | A marine mammal’s horizontal tail structure that produces propulsion by moving up and down | A fish’s vertical tail fin moving mainly from side to side |
