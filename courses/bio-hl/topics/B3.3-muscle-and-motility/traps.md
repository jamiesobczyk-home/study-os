# B3.3 Muscle and motility — where the marks go

## Saying the filaments become shorter

**Commonly written:** “Actin and myosin contract, making the sarcomere shorter.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Actin and myosin keep the same length. The sarcomere shortens because their overlap increases as they slide past one another.

**Scores instead:** “Myosin heads pull actin toward the centre of the sarcomere, increasing filament overlap and bringing the Z lines closer together.”

## Giving ATP only one job

**Commonly written:** “ATP provides energy for the power stroke.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The cross-bridge cycle also requires ATP binding to detach myosin from actin and ATP hydrolysis to re-energize the head.

**Scores instead:** “ATP binding detaches myosin from actin, and ATP hydrolysis re-energizes and repositions the myosin head for another cycle.”

## Saying muscles push bones

**Commonly written:** “The relaxed muscle pushes the bone back to its original position.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Muscle tissue produces force by contracting, so reversal requires contraction of an antagonistic muscle rather than a push from the relaxing muscle.

**Scores instead:** “The antagonistic muscle contracts and pulls the bone in the opposite direction while the first muscle relaxes and lengthens.”

## Treating titin as a motor protein

**Commonly written:** “Titin uses ATP to pull actin during contraction.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Myosin performs ATP-dependent cross-bridge cycling. Titin stores elastic energy, assists recoil and limits overstretching.

**Scores instead:** “When a sarcomere is stretched, titin stores elastic potential energy and helps the sarcomere recoil toward its resting length.”

## Reversing tendons and ligaments

**Commonly written:** “Ligaments attach muscles to bones, while tendons hold the bones together.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The attachments are reversed. Tendons transmit muscle force to bone, while ligaments connect and stabilize bones across a joint.

**Scores instead:** “Tendons join muscle to bone, while ligaments join bone to bone and help stabilize the joint.”

## Defining a motor unit as one fibre

**Commonly written:** “A motor unit is one motor neuron connected to one muscle fibre.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** One motor neuron branches and can control multiple muscle fibres, all of which belong to the same motor unit.

**Scores instead:** “A motor unit consists of one motor neuron and all the skeletal muscle fibres it controls.”

## Calling every movement locomotion

**Commonly written:** “A plant growing toward light is locomotion.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Locomotion changes the location of the whole organism. Directional plant growth is movement, but the rooted plant remains in place.

**Scores instead:** “A shoot growing toward light shows movement in a sessile organism, but it isn’t locomotion because the whole plant doesn’t change location.”

## Giving fish features to a whale

**Commonly written:** “A whale swims by moving its vertical tail fin from side to side.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Whales have horizontal tail flukes that generate propulsion by moving up and down.

**Scores instead:** “A whale’s horizontal tail flukes move up and down to produce forward thrust.”
