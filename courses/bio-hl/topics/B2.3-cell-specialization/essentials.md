# B2.3 Cell specialization — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how mitosis produces unspecialized cells after fertilization, then explain how morphogen concentration gradients cause different patterns of gene expression and differentiation.
- [ ] State the two defining properties of stem cells: self-renewal and the capacity to differentiate.
- [ ] Explain how stem cell niches in bone marrow and hair follicles maintain stem cells or promote their proliferation and differentiation.
- [ ] Distinguish totipotent, pluripotent and multipotent stem cells by the range of cell types each can produce.
- [ ] Explain how the sizes and shapes of human sperm, eggs, erythrocytes, leukocytes, neurons and striated muscle fibres suit different functions.
- [ ] Calculate surface area-to-volume ratios for model cells and explain why this ratio decreases as a cell grows.
- [ ] Explain why surface area-to-volume ratio constrains cell size by linking membrane area to exchange and cell volume to metabolic demand.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how flattening, microvilli and membrane invagination increase surface area-to-volume ratio, using erythrocytes and proximal convoluted tubule cells as examples.
- [ ] Compare the adaptations and functions of type I and type II pneumocytes in alveoli.
- [ ] Compare cardiac muscle cells with striated skeletal muscle fibres, including their myofibrils, branching, length and number of nuclei.
- [ ] Discuss whether a striated skeletal muscle fibre should be described as one cell.
- [ ] Compare the adaptations of human sperm and egg cells and connect each structure to its role in fertilization and early development.

## Links to other topics

- **D2.2 Gene expression** — differentiation depends on different genes being expressed in cells that usually contain the same genome. D2.2 supplies the molecular mechanism behind specialization.
- **D2.1 Cell and nuclear division** — mitosis increases cell number during embryonic development and renews cells in adult tissues. It explains how one zygote can produce many genetically similar cells before they differentiate.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — exchange happens across plasma membranes. Membrane transport explains why surface area becomes a limit and why microvilli help absorption.
- **B3.1 Gas exchange** — type I pneumocytes provide a short diffusion distance, while type II pneumocytes secrete surfactant. B2.3 supplies the cellular adaptations behind alveolar function.
- **B3.2 Transport** — erythrocyte shape is an example of specialization for oxygen transport, and proximal convoluted tubule cells are specialized for reabsorption.
- **B3.3 Muscle and motility** — myofibrils allow contraction. B2.3 focuses on how cardiac cells and skeletal muscle fibres are built differently for their jobs.
- **D3.1 Reproduction** — the contrasting structures of sperm and eggs prepare them for fertilization. D3.1 follows what happens to the gametes and zygote.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell specialization | The development of structural and functional features suited to a particular role | Every cell losing most of its genes |
| Differentiation | The process by which an unspecialized cell develops into a specialized cell through changes in gene expression | Mutation of the cell into a new genetic type |
| Morphogen | A signaling substance whose concentration helps determine gene expression and cell fate during development | A gene or a substance that changes the DNA sequence |
| Stem cell | An unspecialized cell capable of self-renewal and differentiation | Any cell that can undergo one mitotic division |
| Self-renewal | Division that maintains a supply of stem cells | Differentiation into mature cell types |
| Stem cell niche | The local cellular and chemical environment that maintains stem cells or regulates their proliferation and differentiation | A type of stem cell or an empty cavity in a tissue |
| Totipotent | Able to produce all embryonic and extraembryonic cell types needed to form a whole organism | Able to produce only the body’s specialized cells |
| Pluripotent | Able to produce cell types from all three embryonic germ layers, but not a whole organism because extraembryonic tissues can’t all be formed | Restricted to one family of related cells |
| Multipotent | Able to produce a limited range of related cell types | Able to produce every cell type in the body |
| Surface area-to-volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume; a measure of exchange area relative to the amount of cell contents | The total surface area or volume by itself |
| Microvillus | A microscopic projection of the plasma membrane that increases surface area | A villus, which is a larger multicellular projection |
| Pneumocyte | An epithelial cell forming part of the alveolar lining | Any cell found in lung tissue |
| Lamellar body | A secretory vesicle in a type II pneumocyte that stores and releases surfactant | An organelle that carries out aerobic respiration |
| Surfactant | A secretion that reduces surface tension at the alveolar air–liquid boundary | Mucus or a substance that increases diffusion distance |
| Myofibril | A contractile structure made from organized protein filaments inside a muscle cell or fibre | A complete muscle fibre or an individual muscle |
| Striated skeletal muscle fibre | A long, unbranched, multinucleate contractile unit formed by fusion of precursor cells | A chain of separate short cells like cardiac muscle tissue |
