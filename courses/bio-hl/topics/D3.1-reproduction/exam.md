# D3.1 Reproduction — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If a point only looked familiar after the reveal, it wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Compare and contrast sexual and asexual reproduction. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Asexual reproduction involves one parent / no fusion of gametes, whereas sexual reproduction involves fusion of gametes. **[1]**
- Asexual reproduction normally uses mitosis, whereas sexual reproduction includes meiosis and fertilization. **[1]**
- Asexual reproduction produces genetically identical offspring / clones, whereas sexual reproduction produces new combinations of alleles. **[1]**
- Asexual reproduction can produce many offspring rapidly from an individual adapted to an existing environment. **[1]**
- Sexual reproduction produces variation that may allow adaptation to environmental change. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “sexual reproduction creates variation” needs a mechanism. Meiosis breaks up parental allele combinations, and fertilization combines alleles from two gametes._

## Q2. Explain the hormonal regulation of the ovarian and uterine cycles from follicle development to menstruation. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- FSH from the anterior pituitary stimulates follicle development / oestradiol secretion. **[1]**
- Oestradiol stimulates repair and thickening of the endometrium. **[1]**
- Sustained high oestradiol causes positive feedback / stimulates an LH surge. **[1]**
- The LH surge causes ovulation / formation of the corpus luteum. **[1]**
- Progesterone from the corpus luteum maintains the endometrium and inhibits FSH/LH by negative feedback. **[1]**
- Degeneration of the corpus luteum causes progesterone and oestradiol to fall, leading to menstruation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming all four hormones earns little without the causal sequence. Full marks connect each hormone to its source or effect and include both positive and negative feedback._

## Q3. Explain sexual reproduction in a flowering plant, beginning with pollination and ending with embryo formation. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Pollination transfers pollen from an anther to a compatible stigma. **[1]**
- A pollen grain germinates / develops a pollen tube on the stigma. **[1]**
- The pollen tube grows through the style toward an ovule. **[1]**
- A male gamete/nucleus travels through the pollen tube and fuses with the female gamete in the ovule. **[1]**
- Fertilization produces a zygote that develops into an embryo within a seed. **[1]**

_Examiner note: pollination and fertilization are separate events. An answer that says pollen “fertilizes the stigma” loses both the transfer stage and the fusion of gametes inside the ovule._

## Q4. Compare spermatogenesis and oogenesis in humans. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both include mitosis of diploid precursor cells / cell growth before meiosis. **[1]**
- Both include two meiotic divisions and produce haploid cells. **[1]**
- Spermatogenesis has approximately equal cytokinesis, whereas oogenesis has unequal cytokinesis. **[1]**
- One primary spermatocyte produces four functional sperm, whereas one primary oocyte produces one functional egg and polar bodies. **[1]**
- Sperm are small and contain little cytoplasm, whereas the egg is large and retains most of the cytoplasm. **[1]**
- Spermatids differentiate into motile sperm with specialized structures, whereas the egg remains non-motile with reserves for early development. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “both use meiosis” is one similarity, not a comparison worth several marks. The high-value distinction is how unequal cytokinesis changes the number and size of functional gametes._

## Q5. Explain how fertilization, prevention of polyspermy and implantation occur in humans. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- The acrosome reaction releases enzymes that permit penetration of the zona pellucida. **[1]**
- The sperm and egg cell membranes fuse / the sperm nucleus enters the egg. **[1]**
- Fusion triggers the cortical reaction / release of cortical granule contents. **[1]**
- The zona pellucida is modified or hardened, preventing entry of additional sperm. **[1]**
- The zygote undergoes mitotic divisions and develops into a blastocyst. **[1]**
- The blastocyst attaches to and embeds in the endometrium. **[1]**

_Examiner note: polyspermy means entry of more than one sperm, not the presence of several sperm around the egg. The cortical reaction must be linked to a changed zona pellucida._

## Q6. Explain how evidence changed the conclusion about HRT and coronary heart disease. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Early observational studies found a correlation between HRT use and lower incidence of coronary heart disease. **[1]**
- HRT users tended to have higher socioeconomic status / socioeconomic status was a confounding variable associated with lower coronary heart disease risk. **[1]**
- Randomized controlled trials reduce systematic differences between treatment and control groups / provide stronger evidence about causation. **[1]**
- Randomized controlled trials found a small increase in coronary heart disease risk with HRT, so the earlier correlation didn't show a protective causal effect. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the answer must name the confounding variable and explain why random allocation changes the strength of the causal claim. Saying “the later study was better” doesn’t identify either point._
