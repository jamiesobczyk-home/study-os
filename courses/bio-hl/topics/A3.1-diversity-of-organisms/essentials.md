# A3.1 Diversity of organisms — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how variation within and between species provides the patterns used to identify, name and classify organisms.
- [ ] Distinguish the morphological species concept from the biological species concept, and explain why neither definition works in every case.
- [ ] Write and recognize a correctly formatted binomial name, and explain why scientists use this naming system.
- [ ] Explain why gradual divergence during speciation can make the boundary between a population and a species difficult to place.
- [ ] Compare chromosome numbers between species, explain why diploid chromosome numbers are even, and recall that humans normally have 46 while chimpanzees have 48.
- [ ] Distinguish a karyotype from a karyogram, pair chromosomes using length, banding pattern and centromere position, and identify human sex chromosomes.
- [ ] Evaluate the chromosome-count, sequence, telomere and centromere evidence that human chromosome 2 arose by fusion in a shared human ancestor.
- [ ] Explain how members of one species show both genomic unity and genomic diversity, including variation caused by single-nucleotide polymorphisms.
- [ ] Compare genome size and base sequence within and between species, and explain why genome size isn’t a reliable measure of organism complexity.
- [ ] Outline current and potential uses of whole genome sequencing, including research into evolutionary relationships and personalized medicine.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain why the biological species concept is difficult to apply to asexually reproducing organisms and bacteria that exchange DNA by horizontal gene transfer.
- [ ] Explain how meiosis and fertilization maintain chromosome number within a species, and why a mule produced by a horse and donkey is usually sterile.
- [ ] Use and construct a dichotomous key from paired, contrasting statements based on observable traits.
- [ ] Explain how DNA barcodes identify species from environmental DNA, then evaluate what this method can and can’t reveal about a habitat.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variation | Differences in traits among organisms | A claim that members of a species have nothing in common |
| Species | A group defined using agreed evidence, such as shared traits or the ability to interbreed and produce fertile offspring | A category with one definition that works for every organism |
| Morphological species concept | Classification of organisms into species using shared observable traits | Proof that similar-looking organisms can interbreed |
| Biological species concept | A species is a group whose members can interbreed and produce fertile offspring | Any two organisms that can mate or produce a hybrid |
| Population | Members of the same species living in the same area at the same time | A different species whenever it becomes geographically isolated |
| Speciation | The splitting of one species into two or more species | A sudden change that always gives a sharp boundary |
| Binomial name | A two-part scientific name made from the genus and specific epithet | A common name or a two-word label with both words capitalized |
| Karyotype | The number and appearance of the chromosomes in a cell or organism | The arranged image on which the chromosomes are displayed |
| Karyogram | An image of chromosomes arranged in homologous pairs and by recognizable features | The chromosome characteristics themselves |
| Genome | All the genetic information of an organism | Only its protein-coding genes |
| SNP | A single-nucleotide polymorphism, which is variation at one nucleotide position in a population | Any mutation involving a whole chromosome |
| Whole genome sequencing | Determining the order of nucleotides across an organism’s genome | Counting chromosomes or sequencing one short barcode alone |
| Horizontal gene transfer | Transfer of genetic material between organisms without parent-to-offspring reproduction | Inheritance of alleles from a parent |
| Dichotomous key | An identification tool that presents a sequence of two contrasting choices | An evolutionary tree showing common ancestry |
| DNA barcode | A short, standardized genetic sequence used to identify a species by comparison with a reference database | The complete genome of the organism |
| Environmental DNA | DNA collected from material organisms have released into their surroundings | DNA that proves the source organism is alive or shows its population size |
