# B1.2 Proteins

- **Theme:** B — Form and function
- **Level:** Molecules
- **Level of study:** SL and HL — but **most of this topic is HL**
- **Status:** ready

## The one-sentence version

Twenty building blocks, joined in any order and any length, fold into shapes
determined entirely by that order — and the shape is what does the job, which
is why heating a protein or shifting its pH destroys the function without
breaking a single link in the chain.

## Why this topic is worth getting right

Look at the split before you start. Of the twelve syllabus statements in B1.2,
**seven are HL only** — everything from R-group chemistry through primary,
secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure to fibrous versus globular. SL
stops at amino acid structure, condensation, diet and denaturation.

That matters for two reasons. It means this topic is much larger for you than
it looks in a shared textbook chapter. And it means the HL structural material
is where the marks are, so the folding levels are worth more of your time than
the amino acid diagram.

It also pays off repeatedly. Enzymes are proteins, so C1.1 is this topic
applied. Membrane proteins in B2.1 are this topic applied. Protein synthesis in
D1.2 is this topic's primary structure being built. Get the bonding right once
here and three later topics get easier.

## In your study guide

Allott, _Biology Study Guide_ (2023), **section B1.2, pp. 14–17**. Read it
*after* the capture sheet, never before — it’s condensed enough to make a fast
check of what you left out, and a slow way to meet the topic for the first time.
