# A1.2 Nucleic acids — what to watch

Watch **one** explanation, then close it and draw the strands from memory. Watching the same idea twice can make it familiar without making it available when you need to produce it.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science (Alex Lee) — A1.2 Nucleic acids**

* Channel: [https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience](https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience)
* Search: [https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+IB+Biology+A1.2+nucleic+acids](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+IB+Biology+A1.2+nucleic+acids)

**Watch for:**

* Where are the phosphate, sugar and base on one nucleotide?
* Which bonds join nucleotides along one strand, and which bonds join the two DNA strands?
* Why does each DNA strand run in the opposite direction to the other?
* How does one strand's base order determine the complementary strand's order?

**Pause at:** the first double-stranded DNA diagram. Hide its labels and draw three paired nucleotides. Mark both 5′ and 3′ ends, circle the sugar–phosphate backbones, then add A–T and G–C between them.

## Worth it once, not for revision

An **animation** of DNA wrapping around histones earns one extra watch because the nucleosome is spatial. You need to see which part winds around the core and which DNA connects neighboring nucleosomes.

* Search: [https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=DNA+nucleosome+histone+octamer+linker+DNA+animation](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=DNA+nucleosome+histone+octamer+linker+DNA+animation)

Afterward, sketch one eight-histone core with DNA around it and linker DNA leaving on both sides. Don't use the animation as a replacement for that sketch.
