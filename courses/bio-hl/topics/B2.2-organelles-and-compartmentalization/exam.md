# B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If the answer only looked familiar after the reveal, it wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Distinguish organelles from other cell structures. **[3]**

Define an organelle and classify the cell wall, ribosome and plasma membrane according to the definition used in this syllabus.

### Mark scheme

- An organelle is a discrete subunit of a cell adapted to perform a specific function. **[1]**
- Ribosomes and the plasma membrane are classified as organelles. **[1]**
- The cell wall is not classified as an organelle. **[1]**

_Examiner note: defining organelles as "membrane-bound structures" prevents the ribosome mark and doesn’t match the definition required by this syllabus._

## Q2. Explain the advantages of compartmentalization in cells. **[4]**

Explain two advantages of compartmentalization, including one example involving lysosomes.

### Mark scheme

- Enzymes and metabolites can be concentrated within a small region / frequency of enzyme-substrate collisions can be increased. **[1]**
- Different conditions can be maintained for particular biochemical processes. **[1]**
- Incompatible / potentially damaging biochemical processes can be separated from the cytosol. **[1]**
- Lysosomes fuse with phagocytic vacuoles so hydrolytic enzymes digest engulfed material inside an isolated compartment. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "organelles make reactions more efficient" earns little without the mechanism. Full marks require concentration or controlled conditions and a specific explanation of isolated digestion._

## Q3. Explain adaptations of a mitochondrion for ATP production. **[5]**

Explain how four structural features of a mitochondrion support aerobic ATP production.

### Mark scheme

- A double membrane creates a separate intermembrane compartment. **[1]**
- The small volume of the intermembrane space allows rapid establishment of a steep proton concentration gradient. **[1]**
- Cristae provide a large inner-membrane surface area. **[1]**
- The inner membrane contains electron transport chains and ATP synthase. **[1]**
- The matrix compartmentalizes enzymes and substrates of the Krebs cycle. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "cristae make more ATP" is a near miss. The answer must connect their increased membrane area to electron transport chains or ATP synthase._

## Q4. Compare and contrast the compartmentalization of mitochondria and chloroplasts. **[6]**

Compare how the structures of mitochondria and chloroplasts support energy conversion.

### Mark scheme

- Both contain extensive internal membranes that provide a large surface area for electron transport / ATP synthase. **[1]**
- Cristae are folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane, whereas thylakoids are the internal photosynthetic membranes of chloroplasts. **[1]**
- Both contain a small compartment in which protons accumulate / both establish proton gradients. **[1]**
- Protons accumulate in the mitochondrial intermembrane space, whereas they accumulate in the thylakoid lumen of chloroplasts. **[1]**
- The mitochondrial matrix contains enzymes and substrates of the Krebs cycle. **[1]**
- The chloroplast stroma contains enzymes and substrates of the Calvin cycle. **[1]**

_Examiner note: two separate descriptions don’t fully answer "compare and contrast." Paired statements using "both" and "whereas" make the similarities and differences explicit._

## Q5. Explain the separation of transcription and translation in eukaryotic cells. **[4]**

Explain how the nuclear envelope separates the two processes and why that separation is useful.

### Mark scheme

- Transcription occurs in the nucleus, whereas translation occurs on ribosomes outside the nucleus. **[1]**
- The nuclear envelope prevents newly transcribed RNA from meeting cytoplasmic ribosomes immediately. **[1]**
- Post-transcriptional modification / processing of mRNA can occur before translation. **[1]**
- Processed mRNA leaves the nucleus through nuclear pores. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the nucleus "controls the cell" doesn’t answer the question. Full marks require the locations, the delayed contact with ribosomes and the opportunity for mRNA processing._

## Q6. Outline the pathway followed by a secreted protein. **[6]**

Outline the movement of a protein from its synthesis to its release from the cell.

### Mark scheme

- The protein is synthesized by a ribosome bound to rough endoplasmic reticulum. **[1]**
- The protein enters the rough ER / is processed or folded in the rough ER. **[1]**
- A transport vesicle buds from the rough ER and carries the protein to the Golgi apparatus. **[1]**
- The Golgi apparatus processes and sorts the protein. **[1]**
- The protein is packaged into a secretory vesicle. **[1]**
- The secretory vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane and releases the protein by exocytosis. **[1]**

_Examiner note: rough ER, Golgi and vesicle names alone don’t establish a route. The stages must be in order, and membrane fusion at the Golgi or plasma membrane must be distinguished from protein passage through an open channel._
