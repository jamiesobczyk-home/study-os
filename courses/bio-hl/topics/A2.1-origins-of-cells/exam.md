# A2.1 Origins of cells — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that felt familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain prebiotic formation of carbon compounds. **[4]**

Explain how conditions on early Earth could have allowed carbon compounds to form through processes that rarely occur on Earth today.

### Mark scheme

- The early atmosphere lacked free oxygen / was anaerobic. **[1]**
- The absence of oxygen resulted in little or no ozone, allowing greater ultraviolet radiation to reach Earth’s surface. **[1]**
- Higher concentrations of carbon dioxide and methane contributed to higher temperatures. **[1]**
- Ultraviolet radiation / heat / electrical energy could drive abiotic chemical reactions producing carbon compounds. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming gases earns limited credit. Full marks require links from atmospheric conditions to energy availability and abiotic chemical reactions._

## Q2. Evaluate the Miller–Urey experiment. **[5]**

Evaluate the Miller–Urey experiment as evidence for the origin of carbon compounds on prebiotic Earth.

### Mark scheme

- Water vapor was circulated with methane, ammonia and hydrogen in the absence of free oxygen / under reducing conditions. **[1]**
- Electrical discharges supplied energy / simulated lightning. **[1]**
- Amino acids / other carbon compounds formed from simpler starting substances. **[1]**
- The result demonstrates that organic building blocks can form abiotically under the tested conditions. **[1]**
- The atmospheric composition of early Earth is uncertain / the experiment did not produce self-replicating systems or cells. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “Miller and Urey created life” loses the evaluation. The experiment supports abiotic synthesis of building blocks under one set of proposed conditions._

## Q3. Explain fatty-acid vesicle formation and its importance. **[4]**

Explain how fatty acids could form spherical bilayer vesicles and why this mattered in the origin of cells.

### Mark scheme

- Fatty acids are amphipathic / have hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions. **[1]**
- Hydrophilic regions face the aqueous solutions while hydrophobic regions coalesce away from water / face inward toward one another. **[1]**
- A bilayer closes into a sphere / forms a membrane-bound vesicle with an aqueous interior. **[1]**
- The membrane permits internal chemistry to differ from external conditions / concentrates and retains reacting molecules. **[1]**

_Examiner note: a diagram of a bilayer can earn structural marks, but full marks require the consequence of compartmentalization. A vesicle isn’t automatically a living cell._

## Q4. Explain RNA as the presumed first genetic material. **[4]**

Explain the evidence and reasoning behind the RNA world hypothesis.

### Mark scheme

- RNA nucleotide sequences can store genetic information. **[1]**
- RNA can act as a template for complementary copying / can be replicated. **[1]**
- Some RNA molecules have catalytic activity / are ribozymes. **[1]**
- Ribosomal RNA catalyzes peptide bond formation, supporting an ancient catalytic role for RNA. **[1]**

_Examiner note: describing RNA as “simpler than DNA” doesn’t answer the mechanism. Full marks come from its combined roles in heredity and catalysis plus the ribosome evidence._

## Q5. Distinguish LUCA from the first cell and outline evidence for LUCA. **[5]**

Distinguish the last universal common ancestor from the first living cell, and outline molecular evidence for its existence.

### Mark scheme

- LUCA was the most recent ancestral population shared by all organisms alive today. **[1]**
- LUCA was not necessarily the first cell / earlier cellular lineages could have existed. **[1]**
- The genetic code is shared across living organisms / is nearly universal. **[1]**
- Conserved genes / sequences for fundamental cellular processes occur across all domains. **[1]**
- Other early lineages may have become extinct through competition with LUCA and its descendants. **[1]**

_Examiner note: expanding the acronym earns no mark by itself. The answer must state which organisms share this ancestor and why LUCA needn’t have been the first life._

## Q6. Explain evidence connecting LUCA with hydrothermal vents. **[6]**

Explain how fossil and genomic evidence support the hypothesis that LUCA evolved near hydrothermal vents, and give one limitation of the evidence.

### Mark scheme

- Hydrothermal fluids contain dissolved minerals that precipitate on contact with seawater. **[1]**
- Precipitates can mineralize / preserve structures or chemical signatures produced by organisms. **[1]**
- Ancient seafloor hydrothermal vent precipitates contain fossilized evidence interpreted as early life. **[1]**
- Genomic comparisons identify conserved sequences shared by bacteria and archaea / sequences inferred to have been present in LUCA. **[1]**
- Some inferred genes indicate anaerobic, hydrogen-dependent or high-temperature metabolism compatible with vent conditions. **[1]**
- Fossil-like structures may have abiotic explanations / reconstructed genes support but do not prove LUCA’s exact habitat. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “vents supplied energy” is a plausible idea but doesn’t answer an evidence question. Full marks require both ancient precipitates and conserved-sequence evidence._
