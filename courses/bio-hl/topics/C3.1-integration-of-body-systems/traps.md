# C3.1 Integration of body systems — where the marks go

## Treating a nerve as one neuron

**Commonly written:** “A nerve is a long neuron surrounded by myelin.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A nerve contains many nerve fibres within a protective sheath and may include both sensory and motor fibres.

**Scores instead:** “A nerve is a bundle of nerve fibres enclosed by protective connective tissue, with myelinated and unmyelinated fibres visible in transverse section.”

## Sending a reflex to the brain before withdrawal

**Commonly written:** “The sensory neuron sends the pain signal to the brain, which tells the hand to pull away.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The spinal cord integrates the withdrawal response through an interneuron before conscious processing by the brain is required.

**Scores instead:** “The sensory neuron enters the spinal cord, an interneuron relays the signal to a motor neuron, and skeletal muscle withdraws the hand.”

## Reversing sensory and motor neurons

**Commonly written:** “A motor neuron carries the stimulus from the receptor to the spinal cord.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Sensory neurons carry input from receptors to the central nervous system; motor neurons carry output to effectors.

**Scores instead:** “The sensory neuron carries impulses to the spinal cord, and the motor neuron carries impulses from it to skeletal muscle.”

## Saying low oxygen alone controls exercise ventilation

**Commonly written:** “Breathing speeds up because chemoreceptors detect that the blood has run out of oxygen.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The expected feedback chain emphasizes increased carbon dioxide, the resulting fall in blood pH and detection by chemoreceptors.

**Scores instead:** “Exercise increases carbon dioxide production, lowering blood pH, so chemoreceptor input to the medulla causes ventilation to increase.”

## Giving the cerebellum the wrong job

**Commonly written:** “The cerebellum starts the pain reflex and sends the first signal to the muscle.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The spinal cord integrates the pain reflex; the cerebellum coordinates skeletal muscle contractions and balance during movement.

**Scores instead:** “The cerebellum coordinates the timing and force of skeletal muscle contractions and helps maintain balance.”

## Calling all digestive movement voluntary

**Commonly written:** “The brain voluntarily controls the muscles that push food through the entire digestive tract.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Only the initiation of swallowing and egestion has voluntary control here; the enteric nervous system controls intervening peristalsis involuntarily.

**Scores instead:** “The CNS controls the voluntary endpoints, while the enteric nervous system coordinates involuntary peristalsis through the gut.”

## Saying auxin makes a shoot move toward light

**Commonly written:** “Auxin pulls the shoot toward the light.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A shoot bends because auxin causes greater cell elongation on the shaded side, not because auxin exerts a pulling force.

**Scores instead:** “More auxin on the shaded side promotes greater cell elongation there, causing the shoot to curve toward the light.”

## Calling ethylene regulation negative feedback

**Commonly written:** “Ethylene causes ripening, and the fruit reduces ethylene to keep ripening stable.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Ripening increases ethylene production, which causes further ripening, so the response amplifies rather than being reversed.

**Scores instead:** “Ethylene promotes ripening, and ripening promotes further ethylene production, producing rapid, synchronized ripening by positive feedback.”
