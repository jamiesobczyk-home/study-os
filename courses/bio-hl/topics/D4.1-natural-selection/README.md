# D4.1 Natural selection

- **Theme:** D — Continuity and change
- **Level:** Ecosystems
- **Level of study:** SL and HL
- **Status:** ready

## The one-sentence version

Natural selection changes a population when individuals with advantageous heritable traits leave more offspring, causing their alleles to become more frequent.

## Why this topic is worth getting right

The mechanism is short, but near-miss answers often leave out heredity, reproduction or the fact that populations change rather than individuals. Learn it as a causal chain you can apply to an unfamiliar organism. At HL, keep the biological argument connected to the math: Hardy–Weinberg predicts genotype frequencies under stated conditions, while selection explains why allele frequencies may change.

## In your study guide

Allott, _Biology Study Guide_ (2023), **section D4.1, pp. 215–219**. Read it *after*
the capture sheet, never before. It's condensed enough to make a fast check of
what you left out, and a slow way to meet the topic for the first time.
