# A4.1 Evolution and speciation — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Define evolution as a change in the heritable characteristics of a population and explain why an acquired characteristic isn’t evolution.
- [ ] Use similarities and differences in DNA, RNA or amino acid sequences as evidence for common ancestry.
- [ ] Explain how selective breeding of domesticated animals and crop plants provides evidence that populations can evolve rapidly.
- [ ] Explain how homologous structures, including pentadactyl limbs, provide evidence of common ancestry and divergent evolution.
- [ ] Distinguish analogous from homologous structures and explain how convergent evolution produces analogous structures.
- [ ] Distinguish speciation from gradual evolutionary change within one species and state how speciation and extinction affect species number.
- [ ] Explain how reproductive isolation and differential selection can split one ancestral species into two.
- [ ] Apply the sequence of geographical isolation, differential selection and divergence to bonobos and common chimpanzees separated by the Congo River.

## Higher level

- [ ] Compare allopatric and sympatric speciation, including what prevents gene flow in each process.
- [ ] Explain how geographical, behavioral and temporal isolation can produce reproductive isolation.
- [ ] Explain how adaptive radiation increases biodiversity when vacant ecological niches are available.
- [ ] Distinguish prezygotic barriers to hybridization from postzygotic hybrid sterility, using courtship behavior and mules as examples.
- [ ] Explain how hybridization and polyploidy can cause abrupt plant speciation, using Persicaria as the required example.
- [ ] Distinguish autopolyploidy from allopolyploidy and explain how chromosome number can isolate a polyploid from its parent population.

## Links to other topics

- **A3.2 Classification and cladistics** — sequence comparisons and homologous structures help reconstruct evolutionary relationships. This connection lets you explain why a cladogram is evidence-based rather than a diagram of appearance.
- **A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity** — speciation increases species diversity, while extinction decreases it. A4.1 supplies the process behind changes in species richness.
- **D1.3 Mutations and gene editing** — mutation creates new alleles and therefore the heritable variation on which evolutionary processes can act.
- **D2.1 Cell and nuclear division** — errors in meiosis or mitosis can produce polyploid cells. Chromosome behavior during meiosis explains both restored fertility in some polyploids and sterility in many hybrids.
- **D4.1 Natural selection and sustainability** — natural selection changes allele frequencies through differences in survival and reproduction. A4.1 applies that process to populations that are becoming separate species.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evolution | Change in the heritable characteristics of a population over generations | Growth, learning or another acquired change within one organism |
| Common ancestry | Descent of different populations or species from a shared ancestral population | A claim that the organisms remain the same species |
| Homologous structure | A structure with the same evolutionary origin as one in another organism, although its function may differ | Any two structures that perform the same function |
| Analogous structure | A structure with a similar function to one in another organism but a different evolutionary origin | Evidence by itself of a recent common ancestor |
| Divergent evolution | Accumulation of differences in related populations descended from a common ancestor | Unrelated lineages independently evolving similar features |
| Convergent evolution | Independent evolution of similar features in lineages facing similar selection pressures | Inheritance of the feature from their most recent common ancestor |
| Speciation | Splitting and divergence of a pre-existing species to form new species | Gradual change that leaves a single species |
| Reproductive isolation | Prevention of gene flow between populations because they don’t interbreed successfully | Separation that still allows frequent exchange of alleles |
| Differential selection | Different selection pressures favor different heritable characteristics in separate populations | Organisms choosing to develop the characteristics they need |
| Allopatric speciation | Speciation involving geographical separation of populations | Speciation within the same geographical area |
| Sympatric speciation | Speciation without geographical separation | Speciation with no reproductive barrier |
| Adaptive radiation | Diversification of an ancestral species into multiple species adapted to different niches | One species changing while continuing to occupy the same niche |
| Prezygotic barrier | A barrier that prevents mating or fertilization | Sterility that appears after a hybrid has developed |
| Interspecific hybrid | Offspring produced by parents belonging to different species | Any heterozygous offspring within one species |
| Polyploidy | Possession of more than two complete sets of chromosomes | A change in the number of one chromosome |
| Autopolyploidy | Polyploidy produced by chromosome-set duplication within one species | Polyploidy involving chromosome sets from different species |
| Allopolyploidy | Polyploidy involving hybridization between species followed by chromosome doubling | Ordinary sexual reproduction between members of one species |
