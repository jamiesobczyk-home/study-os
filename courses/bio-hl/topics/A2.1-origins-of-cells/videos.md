# A2.1 Origins of cells — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and reconstruct the proposed sequence from non-living chemistry to cells. A second explanation can make the story feel familiar without making it producible, and producing the sequence is what the exam requires.

## Main

**Alex Lee Biology — A2.1 Origins of cells**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+A2.1+Origins+of+cells>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the explanation made for A2.1.

**Watch for:**

- What evidence supports each proposed stage, rather than which stages sound plausible.
- Why RNA could combine heredity and catalysis in one kind of molecule.
- Why a vesicle helps without automatically becoming alive.
- Which evidence supports LUCA, and which evidence specifically connects early life with hydrothermal vents.

**Pause at:** the first complete sequence from prebiotic compounds to a protocell. Cover the labels and reproduce it using the terms catalysis, self-assembly, self-replication and compartmentalization. Beside each term, state what problem it solves.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Fatty-acid self-assembly is spatial, so an **animation** earns an extra watch. Movement makes it easier to see why hydrophilic regions face water, hydrophobic regions cluster away from it and a bilayer closes into a sphere.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=fatty+acid+vesicle+self+assembly+animation>

After watching, draw a cross-section of a spherical bilayer vesicle. Label the water-facing regions, inward-facing hydrophobic regions, aqueous interior and external solution. Then explain why the vesicle creates a compartment but doesn’t qualify as living by itself.
