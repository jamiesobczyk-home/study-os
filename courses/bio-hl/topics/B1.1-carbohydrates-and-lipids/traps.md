# B1.1 Carbohydrates and lipids — where the marks go

## Saying condensation adds water

**Commonly written:** "Condensation joins two monomers by adding a water molecule."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Condensation forms a covalent bond and releases water. Adding water to break the bond describes hydrolysis.

**Scores instead:** "Condensation joins monomers by forming a covalent bond and releasing water, while hydrolysis breaks the bond by adding water."

## Calling triglycerides polymers

**Commonly written:** "A triglyceride is a polymer made from glycerol and fatty-acid monomers."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A triglyceride contains one glycerol and three fatty acids rather than a chain of repeating monomer units.

**Scores instead:** "A triglyceride forms when one glycerol joins three fatty acids by condensation, producing three ester bonds."

## Mixing up starch and cellulose monomers

**Commonly written:** "Cellulose is a strong, unbranched polymer of alpha-glucose."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Cellulose contains beta-glucose. Alpha-glucose forms the energy-storage polysaccharides starch and glycogen.

**Scores instead:** "Cellulose contains beta-glucose monomers whose alternating orientation produces straight chains."

## Putting hydrogen bonds along a cellulose chain

**Commonly written:** "Hydrogen bonds join the beta-glucose monomers into each cellulose chain."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Beta-glucose monomers within a chain are joined by glycosidic bonds; hydrogen bonds form between neighboring chains.

**Scores instead:** "Beta-1,4 glycosidic bonds join monomers within each chain, and hydrogen bonds cross-link parallel chains into microfibrils."

## Saying every double bond creates a kink

**Commonly written:** "Any C=C bond bends a fatty acid and makes the lipid an oil."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A cis double bond produces a kink, but a trans double bond leaves the chain relatively straight and able to pack closely.

**Scores instead:** "Cis C=C bonds kink fatty-acid chains, reduce close packing and generally lower the melting point."

## Reversing phospholipid orientation

**Commonly written:** "The hydrophobic heads face the water while the hydrophilic tails meet inside the membrane."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The phosphate-containing heads are hydrophilic, while the hydrocarbon fatty-acid tails are hydrophobic.

**Scores instead:** "Hydrophilic heads face the aqueous solutions, while hydrophobic tails face inward toward one another."

## Explaining steroid diffusion by size alone

**Commonly written:** "Steroid hormones pass through membranes because they are small molecules."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The key property is non-polarity, which allows a steroid to dissolve in and cross the bilayer’s hydrophobic interior.

**Scores instead:** "Non-polar steroid hormones dissolve in the hydrophobic core of the phospholipid bilayer and diffuse through it."

## Calling branching wasted space

**Commonly written:** "Branched glycogen takes up more space and releases glucose more slowly."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Branching keeps glycogen compact and creates many chain ends where glucose units can be added or removed.

**Scores instead:** "Glycogen is compact and highly branched, giving enzymes many ends from which glucose can be mobilized."
