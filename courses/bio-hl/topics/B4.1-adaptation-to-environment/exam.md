# B4.1 Adaptation to environment — exam practice

## Q1. Explain how adaptations of marram grass allow it to survive on coastal sand dunes. **[4]**

Marram grass grows in dry, windy dunes where sand can shift or accumulate. Explain how its structures suit these conditions.

### Mark scheme

- Rolled leaves reduce the surface area exposed to moving air / enclose humid air. **[1]**
- Sunken stomata / stomata in leaf furrows maintain humid air and reduce the water-vapor gradient for transpiration. **[1]**
- Thick waxy cuticle reduces evaporation from the leaf surface. **[1]**
- Extensive roots / rhizomes anchor the plant in shifting sand and provide access to water / permit growth through accumulating sand. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming a feature earns little unless its effect is linked to drought, wind or unstable sand._

## Q2. Explain how a transect can be used to investigate the relationship between soil pH and the distribution of a plant species. **[5]**

Describe the measurements and analysis needed for a valid investigation.

### Mark scheme

- Position a line / belt transect across a gradient in soil pH. **[1]**
- Place equal-sized quadrats at regular intervals / use a stated systematic sampling method along the transect. **[1]**
- Measure abundance / frequency / percentage cover of the plant in each quadrat. **[1]**
- Measure soil pH at the corresponding sampling positions using a calibrated probe / sensor. **[1]**
- Plot paired data / calculate a correlation and repeat transects to improve reliability. **[1]**

_Examiner note: measuring pH at one end and plants at the other doesn’t produce paired data. Each abundance measurement needs a corresponding pH measurement._

## Q3. Explain why coral reefs form only in a restricted range of marine environments. **[5]**

Refer to five abiotic requirements in your answer.

### Mark scheme

- Shallow water allows sufficient light to reach photosynthetic zooxanthellae. **[1]**
- Clear / low-turbidity water allows light penetration for photosynthesis. **[1]**
- Warm water within the corals’ temperature tolerance range permits normal metabolism / survival of coral and zooxanthellae. **[1]**
- Salinity must remain within the tolerance range to prevent osmotic stress. **[1]**
- pH must be sufficiently high / suitable for deposition of calcium carbonate skeletons. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “corals need sunlight” is incomplete because the question asks why their distribution is restricted. Connect light availability to shallow, clear water and zooxanthellae._

## Q4. Compare and contrast the climatic conditions of tropical forest, taiga and hot desert biomes. **[6]**

Use paired comparisons involving temperature, precipitation and seasonality.

### Mark scheme

Award any six:

- Tropical forest has high temperature, whereas taiga has low temperature / hot desert has high daytime temperature. **[1]**
- Tropical forest has high precipitation, whereas hot desert has very low precipitation. **[1]**
- Taiga has moderate to low precipitation, greater than that of hot desert but lower than that of tropical forest. **[1]**
- Tropical forest has little seasonal temperature variation. **[1]**
- Taiga has long, cold winters and short, cool summers / strong seasonal temperature variation. **[1]**
- Hot desert has scarce, unpredictable rainfall / large day–night temperature variation. **[1]**
- Both tropical forest and hot desert can have high temperatures, but they differ greatly in precipitation. **[1]**

_Examiner note: three separate biome descriptions can miss the command term. Full marks require statements that use one biome as a reference for another._

## Q5. Explain how convergent evolution can produce similar organisms in widely separated deserts. **[4]**

Use cacti in the Americas and euphorbias in Africa as examples.

### Mark scheme

- Cacti and euphorbias are distantly related / the similar features weren’t inherited from a recent common ancestor. **[1]**
- Both groups experience similar selection pressures, including low water availability / high temperature. **[1]**
- Natural selection independently favors features that conserve or store water. **[1]**
- Similar features such as succulent stems / reduced leaves / waxy cuticles evolve independently in both groups. **[1]**

_Examiner note: resemblance alone doesn’t establish convergent evolution. The answer needs distant relationship, shared selection pressure and independent evolution._

## Q6. Suggest how a sustained decrease in rainfall could alter the distribution of a plant species. **[4]**

The species currently occurs where rainfall is close to its optimum. Use the idea of a tolerance range.

### Mark scheme

- Reduced rainfall lowers water availability / moves conditions away from the species’ optimum. **[1]**
- Populations enter a zone of physiological stress, reducing growth / survival / reproduction. **[1]**
- Where rainfall passes the lower limit of tolerance, local populations cannot persist / local extinction occurs. **[1]**
- Distribution may contract or shift toward areas where rainfall remains within the tolerance range. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the plant “adapts to less rain” misses the timescale and the question. The immediate prediction concerns stress, population loss and a changed distribution._
