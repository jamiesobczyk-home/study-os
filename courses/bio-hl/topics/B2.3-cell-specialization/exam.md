# B2.3 Cell specialization — exam practice

## Q1. Explain how morphogen gradients can cause cell specialization in an early human embryo. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- A morphogen is a signaling substance distributed in a concentration gradient across embryonic tissue. **[1]**
- Cells in different positions are exposed to different morphogen concentrations. **[1]**
- Different concentrations regulate different genes / patterns of gene expression. **[1]**
- Different proteins are produced, causing cells to differentiate into specialized cell types. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that morphogens "tell cells what to become" scores little unless the answer links concentration to gene expression and gene expression to different proteins._

## Q2. Distinguish totipotent, pluripotent and multipotent stem cells. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Totipotent cells can form all embryonic and extraembryonic cell types / can produce a whole organism. **[1]**
- Accept: a zygote / very early embryonic cell as a totipotent example. **[1]**
- Pluripotent cells can form cell types from all three germ layers but cannot form all tissues required for a whole organism. **[1]**
- Multipotent cells can form a limited range of related cell types. **[1]**
- Accept: haemopoietic stem cells produce blood-cell lineages / bone marrow stem cells as a multipotent example. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "totipotent means any cell, pluripotent means many cells" is too imprecise. Full marks depend on the whole-organism limit and the restricted, related range of multipotent cells._

## Q3. Explain how surface area-to-volume ratio constrains cell size. **[4]**

A cubic cell increases in side length from 1 µm to 3 µm. Use calculations in your answer.

### Mark scheme

- Surface area-to-volume ratio at 1 µm side length is 6:1 / surface area is 6 µm² and volume is 1 µm³. **[1]**
- Surface area-to-volume ratio at 3 µm side length is 2:1 / surface area is 54 µm² and volume is 27 µm³. **[1]**
- Volume / metabolic demand increases faster than surface area / membrane area as linear size increases. **[1]**
- At a low surface area-to-volume ratio, membrane exchange may be insufficient to supply the cell or remove its wastes. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "the larger cell has less surface area" is false. Its surface area is larger, but it has less surface area for each unit of volume._

## Q4. Explain how type I and type II pneumocytes are adapted to the functions of alveoli. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Type I pneumocytes are flattened / extremely thin. **[1]**
- Type I pneumocytes reduce diffusion distance for oxygen and carbon dioxide. **[1]**
- Type II pneumocytes contain many secretory vesicles / lamellar bodies that release surfactant into the alveolar lumen. **[1]**
- Surfactant reduces surface tension / helps prevent alveolar collapse / makes inflation easier. **[1]**

_Examiner note: identifying both cell types without assigning the correct job to each is a near miss. Thinness belongs to type I; lamellar bodies and surfactant secretion belong to type II._

## Q5. Compare and contrast cardiac muscle cells and striated skeletal muscle fibres. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both contain contractile myofibrils / organized protein filaments. **[1]**
- Cardiac muscle cells are short, whereas skeletal muscle fibres are much longer. **[1]**
- Cardiac muscle cells are branched, whereas skeletal muscle fibres are unbranched. **[1]**
- Cardiac muscle cells usually have one nucleus, whereas skeletal muscle fibres are multinucleate. **[1]**
- Skeletal muscle fibres form by fusion of precursor cells / have one continuous plasma membrane despite containing many nuclei. **[1]**
- Cardiac branching forms an interconnected contractile network, whereas long skeletal fibres shorten along the direction of pull on a bone. **[1]**

_Examiner note: striations don't distinguish these tissues because both contain organized myofibrils. The comparison must use branching, length, nuclei or developmental origin._

## Q6. Compare and contrast the adaptations of human sperm and egg cells. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

Award any six:

- Both are haploid gametes / contribute one set of chromosomes at fertilization. **[1]**
- Sperm are small and motile, whereas eggs are large and non-motile. **[1]**
- A sperm tail / flagellum contains an axoneme that bends to provide propulsion. **[1]**
- The sperm midpiece contains many mitochondria that supply ATP for movement. **[1]**
- The sperm acrosome contains hydrolytic enzymes that help penetrate the egg's surrounding layers. **[1]**
- The egg contributes most of the zygote's cytoplasm and organelles. **[1]**
- The egg has cortical granules whose contents help prevent polyspermy after sperm fusion. **[1]**
- The egg is surrounded by a zona pellucida and corona radiata. **[1]**

_Examiner note: a list of sperm structures earns only the sperm marks. The command term requires both gametes and at least one valid similarity or direct contrast._
