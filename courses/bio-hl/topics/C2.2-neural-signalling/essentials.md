# C2.2 Neural signalling — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Identify the cell body, dendrites and axon of a neuron, and connect each structure to the direction in which it carries information.
- [ ] Explain how ATP-powered pumping and selective ion movement establish a negative resting potential across a neuron's plasma membrane.
- [ ] Explain why a nerve impulse is an electrical action potential rather than a flow of electricity through an axon like a wire.
- [ ] Explain how axon diameter and myelination affect conduction speed, and interpret correlations involving conduction speed, including \(r\) and \(R^2\).
- [ ] Distinguish the presynaptic neuron, synaptic cleft and postsynaptic cell, and explain why transmission across a typical chemical synapse is one-way.
- [ ] Explain how depolarization of a presynaptic terminal causes calcium ion entry and neurotransmitter release by exocytosis.
- [ ] Explain how acetylcholine produces an excitatory postsynaptic potential after crossing a synapse.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain depolarization and repolarization in terms of threshold and voltage-gated sodium and potassium channels.
- [ ] Explain how local currents propagate an action potential along an axon without the original action potential moving as one object.
- [ ] Interpret an oscilloscope trace by linking its phases to ion-channel events and calculating impulse frequency.
- [ ] Explain why saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier is faster than conduction along an unmyelinated axon.
- [ ] Explain how neonicotinoids and cocaine disrupt synaptic transmission by different mechanisms.
- [ ] Explain how an inhibitory neurotransmitter hyperpolarizes a postsynaptic membrane and lowers the chance of an action potential.
- [ ] Predict whether summation of excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials will bring a postsynaptic neuron to threshold.
- [ ] Explain how high temperature, acid or capsaicin can lead from stimulation of a free nerve ending to the perception of pain.
- [ ] Explain why consciousness is described as an emergent property of interacting neurons rather than a property of one neuron.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membrane potential | The voltage difference across a plasma membrane caused by unequal distributions of charge | The concentration of one ion on one side |
| Resting potential | The negative membrane potential of a neuron that isn’t transmitting an action potential | A neuron with no ion movement or no stored energy |
| Polarized membrane | A membrane with a difference in electrical potential across it | A membrane made from polar phospholipids |
| Depolarization | A change in membrane potential that makes the inside less negative, or positive during an action potential | Every movement of sodium ions across any membrane |
| Repolarization | The return toward a negative membrane potential after depolarization | Restoration of all ion concentration gradients by voltage-gated channels |
| Threshold potential | The membrane potential that must be reached to trigger the opening of enough voltage-gated sodium channels for an action potential | The highest voltage reached by an action potential |
| Action potential | A rapid, temporary reversal of membrane potential that can be propagated along an axon | A chemical moving from one end of the neuron to the other |
| Synapse | A junction where a neuron communicates with another neuron or an effector cell | The synaptic cleft alone |
| Neurotransmitter | A signalling chemical released by a presynaptic neuron that binds to receptors on a postsynaptic cell | The electrical impulse inside the axon |
| Excitatory postsynaptic potential | A graded depolarization of the postsynaptic membrane that makes threshold more likely to be reached | An action potential that must occur after every neurotransmitter release |
| Inhibitory postsynaptic potential | A graded hyperpolarization that makes threshold less likely to be reached | Destruction of the postsynaptic neuron or permanent closure of every channel |
| Hyperpolarization | A change that makes the inside of the membrane more negative than before | Repolarization after every action potential |
| Summation | The combined effect of multiple postsynaptic potentials on whether threshold is reached | An increase in the size of individual action potentials |
| Saltatory conduction | Propagation in a myelinated axon in which action potentials are regenerated at successive nodes of Ranvier | An action potential physically leaping through extracellular space |
| Exogenous chemical | A chemical that enters an organism from an external source | A neurotransmitter naturally released by the organism |
| Emergent property | A property produced by interactions among components that no component has alone | A property stored inside one component and passed to the whole system |
