# A2.3 Viruses — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Outline the structural features shared by viruses and explain their dependence on host cells. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Genetic material consists of nucleic acid / DNA or RNA. **[1]**
- Protein capsid surrounds / protects the genetic material. **[1]**
- Viruses have no cytoplasm and few or no enzymes. **[1]**
- Viruses lack ribosomes / cannot carry out independent protein synthesis. **[1]**
- Viruses depend on host cells for energy, nutrients, protein synthesis and other life functions. **[1]**

_Examiner note: an envelope can’t be credited as a shared feature because many viruses are non-enveloped._

## Q2. Compare and contrast the structures of bacteriophage lambda, a coronavirus and HIV. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

Award any six:

- All contain a nucleic-acid genome surrounded by protein / a capsid. **[1]**
- Lambda contains double-stranded DNA, whereas coronaviruses and HIV contain single-stranded RNA. **[1]**
- Lambda is non-enveloped, whereas coronaviruses and HIV are enveloped. **[1]**
- Lambda has a complex head-and-tail structure / tail involved in attachment to bacteria. **[1]**
- Coronavirus has positive-sense single-stranded RNA and projecting spike proteins. **[1]**
- HIV has two copies of positive-sense single-stranded RNA. **[1]**
- HIV contains reverse transcriptase. **[1]**
- Both coronavirus and HIV envelopes contain viral glycoproteins used in host-cell recognition / entry. **[1]**

_Examiner note: three separate descriptions earn fewer marks than direct comparisons. Full marks require shared features and clear differences among the named viruses._

## Q3. Explain the lytic cycle of bacteriophage lambda. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Phage attaches to specific receptors on a bacterial cell. **[1]**
- Phage DNA is injected into the host cell. **[1]**
- Viral genes redirect host-cell activities / viral DNA is replicated using host resources. **[1]**
- Viral proteins are synthesized using host ribosomes. **[1]**
- Viral nucleic acid and proteins assemble into new virions. **[1]**
- Host cell lyses and releases virions. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the phage “reproduces in the bacterium” misses the sequence. The DNA enters, components are produced separately, virions assemble, and the host lyses._

## Q4. Compare and contrast the lytic and lysogenic cycles of bacteriophage lambda. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both begin with attachment to a bacterial host and injection of phage DNA. **[1]**
- In the lytic cycle, viral DNA remains separate from the host chromosome / is replicated for virion production. **[1]**
- In the lysogenic cycle, viral DNA integrates into the bacterial chromosome as a prophage. **[1]**
- Lytic infection produces and assembles new virions, whereas lysogeny has no immediate virion production. **[1]**
- Lytic infection ends in host-cell lysis, whereas the lysogenic host survives and reproduces. **[1]**
- The prophage is copied with the host chromosome and passed to daughter cells / induction can switch it into the lytic cycle. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “active” and “dormant” are too loose by themselves. A full comparison tracks the viral DNA, virion production and fate of the host._

## Q5. Explain the evidence and hypotheses supporting several origins of viruses from cellular organisms. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Viruses show extensive diversity in genome type, replication mechanism and structure. **[1]**
- Dependence on cellular hosts / the shared genetic code supports an origin after or from cellular life. **[1]**
- Progressive / escape hypothesis: cellular genetic elements gained the ability to transfer between cells. **[1]**
- Regressive / reduction hypothesis: cellular parasites lost structures and functions as host dependence increased. **[1]**
- Shared viral features may have evolved independently by convergent evolution under similar selection pressures. **[1]**

_Examiner note: the two hypotheses aren’t rival descriptions of one known event. They offer possible origins for different viral groups, which fits the evidence for several origins._

## Q6. Explain why influenza viruses and HIV evolve rapidly and discuss one consequence for managing each disease. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Rapid genome replication / short generation time produces many generations in a short period. **[1]**
- Large numbers of virions increase the number of mutations arising. **[1]**
- Error-prone genome copying / limited proofreading produces a high mutation rate in many RNA viruses. **[1]**
- Influenza antigenic drift changes surface antigens, requiring vaccine strains to be reviewed or updated. **[1]**
- Accept: reassortment of influenza genome segments can cause antigenic shift / a major antigen change. **[1]**
- HIV reverse transcriptase lacks proofreading, producing variants from which drug-resistant forms can be selected. **[1]**
- Accept: combination antiretroviral therapy reduces the probability that a virus is resistant to every drug used. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “viruses mutate because they need to escape” reverses the logic. Mutations arise without regard to need, and treatment or immunity selects among the resulting variants._
