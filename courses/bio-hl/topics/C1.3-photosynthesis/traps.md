# C1.3 Photosynthesis — where the marks go

## Saying the released oxygen comes from carbon dioxide

**Commonly written:** "Carbon dioxide is split, and its oxygen is released from the leaf."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Oxygen gas is produced by photolysis of water. Carbon dioxide supplies carbon that enters organic compounds.

**Scores instead:** "Photolysis splits water into electrons, protons and oxygen, while carbon dioxide supplies carbon for the Calvin cycle."

## Treating an action spectrum as an absorption spectrum

**Commonly written:** "The action spectrum shows how much light chlorophyll absorbs."

**Why it doesn’t score:** An action spectrum shows photosynthetic rate at each wavelength. An absorption spectrum measures light absorption.

**Scores instead:** "The action spectrum plots photosynthetic rate against wavelength, while an absorption spectrum plots light absorption against wavelength."

## Using distance traveled as the Rf value

**Commonly written:** "The pigment moved 4 cm, so its Rf value is 4."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Rf is a ratio of two distances measured from the baseline, so it has no unit and is normally between zero and one.

**Scores instead:** "Rf equals the distance traveled by the pigment divided by the distance traveled by the solvent front."

## Assuming a plateau means light has no effect on photosynthesis

**Commonly written:** "The graph levels off because the plant can’t use any more light."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The plateau shows that light is no longer limiting under those conditions. Another factor now restricts the rate.

**Scores instead:** "At the plateau, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature or another factor limits photosynthesis, so more light doesn’t raise the rate."

## Swapping RuBP and rubisco

**Commonly written:** "RuBP is the enzyme that joins rubisco to carbon dioxide."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Rubisco is the enzyme, while RuBP is the five-carbon compound that accepts carbon dioxide.

**Scores instead:** "Rubisco catalyzes the fixation of carbon dioxide by combining it with the five-carbon compound RuBP."

## Sending electrons through ATP synthase

**Commonly written:** "Electrons flow through ATP synthase and provide the energy to make ATP."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Electrons pass through an electron transport chain. Protons flow through ATP synthase down their electrochemical gradient.

**Scores instead:** "Electron transfer builds a proton gradient, and protons flowing through ATP synthase drive ATP production."

## Saying ATP supplies hydrogen in the Calvin cycle

**Commonly written:** "ATP provides the hydrogen needed to reduce glycerate 3-phosphate."

**Why it doesn’t score:** ATP supplies energy, while reduced NADP supplies electrons and hydrogen for reduction.

**Scores instead:** "ATP supplies energy and reduced NADP supplies reducing power for the conversion of glycerate 3-phosphate to triose phosphate."

## Calling the Calvin cycle independent of light

**Commonly written:** "The Calvin cycle can continue in darkness because it doesn’t use light directly."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The cycle depends on ATP and reduced NADP from the light-dependent reactions, so it stops when their supplies run out.

**Scores instead:** "The Calvin cycle doesn’t absorb light directly, but it depends on ATP and reduced NADP produced by the light-dependent reactions."
