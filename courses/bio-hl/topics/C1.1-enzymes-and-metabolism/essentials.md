# C1.1 Enzymes and metabolism — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how enzymes increase reaction rates without being used up, and why cells need different enzymes to control metabolism.
- [ ] Distinguish anabolic reactions from catabolic reactions and give named examples of each.
- [ ] Relate an enzyme’s globular 3D structure and active-site amino acids to its catalytic function.
- [ ] Describe induced-fit binding, including changes in the shapes of both the enzyme and substrate.
- [ ] Explain how molecular motion, collision frequency, orientation and energy affect enzyme-catalyzed reactions.
- [ ] Explain enzyme specificity and how denaturation changes an active site and causes loss of function.
- [ ] Interpret the effects of temperature, pH and substrate concentration on enzyme activity using collision theory, denaturation and active-site saturation.
- [ ] Design an enzyme investigation, measure substrate disappearance or product formation, and calculate reaction rate.
- [ ] Interpret an energy-profile graph and explain how an enzyme lowers activation energy.

## Higher level

- [ ] Distinguish intracellular from extracellular enzyme-catalyzed reactions using glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and digestion as examples.
- [ ] Explain why metabolism generates heat and how mammals and birds use metabolic heat to maintain body temperature.
- [ ] Distinguish linear from cyclical metabolic pathways using glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and the Calvin cycle.
- [ ] Compare competitive and non-competitive inhibition, including binding sites and the effect of increasing substrate concentration.
- [ ] Explain feedback inhibition using isoleucine inhibition of threonine deaminase.
- [ ] Explain irreversible mechanism-based inhibition by penicillin and how altered transpeptidases can confer resistance.

## Links to other topics

- **B1.2 Proteins** — amino-acid interactions produce the enzyme’s 3D structure and active site. This connection gives specificity and denaturation a molecular cause.
- **C1.2 Cell respiration** — glycolysis and the Krebs cycle are enzyme-controlled metabolic pathways. C1.1 supplies the language for rates, regulation and energy transfer.
- **C1.3 Photosynthesis** — the Calvin cycle is a cyclical enzyme-controlled pathway, while photosynthesis is an anabolic process. This topic makes the pathway easier to organize.
- **B3.2 Transport** — digestion produces absorbable molecules through extracellular enzyme activity. Knowing where enzymes act prevents confusion between digestion and absorption.
- **D3.3 Homeostasis** — metabolic heat contributes to thermoregulation, while feedback inhibition regulates chemical pathways inside cells.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalyst | A substance that increases reaction rate without being used up in the reaction | A reactant that becomes part of the product |
| Enzyme | A biological catalyst with an active site where a specific reaction is catalyzed | A substance that supplies energy to a reaction |
| Metabolism | The network of interdependent chemical reactions in an organism | Digestion alone |
| Anabolism | Reactions that build larger or more complex molecules, usually requiring an energy input | Any reaction that releases energy |
| Catabolism | Reactions that break down or oxidize molecules, often releasing usable energy | Denaturation of an enzyme |
| Substrate | A reactant that binds to an enzyme’s active site | The enzyme or the product |
| Active site | The region formed by a few amino acids where the substrate binds and catalysis occurs | The whole surface of the enzyme |
| Induced fit | Changes in enzyme and substrate shape during binding that improve their interaction and favor catalysis | A rigid lock-and-key fit with no conformational change |
| Specificity | The ability of an active site to bind and catalyze reactions involving particular substrates | A claim that every enzyme acts on exactly one molecule |
| Denaturation | Disruption of an enzyme’s 3D conformation, changing its active site and function | Breaking every peptide bond in the enzyme |
| Activation energy | The minimum energy barrier that must be overcome for a reaction to occur | The total energy released by the reaction |
| Saturation | The condition in which all available active sites are occupied as often as possible | Denaturation caused by excess substrate |
| Allosteric site | A specific regulatory binding site separate from the active site | Another name for the active site |
| Competitive inhibitor | A reversible inhibitor that competes with the substrate for the active site | An inhibitor that permanently changes the enzyme |
| Non-competitive inhibitor | A reversible inhibitor that binds at an allosteric site and changes the active site’s conformation | A molecule displaced from the active site by extra substrate |
| Feedback inhibition | Regulation in which a pathway’s end product inhibits an enzyme acting earlier in the pathway | Product inhibition caused by damaged enzymes |
| Mechanism-based inhibitor | An inhibitor processed at the active site that causes irreversible chemical change and inactivation | Any reversible competitive inhibitor |
