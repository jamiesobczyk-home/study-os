# B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain the topic from a blank diagram. A second explanation of the same material builds recognition, which is the skill that’s been covering up what you can’t yet produce.

## Main

**Alex Lee Biology — B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+B2.2+Organelles+and+compartmentalization>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the explanation built for B2.2.

**Watch for:**

- What counts as an organelle in this syllabus, including the less obvious examples and exclusions.
- How a compartment increases reaction efficiency or separates incompatible processes.
- How the structures of mitochondria and chloroplasts support ATP production and photosynthesis.
- How a secreted protein moves from a ribosome to the rough ER, Golgi apparatus and plasma membrane.

**Pause at:** the first complete eukaryotic cell diagram. Cover the labels and point to the nucleus, rough ER, Golgi apparatus, mitochondrion and a vesicle. For each one, say what its boundary allows that an unsegmented cytoplasm wouldn’t.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Protein transport is a spatial process, so an **animation** earns an exception to the one-video rule. Movement makes vesicle budding, transport and membrane fusion clearer than a still diagram does.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=protein+secretion+rough+ER+Golgi+vesicle+animation>

After watching, draw a five-stage route from a bound ribosome to exocytosis. Label where translation occurs, where the protein enters the endomembrane system, where it’s processed, and which membranes bud or fuse.
