# D3.3 Homeostasis — where the marks go

## Describing homeostasis as perfect constancy

**Commonly written:** "Homeostasis keeps the internal environment constant."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Regulated variables fluctuate around set points and are kept within preset limits; they aren’t fixed at one value.

**Scores instead:** "Homeostasis maintains the internal environment within preset limits despite internal or external changes."

## Reversing the pancreatic cells

**Commonly written:** "Alpha cells release insulin, while beta cells release glucagon."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The hormone sources are reversed. Beta cells secrete insulin, and alpha cells secrete glucagon.

**Scores instead:** "A rise in blood glucose stimulates pancreatic beta cells to secrete insulin, while a fall stimulates alpha cells to secrete glucagon."

## Treating type 2 diabetes as no insulin production

**Commonly written:** "People with type 2 diabetes can’t produce insulin."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Type 2 diabetes begins mainly with reduced responsiveness of target cells to insulin, although insulin secretion may decline later.

**Scores instead:** "Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance, so target cells respond less effectively to the insulin being produced."

## Saying vasodilation cools the blood directly

**Commonly written:** "Vasodilation makes the blood colder."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Vasodilation increases blood flow through skin capillaries, which raises heat transfer from the body surface to the surroundings.

**Scores instead:** "Skin arterioles dilate, bringing more warm blood near the surface and increasing heat loss to the surroundings."

## Saying the kidney filters wastes out selectively

**Commonly written:** "The glomerulus filters urea out but keeps useful substances in the blood."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Ultrafiltration separates mainly by size, so small useful solutes such as glucose enter the filtrate along with urea.

**Scores instead:** "Pressure forces water and small solutes into Bowman’s capsule, after which useful substances are selectively reabsorbed."

## Saying all filtered glucose enters urine

**Commonly written:** "Glucose is small enough to be filtered, so it’s excreted in urine."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Glucose enters glomerular filtrate but is normally reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule.

**Scores instead:** "Glucose is ultrafiltered into Bowman’s capsule and then selectively reabsorbed by sodium-glucose cotransport in the proximal convoluted tubule."

## Saying ADH pumps water

**Commonly written:** "ADH actively transports water from the collecting duct into the blood."

**Why it doesn’t score:** ADH inserts aquaporins and increases membrane permeability; water then moves passively by osmosis.

**Scores instead:** "ADH causes aquaporins to enter collecting-duct membranes, so more water is reabsorbed by osmosis."

## Saying every organ gets more blood during exercise

**Commonly written:** "Exercise raises the heart rate, so all organs receive more blood."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Cardiac output rises, but blood is redistributed: active muscles receive more while the gut and kidneys receive less.

**Scores instead:** "During vigorous exercise, blood flow to skeletal muscle rises while arteriole constriction reduces flow to the gut and kidneys."
