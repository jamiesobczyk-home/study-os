# D2.3 Water potential — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain how water solvates polar molecules, positive ions and negative ions.
- [ ] Use hypotonic, hypertonic and isotonic correctly to compare two solutions.
- [ ] Explain osmosis as the net movement of water through a partially permeable membrane.
- [ ] Explain why water still moves in both directions at isotonic equilibrium.
- [ ] Predict changes in the mass or length of plant tissue placed in solutions of different solute concentrations.
- [ ] Use percentage change, a graph, standard deviation and standard error to analyze a plant-tissue investigation.
- [ ] Explain swelling, lysis, shrinking and crenation in cells that lack a cell wall.
- [ ] Explain how contractile vacuoles protect freshwater unicellular organisms from excessive water uptake.
- [ ] Explain how cell walls produce turgor pressure in hypotonic conditions and how plasmolysis occurs in hypertonic conditions.
- [ ] Explain why intravenous fluids, tissue fluid and solutions used to store transplant organs should be isotonic.

## Higher level

- [ ] Define water potential, state its reference conditions and give its usual unit.
- [ ] Predict net water movement from water-potential values, remembering that a less negative value is higher.
- [ ] Use Ψw = Ψs + Ψp to calculate water potential and explain the effects of solutes and pressure.
- [ ] Explain why pressure potential is generally positive inside cells but can be negative in xylem.
- [ ] Explain changes in plant cells placed in hypotonic or hypertonic solutions using solute potential, pressure potential and total water potential.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.1 Water** — polarity explains how water forms hydrogen bonds with polar solutes and electrostatic attractions with ions. That gives solvation a molecular cause.
- **B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport** — osmosis depends on a membrane that water can cross more readily than some solutes. This topic applies selective permeability to whole cells.
- **B3.2 Transport** — water-potential gradients help explain water entry into roots, while negative pressure potential helps explain movement through xylem.
- **D3.3 Homeostasis** — maintaining nearly isotonic tissue fluid prevents harmful changes in cell volume. This is one condition the internal environment has to regulate.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solvation | Surrounding and interaction of dissolved particles with solvent molecules | The movement of water across a membrane |
| Osmosis | Net movement of water through a partially permeable membrane from a hypotonic solution to a hypertonic solution | Movement of solute particles, or any movement of water without a membrane |
| Partially permeable membrane | A membrane that permits some substances to cross more readily than others | A membrane that blocks every substance |
| Hypotonic | Having a lower solute concentration than the solution being compared | A permanent property of a solution without a comparison |
| Hypertonic | Having a higher solute concentration than the solution being compared | A synonym for high water potential in every situation |
| Isotonic | Having the same solute concentration as the solution being compared | A condition in which water molecules stop moving |
| Dynamic equilibrium | Equal rates of movement in opposite directions, producing no net change | An absence of molecular movement |
| Turgor pressure | Pressure produced as the contents of a plant cell press the membrane against the cell wall | Osmosis itself, or pressure generated by the wall pumping water |
| Plasmolysis | Separation of the cell membrane from the cell wall after water leaves a walled cell | Bursting of an animal cell |
| Crenation | Shrinking and development of an irregular surface in an animal cell after water loss | Plasmolysis, which involves separation from a cell wall |
| Water potential, Ψw | Potential energy of water per unit volume, measured relative to pure water at atmospheric pressure and 20°C | The concentration of water molecules |
| Solute potential, Ψs | The contribution of dissolved solutes to water potential; it is zero or negative | A positive pressure exerted by the cell contents |
| Pressure potential, Ψp | The contribution of physical pressure or tension to water potential | The reduction in water potential caused by dissolved solute |
