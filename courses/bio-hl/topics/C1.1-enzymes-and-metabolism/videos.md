# C1.1 Enzymes and metabolism — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and produce the explanation yourself. A second explanation can make the material feel familiar without proving that you can retrieve it, and recognition is the problem this pack is meant to expose.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — C1.1 Enzymes and metabolism**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+C1.1+Enzymes+and+metabolism>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so begin with the explanation made for C1.1.

**Watch for:**

- How active-site structure causes specificity without making the enzyme completely rigid.
- Why temperature can increase collision frequency before high temperature denatures the enzyme.
- How competitive, non-competitive and mechanism-based inhibitors differ.
- How enzymes turn separate reactions into controlled metabolic pathways.

**Pause at:** the first enzyme–substrate diagram. Cover its labels and explain the sequence from random collision to induced fit, catalysis, product release and enzyme reuse.

**Amoeba Sisters — enzymes**

Use this for a more visual account of active sites, specificity and denaturation. When it ends, draw the process and narrate it without the video.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Induced fit and allosteric inhibition involve changing 3D conformations, so an **animation** earns one exception to the one-video rule.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=induced+fit+allosteric+inhibition+enzyme+animation>

After watching, draw one enzyme before binding, during induced fit and after product release. Then draw an inhibitor at a separate allosteric site and show how its binding changes the active site.
