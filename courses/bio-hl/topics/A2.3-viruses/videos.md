# A2.3 Viruses — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and draw the two replication cycles from memory. A second explanation straight away builds recognition, and recognition is the problem you’re trying to replace with recall.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — A2.3 Viruses**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+A2.3+Viruses>

Alex Lee organizes the course by the current IB topic codes, so start with the explanation made for A2.3.

**Watch for:**

- Which features belong to every virus and which occur in only some viruses.
- What bacteriophage lambda gets from its host during the lytic cycle.
- Where lambda DNA is during the lytic and lysogenic cycles.
- Why influenza and HIV can evolve fast enough to complicate treatment.

**Pause at:** the first comparison of the lytic and lysogenic cycles. Cover the labels and trace both routes aloud from attachment. Include the fate of the viral DNA and the host cell in every step.

## Worth it once, not for revision

The switch between lysogeny and lytic replication is worth seeing as an **animation** once. Movement makes integration, copying with the host chromosome, excision and virion assembly easier to place in sequence.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=bacteriophage+lambda+lytic+lysogenic+cycle+animation>

After watching, draw one bacterial chromosome before infection, during lysogeny and after induction. Label free phage DNA, prophage and newly assembled virions only where each one actually exists.
