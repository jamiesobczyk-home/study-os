# D4.1 Natural selection — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Explain natural selection as a continuous mechanism that changes populations over generations and contributes to Earth's biodiversity.
- [ ] Explain how mutation creates new alleles while sexual reproduction produces new combinations of alleles.
- [ ] Explain how overproduction of offspring and limited resources cause intraspecific competition and promote natural selection.
- [ ] Explain how abiotic factors such as high or low temperature can act as density-independent selection pressures.
- [ ] Use adaptation, survival and reproductive success to explain why some genotypes have greater fitness than others.
- [ ] Explain why natural selection can cause evolutionary change only when differences between individuals are heritable.
- [ ] Explain how mate choice and competition for mates cause sexual selection, using a named animal example.
- [ ] Interpret results from John Endler's guppy experiments in terms of natural selection, sexual selection and controlled selection pressures.

## Higher level

- [ ] Define a gene pool and calculate or compare allele frequencies within populations.
- [ ] Explain why geographically isolated populations can develop different allele frequencies, and interpret human allele-frequency data.
- [ ] Explain natural selection in genetic terms as a change in allele frequencies caused by differential reproductive success.
- [ ] Distinguish directional, stabilizing and disruptive selection from graphs or descriptions.
- [ ] Use p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1 to calculate allele and genotype frequencies.
- [ ] State the Hardy–Weinberg conditions and use a departure from predicted genotype frequencies as evidence that at least one condition hasn't been maintained.
- [ ] Distinguish artificial selection from natural selection, including why antibiotic resistance isn't artificial selection.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural selection | Differential survival and reproduction caused by differences in heritable traits | An organism choosing to change or the environment creating the needed trait |
| Evolution | A change in the heritable characteristics or allele frequencies of a population over generations | Growth, learning or adaptation during one individual's lifetime |
| Variation | Differences among individuals in a population | A claim that every difference is genetic or heritable |
| Mutation | A change in genetic material that can create a new allele | A response directed toward what an organism needs |
| Selection pressure | An environmental factor that affects survival or reproductive success | A force that acts with a goal or predicts which mutations will occur |
| Intraspecific competition | Competition between members of the same species | Competition between different species |
| Carrying capacity | The maximum population size that an environment can sustain over time | The number of offspring a single organism can produce |
| Adaptation | A heritable characteristic that increases fitness in a particular environment | A change acquired by an individual because it uses a body part |
| Fitness | An individual's or genotype's reproductive success relative to others in the population | Strength, health or survival by itself |
| Heritable trait | A trait whose genetic basis can be passed from parents to offspring | Any characteristic an offspring happens to share with a parent |
| Sexual selection | Selection caused by differences in success at obtaining mates and reproducing | Selection that occurs only because organisms reproduce sexually |
| Gene pool | All the genes and their different alleles present in a population | The alleles carried by one organism |
| Allele frequency | The proportion of all copies of a gene in a population that are a particular allele | The proportion of individuals showing a phenotype |
| Directional selection | Selection favoring one extreme phenotype | Selection favoring the intermediate phenotype |
| Stabilizing selection | Selection favoring intermediate phenotypes over both extremes | Selection that prevents every change in allele frequency |
| Disruptive selection | Selection favoring both extreme phenotypes over intermediates | Selection moving the population toward one extreme |
| Genetic equilibrium | A state in which allele frequencies remain constant between generations | A population in which every allele has the same frequency |
| Artificial selection | Deliberate choice by humans of which individuals breed because they have desired traits | Every case in which human activity creates a selection pressure |
