# D3.3 Homeostasis — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. If a point appears only after the reveal, it wasn’t available for the exam yet.

## Q1. Explain negative-feedback control of blood glucose concentration. **[6]**

Explain the responses to both an increase and a decrease in blood glucose concentration.

### Mark scheme

- An increase in blood glucose stimulates pancreatic beta cells to secrete insulin. **[1]**
- Insulin is transported in blood and binds to receptors on target cells. **[1]**
- Insulin increases glucose uptake / stimulates glycogenesis in liver or skeletal muscle, lowering blood glucose. **[1]**
- A decrease in blood glucose stimulates pancreatic alpha cells to secrete glucagon. **[1]**
- Glucagon stimulates glycogenolysis / gluconeogenesis in the liver and glucose release into blood. **[1]**
- Each response opposes the original deviation and its hormonal stimulus decreases as the set point is approached. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming insulin and glucagon earns limited credit. Full marks require their pancreatic sources, target responses and the return toward the set point._

## Q2. Compare and contrast type 1 and type 2 diabetes. **[6]**

Compare their physiological basis, risk factors, prevention and treatment.

### Mark scheme

- Both can cause persistently raised blood glucose concentration / hyperglycemia. **[1]**
- Type 1 involves autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells and little or no insulin production. **[1]**
- Type 2 initially involves reduced responsiveness of target cells to insulin / insulin resistance. **[1]**
- Genetic susceptibility can contribute to both; excess body fat, inactivity and unhealthy diet are important modifiable risk factors for type 2. **[1]**
- There is no established prevention for type 1, whereas type 2 risk can be reduced by physical activity, healthy diet and maintaining a healthy body mass. **[1]**
- Type 1 requires insulin replacement; type 2 may be treated with lifestyle changes, glucose-lowering medication and sometimes insulin. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “type 1 has no insulin and type 2 has too much sugar” misses the comparison. Both produce hyperglycemia, but the underlying defects differ._

## Q3. Explain thermoregulation when core body temperature falls. **[6]**

Include nervous, endocrine and effector responses in your answer.

### Mark scheme

- Thermoreceptors / the hypothalamus detect a temperature below the set point. **[1]**
- Skin arterioles constrict, reducing blood flow near the surface and reducing heat loss. **[1]**
- Skeletal muscles shiver, increasing respiration and heat production. **[1]**
- Hair erection traps an insulating air layer in furry mammals; Accept: limited effect in humans. **[1]**
- Brown adipose tissue carries out uncoupled respiration, releasing energy as heat rather than capturing as much in ATP. **[1]**
- The hypothalamus can act through the pituitary and thyroid glands to increase thyroxin secretion, metabolic rate and heat production. **[1]**

_Examiner note: vasoconstriction conserves existing heat, while shivering, brown fat and increased metabolic rate generate heat. Treating them as the same effect loses precision._

## Q4. Explain the formation of urine from blood plasma. **[7]**

Refer to ultrafiltration and selective reabsorption in your answer.

### Mark scheme

- High hydrostatic pressure is maintained in the glomerulus because the afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent arteriole. **[1]**
- Water and small solutes pass through the fenestrated capillary wall / basement membrane / podocyte filtration slits into Bowman’s capsule. **[1]**
- Blood cells and most large plasma proteins remain in the blood because they cannot cross the filtration barrier. **[1]**
- Useful substances are selectively reabsorbed from the proximal convoluted tubule into the blood. **[1]**
- Microvilli provide a large surface area and mitochondria provide ATP for active transport. **[1]**
- Glucose is reabsorbed by sodium-glucose cotransport / useful ions are moved by transport proteins. **[1]**
- Water follows reabsorbed solutes by osmosis, while urea and other unwanted solutes remain in the filtrate and are excreted in urine. **[1]**

_Examiner note: ultrafiltration isn’t selective removal of wastes. Small useful substances enter the filtrate too, then selective reabsorption recovers them._

## Q5. Explain osmoregulation after a person loses water by sweating. **[6]**

Include the loop of Henle, hypothalamus, pituitary gland and collecting ducts.

### Mark scheme

- Active transport of sodium ions from the ascending limb of the loop of Henle maintains a high osmotic concentration in the medulla. **[1]**
- Water loss raises blood osmotic concentration / lowers blood water potential. **[1]**
- Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect this change. **[1]**
- The posterior pituitary releases more antidiuretic hormone into the blood. **[1]**
- ADH causes aquaporin-containing vesicles to fuse with collecting-duct cell membranes, increasing water permeability. **[1]**
- More water is reabsorbed by osmosis, producing a smaller volume of more concentrated urine. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that ADH “adds water to the blood” skips the mechanism. The scoring chain runs through aquaporin insertion, increased collecting-duct permeability and osmosis._

## Q6. Suggest how blood supply changes during vigorous physical activity. **[4]**

Refer to skeletal muscles, the gut, kidneys and brain.

### Mark scheme

- Blood flow to active skeletal muscles increases to supply more oxygen and respiratory substrates / remove carbon dioxide, heat or other wastes. **[1]**
- Arterioles supplying skeletal muscles dilate / more precapillary sphincters in muscle open. **[1]**
- Blood flow to the gut and kidneys decreases because their arterioles constrict and blood is redirected. **[1]**
- Blood flow to the brain remains relatively stable because brain cells require a continuous oxygen and glucose supply. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “blood goes to the muscles” is one point at most. Full marks require redistribution through changes in vessel diameter and the contrasting patterns in the other organs._
