# D2.1 Cell and nuclear division — where the marks go

## Calling mitosis cell division

**Commonly written:** "Mitosis is when one cell divides into two cells."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mitosis divides the nucleus. Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm and completes division of the cell.

**Scores instead:** "Mitosis produces two genetically identical nuclei, after which cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm between the daughter cells."

## Saying replication doubles chromosome number

**Commonly written:** "DNA replication doubles the number of chromosomes."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Replication produces two sister chromatids per chromosome. Joined sister chromatids still count as one chromosome.

**Scores instead:** "DNA replication doubles the DNA content by producing sister chromatids, while chromosome number remains unchanged."

## Swapping the two meiotic separations

**Commonly written:** "Sister chromatids separate in meiosis I, and homologous chromosomes separate in meiosis II."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Homologous chromosomes segregate during meiosis I. Sister chromatids segregate during meiosis II.

**Scores instead:** "Meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes and reduces chromosome number; meiosis II separates sister chromatids."

## Calling random orientation crossing over

**Commonly written:** "Crossing over is when maternal and paternal chromosomes line up randomly."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Random orientation changes which whole homologues enter each nucleus. Crossing over exchanges DNA between non-sister chromatids.

**Scores instead:** "Crossing over exchanges DNA between non-sister chromatids, while random orientation produces different combinations of maternal and paternal homologues."

## Blaming Down syndrome on chromosome replication

**Commonly written:** "Chromosome 21 replicates an extra time, causing Down syndrome."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The usual meiotic error is failed separation, producing a gamete with an extra chromosome 21.

**Scores instead:** "Non-disjunction can produce a gamete with two copies of chromosome 21, leading to trisomy 21 after fertilization."

## Treating interphase as rest

**Commonly written:** "The cell rests during interphase before it starts dividing."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Interphase is metabolically active and includes cell growth, biosynthesis, organelle increase and DNA replication.

**Scores instead:** "During interphase, the cell grows, synthesizes components and replicates its DNA before nuclear division."

## Saying all tumours are cancerous

**Commonly written:** "Any tumour is cancer because its cells divide quickly."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Benign tumours remain localized. Malignant tumours invade tissue and may metastasize, which makes them cancerous.

**Scores instead:** "A malignant tumour invades neighboring tissue and can metastasize, whereas a benign tumour remains localized."

## Using mitotic index as proof of metastasis

**Commonly written:** "A high mitotic index proves that the tumour has spread."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mitotic index measures the proportion of dividing cells. It gives no direct evidence of invasion or spread.

**Scores instead:** "A high mitotic index indicates rapid cell division, while invasion and secondary tumours provide evidence of malignancy and metastasis."
