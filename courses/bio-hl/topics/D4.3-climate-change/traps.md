# D4.3 Climate change — where the marks go

## Treating correlation as proof of causation

**Commonly written:** "Ice cores prove that carbon dioxide causes global warming because both values rise together."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The shared pattern demonstrates positive correlation. Establishing causation requires evidence beyond the correlation itself.

**Scores instead:** "Ice-core data show a positive correlation between carbon dioxide and temperature, while infrared absorption and other evidence support the causal link."

## Leaving a positive feedback chain open

**Commonly written:** "Warming melts ice, so there is less ice."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The answer stops before showing how ice loss amplifies the initial warming and completes the feedback cycle.

**Scores instead:** "Warming melts reflective ice, lowering albedo so more solar radiation is absorbed, causing further warming and melting."

## Calling every carbon store a carbon sink

**Commonly written:** "A boreal forest is a carbon sink because it contains a large amount of carbon."

**Why it doesn’t score:** A sink has net carbon uptake. The amount already stored doesn’t show whether current uptake exceeds current release.

**Scores instead:** "A boreal forest is a carbon sink when carbon fixation and storage exceed carbon release from respiration, decomposition and combustion."

## Reversing the upwelling mechanism

**Commonly written:** "Warm surface water rises and brings nutrients from the deep ocean with it."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Surface warming strengthens stratification and can reduce the upward movement of cold, nutrient-rich deep water.

**Scores instead:** "Warmer surface water reduces vertical mixing and nutrient upwelling, lowering phytoplankton primary production."

## Using acidification and bleaching as synonyms

**Commonly written:** "Carbon dioxide lowers ocean pH, which bleaches corals."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Acidification suppresses calcification by reducing carbonate availability. Increased water temperature is the direct cause of heat-induced bleaching.

**Scores instead:** "Ocean acidification suppresses coral calcification, while high water temperature causes corals to lose photosynthetic symbionts and become bleached."

## Defining phenology as an organism’s life cycle

**Commonly written:** "Phenology is the sequence of stages in an organism’s life cycle."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Phenology concerns the timing of recurring biological events and the environmental variables that influence that timing.

**Scores instead:** "Phenology is research into the timing of events such as flowering, budburst, migration and nesting."

## Saying individual owls evolve

**Commonly written:** "When snowfall decreases, gray tawny owls evolve into brown tawny owls."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Individuals don’t change inherited color variants. Natural selection changes variant or allele frequencies in the population over generations.

**Scores instead:** "Reduced snow cover increases the fitness of brown variants, so alleles associated with brown coloration become more frequent over generations."
