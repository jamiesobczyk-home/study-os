# A4.1 Evolution and speciation — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain how molecular sequence data and selective breeding provide evidence for evolution. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Similarities in DNA / RNA base sequences or amino acid sequences support common ancestry. **[1]**
- Fewer sequence differences indicate a more recent common ancestor / differences accumulate after lineages diverge. **[1]**
- Selective breeding involves choosing organisms with desired heritable characteristics as parents. **[1]**
- Changes in domesticated breeds or crop varieties over generations demonstrate rapid change in heritable characteristics of populations. **[1]**

_Examiner note: listing DNA and dog breeds earns little. Full marks require an explanation of what each observation shows about descent and heritable change._

## Q2. Distinguish homologous and analogous structures, using one example of each. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Homologous structures have a common evolutionary origin / underlying structural plan, although their functions may differ. **[1]**
- Accept: pentadactyl forelimbs of tetrapods modified for walking, swimming or flying. **[1]**
- Analogous structures have similar functions but different evolutionary origins. **[1]**
- Accept: wings of birds and insects as independently evolved structures for flight. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “homologous means similar” misses the defining point. The distinction depends on evolutionary origin, not the amount of visible resemblance._

## Q3. Explain how geographical isolation by the Congo River could have led to the formation of bonobos and common chimpanzees. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- The Congo River separated populations of a common ancestral species / caused geographical isolation. **[1]**
- Interbreeding and gene flow between the populations were reduced / prevented. **[1]**
- The populations experienced different environmental conditions / selection pressures. **[1]**
- Different heritable characteristics / alleles were favored, causing genetic divergence. **[1]**
- Reproductive isolation developed, resulting in two species. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying “the river made them evolve differently” skips the mechanism. The scoring chain runs from blocked gene flow through differential selection to reproductive isolation._

## Q4. Compare and contrast allopatric and sympatric speciation. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Both involve splitting / divergence from a pre-existing ancestral species. **[1]**
- Both require reproductive isolation / reduction or prevention of gene flow. **[1]**
- Both involve genetic divergence of the isolated populations. **[1]**
- Allopatric speciation involves geographical separation by a physical barrier. **[1]**
- Sympatric speciation occurs in the same geographical area through behavioral / temporal isolation or polyploidy. **[1]**

_Examiner note: “one is apart and one is together” identifies location but leaves three shared biological processes unexplained._

## Q5. Explain how adaptive radiation can increase biodiversity in an ecosystem containing vacant niches. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- One ancestral species gives rise to multiple descendant species. **[1]**
- Populations occupy different vacant ecological niches / use different resources. **[1]**
- Different selection pressures favor different heritable characteristics, causing divergence. **[1]**
- The resulting species coexist with reduced competition / species diversity increases. **[1]**

_Examiner note: adaptive radiation means diversification into several niche-specialized species. Adaptation of one species to one niche doesn’t describe radiation._

## Q6. Explain how hybridization and polyploidy can cause abrupt speciation in plants. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- Hybridization combines chromosome sets from two different species. **[1]**
- The initial hybrid may be sterile because non-homologous chromosomes fail to pair correctly during meiosis. **[1]**
- Chromosome doubling provides homologous partners / allows balanced chromosome pairing during meiosis. **[1]**
- The resulting polyploid can be fertile with other polyploids but reproductively isolated from the parent species. **[1]**
- Accept: knotweed / smartweed in the genus Persicaria as an example. **[1]**

_Examiner note: chromosome doubling doesn’t earn the mechanism marks by itself. The answer must connect homologous pairing to restored fertility and incompatible chromosome sets to reproductive isolation._
