# D1.1 DNA replication — what you need to be able to do

## Core

- [ ] Explain why DNA must be copied before reproduction, growth and tissue replacement, and state what an accurate copy preserves.
- [ ] Explain why DNA replication is described as semi-conservative and how complementary base pairing makes accurate copying possible.
- [ ] Describe the roles of helicase and DNA polymerase in DNA replication without confusing separation of strands with assembly of new strands.
- [ ] Explain one complete PCR cycle using denaturation, primer annealing and extension by Taq DNA polymerase.
- [ ] Explain how primers, repeated temperature changes and thermostable Taq DNA polymerase allow PCR to amplify a selected DNA region.
- [ ] Explain how gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments according to length.
- [ ] Explain how PCR and gel electrophoresis are used in DNA profiling for forensic investigations and paternity testing.
- [ ] Explain why using more genetic markers makes a DNA profile match more reliable.

## Higher level

- [ ] Use the 5′ and 3′ ends of DNA strands to explain why DNA polymerases synthesize DNA only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.
- [ ] Compare replication of the leading and lagging strands, including continuous synthesis, discontinuous synthesis, RNA primers and Okazaki fragments.
- [ ] State the role of DNA primase and explain why primers are needed before DNA polymerase can extend a strand.
- [ ] Distinguish the functions of DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I and DNA ligase in prokaryotic DNA replication.
- [ ] Explain how proofreading by DNA polymerase III lowers the frequency of replication errors.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — antiparallel strands, complementary base pairing and the sugar–phosphate backbone explain why replication works. D1.1 turns that structure into a mechanism.
- **D1.2 Protein synthesis** — replication preserves DNA sequences, while transcription uses one DNA strand to make RNA. Keeping those processes separate prevents enzyme and product mix-ups.
- **D1.3 Mutation and gene editing** — proofreading explains why replication errors are uncommon, while errors that remain can become mutations.
- **D2.1 Cell and nuclear division** — DNA replication occurs before mitosis or meiosis so each resulting nucleus can receive a copy of the genome.
- **A3.2 Classification and cladistics** — PCR and gel electrophoresis can provide DNA evidence for comparing organisms and investigating relationships.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DNA replication | Production of DNA molecules with the same base sequence as the original DNA | Transcription, which produces RNA from a DNA template |
| Semi-conservative replication | Replication in which each product DNA molecule contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand | A process in which half of the original base sequence is discarded |
| Template strand | A parental DNA strand whose base sequence determines the sequence of a new complementary strand | A strand copied with the same sequence and orientation |
| Helicase | An enzyme that unwinds DNA and breaks hydrogen bonds between complementary bases | The enzyme that joins nucleotides into a new strand |
| DNA polymerase | An enzyme that adds DNA nucleotides to a growing strand using a template | An enzyme that separates the two parental strands |
| Primer | A short nucleic acid strand with a free 3′ end from which a polymerase can extend | A nucleotide that identifies every DNA sequence by itself |
| PCR | A laboratory method that uses repeated temperature cycles to amplify a selected DNA region | Replication of an entire genome inside a dividing cell |
| Taq DNA polymerase | A heat-stable DNA polymerase used to extend primers during PCR | The enzyme that separates PCR strands during heating |
| Gel electrophoresis | A method that uses an electric field and a gel to separate DNA fragments by length | A method that copies DNA fragments |
| DNA profile | A pattern produced by analyzing variable regions or markers in a DNA sample | A complete sequence of every nucleotide in a person's genome |
| 5′ end and 3′ end | Chemically different ends that give a nucleic acid strand direction | Labels for the top and bottom of any DNA drawing |
| Leading strand | A new DNA strand synthesized continuously toward the advancing replication fork | The parental strand that helicase opens first |
| Lagging strand | A new DNA strand synthesized discontinuously as Okazaki fragments | A strand made in the 3′ to 5′ direction |
| Okazaki fragment | A short DNA section synthesized on the lagging strand | An RNA primer that remains permanently in DNA |
| DNA ligase | An enzyme that seals nicks between adjacent DNA sections by forming phosphodiester bonds | An enzyme that adds complementary nucleotides to a template |
| Proofreading | Detection and correction of a mismatched terminal nucleotide during DNA synthesis | Repair of every mutation at any time in a cell's life |
