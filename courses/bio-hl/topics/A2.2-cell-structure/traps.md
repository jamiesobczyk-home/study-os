# A2.2 Cell structure — where the marks go

## Treating magnification as resolution

**Commonly written:** "Electron microscopes are better because they magnify more."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Magnification only enlarges an image. The key advantage is greater resolution, which reveals two close points as separate.

**Scores instead:** "Electron microscopes have greater resolution than light microscopes, so they reveal finer cell ultrastructure."

## Dividing measurements before matching the units

**Commonly written:** "The image is 20 mm and the cell is 10 µm, so the magnification is ×2."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Image size and actual size must be converted to the same unit before image size is divided by actual size.

**Scores instead:** "20 mm is 20,000 µm, so the magnification is 20,000 ÷ 10 = ×2000."

## Saying prokaryotes have no DNA or ribosomes

**Commonly written:** "Prokaryotic cells have no nucleus, so they have no DNA or ribosomes."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Prokaryotes contain nucleoid DNA and 70S ribosomes; they lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

**Scores instead:** "A prokaryote has naked DNA in a nucleoid region and 70S ribosomes, but no membrane-bound nucleus."

## Calling every plant cell chloroplast-containing

**Commonly written:** "Plant cells can always be identified because they contain chloroplasts."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Only photosynthetic plant cells contain chloroplasts. Non-photosynthetic cells, including many root cells, lack them.

**Scores instead:** "A cell wall supports identification as a plant cell, while chloroplasts identify a photosynthetic plant cell when they’re visible."

## Using image color as micrograph evidence

**Commonly written:** "The organelle is green in the electron micrograph, so it must be a chloroplast."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Electron micrographs are produced without natural color, and added color doesn't establish the identity of a structure.

**Scores instead:** "The organelle is identified as a chloroplast from its internal membranes and its position within a plant cell, not from added color."

## Saying all eukaryotic cells have one nucleus

**Commonly written:** "Every eukaryotic cell has exactly one membrane-bound nucleus."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Some eukaryotic structures are multinucleate, while mature mammalian red blood cells and sieve tube elements lack nuclei.

**Scores instead:** "Typical eukaryotic cells have a nucleus, but skeletal muscle fibers and aseptate hyphae have many, while some differentiated cells have none."

## Listing endosymbiosis evidence without the comparison

**Commonly written:** "Mitochondria have DNA, ribosomes and can reproduce."

**Why it doesn’t score:** The evidence depends on specific prokaryote-like features: naked circular DNA, 70S ribosomes and replication within the cell.

**Scores instead:** "Mitochondria contain naked circular DNA and 70S ribosomes and can replicate, all of which support their prokaryotic origin."

## Saying differentiation changes the genes present

**Commonly written:** "A muscle cell becomes different because it receives different genes from a nerve cell."

**Why it doesn’t score:** Differentiated cells generally retain the same genome; different patterns of gene expression produce different proteins and structures.

**Scores instead:** "Muscle and nerve cells contain the same genome but express different genes, so they produce different proteins and become specialized."
