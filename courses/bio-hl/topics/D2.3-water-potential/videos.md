# D2.3 Water potential — what to watch

Watch **one** main video, then close it and explain osmosis and water potential from memory. A second explanation of the same material builds recognition, which is already the problem.

## Main

**Mister Lee Science — D2.3 Water potential**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@misterleescience> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mister+Lee+Science+D2.3+Water+potential>

Alex Lee organizes his IB Biology videos by the current syllabus codes, so begin with the version built around D2.3.

**Watch for:**

- What crosses the membrane during osmosis, and what doesn’t.
- How hypotonic and hypertonic describe one solution relative to another.
- Why an isotonic condition has movement in both directions but no net movement.
- How Ψs and Ψp combine to determine Ψw at HL.

**Pause at:** the first diagram showing two solutions separated by a membrane. Cover any arrows, label the hypotonic and hypertonic sides, predict the direction of net water movement, and give the reason aloud before continuing.

## If that didn’t land

**Amoeba Sisters — osmosis and water balance**

- Channel: <https://www.youtube.com/@AmoebaSisters> — verified
- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Amoeba+Sisters+osmosis+water+balance+cells>

Use this for a more visual comparison of plant and animal cells. When it ends, draw both cell types in hypotonic and hypertonic solutions from memory.

## Worth it once, not for revision

Plasmolysis is a spatial change, so an **animation** earns an extra watch. Seeing the membrane pull away from the cell wall makes the term harder to confuse with ordinary shrinking.

- Search: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=plant+cell+plasmolysis+animation+osmosis>

Pause once the cell is plasmolyzed. Point to the cell wall, cell membrane and reduced cell contents, then explain where the water moved and why.
