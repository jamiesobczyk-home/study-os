# D1.3 Mutation and gene editing — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

- [ ] Distinguish substitution, insertion and deletion mutations by comparing DNA base sequences.
- [ ] Predict how a base substitution can leave the amino acid unchanged, change one amino acid or introduce a stop codon.
- [ ] Explain why inserting or deleting bases can cause a frameshift, and recognize when an insertion or deletion stays in frame.
- [ ] Explain how errors in DNA replication or repair, chemical mutagens and mutagenic radiation can produce gene mutations.
- [ ] Explain what scientists mean when they say mutations are random, including why some bases can still mutate more often than others.
- [ ] Compare the consequences of mutations in germ cells and somatic cells, including inheritance and cancer.
- [ ] Explain why mutation is the original source of genetic variation and why this variation is required for evolution by natural selection.
- [ ] Interpret commercial genetic-test results as estimates of risk rather than certain predictions.

## Higher level

- [ ] Explain how making a gene inoperative in a knockout organism can provide evidence about the gene’s function.
- [ ] Outline how a guide RNA and Cas9 are used to target and cut a chosen DNA sequence.
- [ ] Describe one successful use of CRISPR-Cas9, linking the edited DNA to the intended biological effect.
- [ ] Discuss why human gene editing raises ethical and regulatory questions, especially when an edit could be inherited.
- [ ] Explain two hypotheses for conserved or highly conserved gene sequences: functional constraint and a lower mutation rate.

## Links to other topics

- **D1.1 DNA replication** — replication errors are one source of mutation, while proofreading and repair reduce the mutation rate. This link explains where spontaneous sequence changes come from.
- **D1.2 Protein synthesis** — codons, degeneracy and reading frames let you predict how a DNA mutation may alter a polypeptide.
- **D2.1 Cell and nuclear division** — mitosis passes a somatic mutation to descendant cells, which helps explain the growth of a mutant cell clone in cancer.
- **D2.2 Gene expression** — a mutation can affect a regulatory sequence without changing a polypeptide’s amino acid sequence.
- **D3.2 Inheritance** — a mutation in a germ cell can create a new allele that’s passed to offspring.
- **D4.1 Natural selection** — mutation creates new alleles, while selection changes their frequencies according to their effects in a particular environment.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gene mutation | A structural change in a gene’s DNA base sequence | A change produced because an organism needs a new trait |
| Substitution | Replacement of one nucleotide by another | Addition or removal of a nucleotide |
| Insertion | Addition of one or more nucleotides to a DNA sequence | Replacement of one nucleotide |
| Deletion | Removal of one or more nucleotides from a DNA sequence | Loss of an entire chromosome in cell division |
| Single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) | Variation at one nucleotide position resulting from a base substitution | Any insertion, deletion or large chromosomal change |
| Degeneracy | The property that more than one codon can specify the same amino acid | One codon specifying several different amino acids |
| Synonymous mutation | A base substitution that doesn’t change the encoded amino acid | A mutation that reverses itself |
| Missense mutation | A base substitution that changes a codon for one amino acid into a codon for another | Every substitution mutation |
| Nonsense mutation | A base substitution that changes an amino-acid codon into a stop codon | A frameshift caused by an insertion or deletion |
| Frameshift mutation | A shift in the grouping of downstream codons after bases are inserted or deleted in a number that isn’t divisible by three | Any mutation that changes one amino acid |
| Mutagen | A physical or chemical agent that increases the mutation rate | A mutation or a mutated gene |
| Somatic mutation | A mutation in a non-reproductive body cell and its descendant cells | A mutation normally inherited by the organism’s offspring |
| Germline mutation | A mutation in a germ cell or its precursor that can enter a gamete and be inherited | A mutation that must affect every cell of the parent |
| Gene knockout | Deliberate inactivation of a gene to investigate its function | Removal of every gene from a cell |
| Guide RNA | RNA designed to bind a complementary target DNA sequence and direct Cas9 there | An enzyme that cuts DNA |
| Cas9 | A nuclease that cuts DNA at a site specified by a guide RNA | The RNA sequence that recognizes the target |
| Conserved sequence | A DNA sequence that’s identical or similar within a species or group of species | A sequence that never undergoes mutation |
| Highly conserved sequence | A sequence that has remained identical or similar over a long period of evolution | A sequence found only in one recently evolved population |
