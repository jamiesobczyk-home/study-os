# D1.1 DNA replication — exam practice

> Write each answer before revealing the mark scheme. A point that looked familiar after the reveal wasn’t recalled and didn’t score.

## Q1. Explain how semi-conservative replication produces accurate copies of DNA. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- Hydrogen bonds between complementary bases are broken / the two parental DNA strands separate. **[1]**
- Each parental strand acts as a template for synthesis of a new strand. **[1]**
- Complementary base pairing occurs, with A pairing with T and C pairing with G. **[1]**
- Each product DNA molecule contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand / has a base sequence identical to the original molecule. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "semi-conservative means half is conserved" scores at most one mark. Full marks require the fate of both parental strands and the copying mechanism._

## Q2. Explain how PCR amplifies a selected region of DNA. **[5]**

### Mark scheme

- DNA is heated to separate the strands / disrupt hydrogen bonds during denaturation. **[1]**
- The mixture is cooled so primers anneal to complementary sequences bordering the target region. **[1]**
- Primers provide free 3′ ends / determine the region to be amplified. **[1]**
- Taq DNA polymerase extends the primers by adding complementary DNA nucleotides. **[1]**
- Repeated temperature cycles produce an approximately exponential increase in the target DNA. **[1]**

_Examiner note: naming the three stages isn't enough. Full marks require what happens to the DNA at each stage and why primers and Taq DNA polymerase are needed._

## Q3. Explain how gel electrophoresis can be used after PCR in a forensic investigation. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- PCR amplifies selected variable DNA regions from the sample. **[1]**
- Negatively charged DNA fragments move through the gel toward the positive electrode in an electric field. **[1]**
- Smaller fragments move farther / faster through the gel than larger fragments. **[1]**
- The resulting band pattern is compared with reference samples at multiple markers. **[1]**

_Examiner note: saying that the gel "shows the DNA" misses the separation principle. The answer needs charge, direction of movement and the effect of fragment length._

## Q4. Compare and contrast synthesis of the leading and lagging strands. **[6]**

### Mark scheme

- Both strands are synthesized by complementary base pairing using a parental DNA template. **[1]**
- Both new strands are synthesized in the 5′ to 3′ direction. **[1]**
- The leading strand is synthesized continuously, whereas the lagging strand is synthesized discontinuously. **[1]**
- Leading-strand synthesis proceeds toward the advancing replication fork, whereas each lagging-strand section is synthesized away from it. **[1]**
- The leading strand requires one RNA primer, whereas the lagging strand requires repeated RNA primers. **[1]**
- The lagging strand is produced as Okazaki fragments that are subsequently joined by DNA ligase. **[1]**

_Examiner note: a diagram can earn marks only if its directions are clear. An answer claiming that the lagging strand grows 3′ to 5′ loses the central comparison._

## Q5. Outline the functions of four enzymes in prokaryotic DNA replication. **[4]**

### Mark scheme

- DNA primase synthesizes RNA primers that provide free 3′ ends. **[1]**
- DNA polymerase III extends the primers with complementary DNA nucleotides / synthesizes DNA 5′ to 3′. **[1]**
- DNA polymerase I removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA. **[1]**
- DNA ligase seals nicks between DNA sections / forms phosphodiester bonds to join Okazaki fragments. **[1]**

_Examiner note: "polymerase copies DNA" can't distinguish the two polymerases. Full marks require a separate, specific function for DNA polymerase I and DNA polymerase III._

## Q6. Explain how proofreading by DNA polymerase III increases replication accuracy. **[3]**

### Mark scheme

- A mismatched nucleotide at the 3′ terminal of the growing strand is detected. **[1]**
- The mismatched terminal nucleotide is removed by DNA polymerase III. **[1]**
- A correctly matched nucleotide is inserted before synthesis continues. **[1]**

_Examiner note: complementary base pairing explains initial nucleotide selection, but this question asks for the correction sequence after a mismatch has been added._
