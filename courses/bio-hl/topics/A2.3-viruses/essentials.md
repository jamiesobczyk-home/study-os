# A2.3 Viruses — what you need to be able to do

> Tick a line only after you can do it with the page closed and without prompts.

## Core

A2.3 has no SL content. Every checklist item below is Higher Level.

## Higher level

- [ ] State the structural features shared by viruses: small, fixed-size particles with a DNA or RNA genome, a protein capsid, no cytoplasm, and few or no enzymes.
- [ ] Explain why viruses depend on host cells for energy, nutrition, protein synthesis and reproduction.
- [ ] Describe variation in viral genomes, including DNA or RNA and single- or double-stranded forms, and distinguish enveloped from non-enveloped viruses.
- [ ] Compare the structures of bacteriophage lambda, coronaviruses and HIV.
- [ ] Distinguish positive-sense RNA viruses, negative-sense RNA viruses, retroviruses and DNA viruses by how they produce mRNA.
- [ ] Outline the attachment, entry, biosynthesis, assembly and release phases of bacteriophage lambda’s lytic cycle.
- [ ] Explain which host-cell resources a virus uses during a lytic infection.
- [ ] Outline bacteriophage lambda’s lysogenic cycle, including integration as a prophage, replication with the host chromosome and induction.
- [ ] Compare the lytic and lysogenic cycles in terms of genome location, virion production, host-cell survival and duration.
- [ ] Explain why viral diversity and complete dependence on cells support several cellular origins rather than one known common viral ancestor.
- [ ] Outline the progressive and regressive hypotheses for viral origins.
- [ ] Explain how convergent evolution could produce features shared by unrelated viruses.
- [ ] Explain why some viruses evolve rapidly, using mutation rate, replication rate, population size and natural selection.
- [ ] Distinguish antigenic drift from antigenic shift in influenza viruses.
- [ ] Explain how rapid evolution occurs in HIV and why it makes treatment difficult.
- [ ] Apply rapid viral evolution to vaccination and drug treatment, including updated influenza vaccines and combination antiretroviral therapy.

## Links to other topics

- **A1.2 Nucleic acids** — viral genomes can be DNA or RNA, and all viruses must produce mRNA that host ribosomes can translate. A1.2 supplies the molecular language for comparing genome types.
- **A2.2 Cell structure** — viruses lack cytoplasm, ribosomes and the organization of cells. Comparing them with prokaryotic cells stops “small bacterium” from becoming your mental model.
- **A4.1 Evolution and speciation** — mutation, natural selection and convergent evolution explain both rapid viral change and similarities among viruses that may have separate origins.
- **C3.2 Defence against disease** — antigenic change helps viruses escape existing immune responses and affects vaccination.
- **D1.2 Protein synthesis** — viruses depend on host ribosomes to translate viral mRNA. Genome type determines the route used to make that mRNA.
- **D3.2 Inheritance** — a lambda prophage is copied with the bacterial chromosome and passed to daughter cells during lysogeny.

## Vocabulary that has to be exact

| Term | What it means | What it’s not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Virus | An acellular infectious agent containing a nucleic-acid genome inside a protein capsid | A very small prokaryotic cell |
| Virion | A complete virus particle outside a host cell or moving between cells | The infected host cell |
| Capsid | The protein coat surrounding and protecting a viral genome | A cell membrane or cell wall |
| Viral envelope | A host-derived lipid membrane surrounding some viruses, usually containing viral proteins | A structure shared by every virus |
| Bacteriophage | A virus that infects bacteria | A bacterium that consumes other cells |
| Obligate parasite | An agent that can reproduce only by using a living host cell | An organism that can reproduce independently when nutrients are supplied |
| Positive-sense RNA | RNA that has the same sense as mRNA and can be translated by ribosomes | RNA that must first be copied into complementary positive-sense RNA |
| Negative-sense RNA | RNA complementary to mRNA, which must be copied into positive-sense RNA before translation | RNA that carries a negative electrical charge |
| Retrovirus | An RNA virus that uses reverse transcriptase to make DNA from its RNA | Any virus whose genome contains RNA |
| Lytic cycle | Viral replication followed by assembly and lysis of the host cell | Viral DNA remaining silent in the host chromosome |
| Lysogenic cycle | A cycle in which phage DNA is integrated into the host chromosome and copied with it | Immediate production and release of many virions |
| Prophage | Bacteriophage DNA integrated into a bacterial chromosome | A complete phage particle attached to a bacterium |
| Induction | Activation and excision of a prophage, leading into the lytic cycle | Initial attachment of a phage to its host |
| Antigenic drift | Gradual antigen change caused by accumulating mutations | Sudden reassortment of whole genome segments |
| Antigenic shift | Major antigen change caused by reassortment between different influenza viruses | The gradual buildup of point mutations |
| Convergent evolution | Independent evolution of similar features under similar selection pressures | Inheritance of a feature from one recent common ancestor |
