# A2.3 Viruses — where the marks go

## Giving every virus an envelope

**Commonly written:** “All viruses have genetic material inside a capsid and a lipid envelope.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** A capsid is universal, but an envelope occurs only in some viruses and is derived from host-cell membrane.

**Scores instead:** “All viruses have a nucleic-acid genome and protein capsid; some also have a host-derived lipid envelope.”

## Calling a virus a small cell

**Commonly written:** “A virus is a tiny cell without many organelles.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Viruses are acellular and lack cytoplasm and ribosomes, rather than being cells with fewer organelles.

**Scores instead:** “A virus is an acellular infectious agent with a nucleic-acid genome inside a protein capsid.”

## Letting the whole bacteriophage enter the bacterium

**Commonly written:** “Bacteriophage lambda enters the bacterial cell and releases its DNA.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Lambda attaches outside the bacterium and injects its DNA; the capsid and tail remain outside.

**Scores instead:** “Lambda attaches to receptors on the bacterium and injects its DNA into the cell.”

## Saying viruses divide

**Commonly written:** “The viral cell grows and divides many times before the bacterium bursts.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Viral genomes and proteins are produced separately using host machinery, then assembled into virions.

**Scores instead:** “The host copies viral nucleic acid and makes viral proteins, which assemble into new virions before lysis.”

## Treating lysogeny as permanent inactivity

**Commonly written:** “During the lysogenic cycle, the viral DNA stays dormant and nothing happens.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** The prophage is copied with the bacterial chromosome and inherited by daughter cells, and induction can activate lytic replication.

**Scores instead:** “The prophage is replicated with the host chromosome until induction causes it to enter the lytic cycle.”

## Calling antigenic shift a large mutation

**Commonly written:** “Antigenic shift happens when one major mutation suddenly changes an influenza antigen.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Antigenic shift results from reassortment of genome segments between different influenza viruses infecting the same cell.

**Scores instead:** “Antigenic shift is a major antigen change caused by reassortment of influenza genome segments.”

## Saying antiviral drugs cause useful mutations

**Commonly written:** “HIV mutates so that it can become resistant to the drug.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Mutations arise without regard to need; treatment selects resistant variants already produced by mutation.

**Scores instead:** “Mutation creates HIV variants, and drug treatment increases the frequency of variants that carry resistance.”

## Claiming one origin because viruses share a capsid

**Commonly written:** “All viruses have capsids, so they must have inherited them from one viral ancestor.”

**Why it doesn’t score:** Similar selection pressures can independently favor protective capsids, so the similarity may result from convergent evolution.

**Scores instead:** “Capsids may have evolved independently in different viral lineages as protection for genetic material outside host cells.”
