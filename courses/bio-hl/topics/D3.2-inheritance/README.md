# D3.2 Inheritance

- **Theme:** D — Continuity and change
- **Level:** Organisms
- **Level of study:** SL and HL
- **Status:** ready

## The one-sentence version

Inheritance connects the movement of chromosomes and alleles during sexual reproduction to predictable patterns in offspring, including dominance, sex linkage, polygenic variation and gene linkage.

## Why this topic is worth getting right

D3.2 can feel like a pile of symbols because several inheritance patterns use similar-looking grids. The way through is to name the biological event before doing the arithmetic: alleles segregate, chromosomes assort, linked loci travel together, and crossing over can separate them. Producing the gametes and explaining their frequencies will carry you through Punnett grids, pedigrees, test crosses and chi-squared questions.

## In your study guide

Allott, _Biology Study Guide_ (2023), **section D3.2, pp. 161–171**. Read it *after*
the capture sheet, never before. It’s condensed enough to make a fast check of
what you left out, and a slow way to meet the topic for the first time.
